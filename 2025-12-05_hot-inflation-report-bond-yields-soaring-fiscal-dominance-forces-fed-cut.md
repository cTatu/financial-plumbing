# Hot Inflation Report Sends Bond Yields Soaring - Why Fiscal Dominance Forces the Fed to Cut Anyway

## 1. Thesis

Hot PCE shows inflation stuck near 3% with prices cumulatively far above target, yet the Fed keeps cutting because $1.20 trillion of interest growing at 27% a year has made debt service the binding constraint. The bond market answers with a bear steepener — the 30-year back to 4.80% for a three-month high while the 30s-2s spread sits only 120bp — plus a softer dollar on Hassett-as-chair chatter, while Japanese 2s and 5s at multi-decade highs threaten repatriation that drags US long yields higher. The presenter reads this as full fiscal dominance: negative real rates and currency debasement instead of price stability, the WWII playbook returning.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] Cumulative prices matter more than the annual rate because a 39% rise in per-person spending against 22% official inflation is the lived purchasing-power loss.
- [TAKE] The long end prices deficits and inflation while the 2-year prices Fed action, so hot PCE lifts 30-year yields even when the print lands below expectations.
- [TAKE] Cutting with core near 3% is fiscal dominance rather than dual-mandate policy because the $1.20 trillion interest bill forces easing into rising prices.
- [TAKE] Japanese 2-year and 5-year yields at multi-decade highs invite repatriation that sells Treasuries and dollars to buy yen and JGBs, pressing US long yields up.
- [TAKE] Negative real rates come at the currency's expense because capping debt costs below nominal growth debases the dollar instead of restoring price stability.

- [PREDICTS] Higher JGB yields pull capital home and drag US long yields higher through Treasury and dollar selling. -> check: JGB 2Y/5Y higher with USD/JPY lower and US 30Y higher confirms repatriation; JGB yields falling with US 30Y falling refutes it.
