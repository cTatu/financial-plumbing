# An Unsustainable US Economy - Is it Fixable?

## 1. Thesis

A data-only tour since 1971 shows every obligation and asset outrunning the output that services them: Social Security grows 2.7 times faster than GDP and Medicare 8.4 times faster, government debt grows 3.6 times faster, home equity 2.6 times faster, and stocks 8.6 times faster than wages, while the per-capita wage share of GDP falls 70% and falls 35% against M2 in 25 years. The distribution makes it political, not just arithmetic — the over-50 cohort owns nearly all housing and equity upside while the under-40 real-estate share falls from 22% to 12%, so the median boomer's wealth lives in a home the state cannot let fall even as entitlements outgrow receipts. With each new debt dollar buying only $0.33 of GDP and interest compounding on the stock, the presenter frames the system as requiring permanent nominal expansion with no painless fix.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] Entitlement outlays outrun the tax base because Social Security grows 2.7 times faster than GDP and Medicare grows 8.4 times faster than GDP while debt grows 3.6 times faster than output.
- [TAKE] Housing functions as a nationalized pension because the median boomer holds very little stock and most wealth in a home the government cannot allow to fall for a prolonged period.
- [TAKE] Asset inflation concentrates gains at the top because top-1% net worth grows 2.1 times faster than GDP while the under-40 share of housing falls from 22% to 12%.
- [TAKE] Wage earners lose purchasing power by construction because the per-capita wage share of GDP falls 70% and falls 35% against M2 while medical costs grow 2 times faster than pay.
- [TAKE] Diminishing returns on debt make the path self-reinforcing because each new dollar of debt buys only about $0.33 of GDP since 1971, so servicing requires further nominal expansion.
