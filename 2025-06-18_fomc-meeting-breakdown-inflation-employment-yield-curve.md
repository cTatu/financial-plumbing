# FOMC Meeting Breakdown: Inflation, Employment, Yield Curve - Explained

## 1. Thesis

The June FOMC held rates and held the 50bp 2025 cut guide, but the SEP quietly stagflations the outlook with lower 2025-2026 growth and higher PCE while lifting the 2026-2027 rate path — a combination the video reads as the Fed starting to price pro-cyclical deficits, peak globalization, and constrained immigration as structural inflation. With U6 at 7.8% against a 4.2-4.5% headline, nominal growth at 4.4% already below the 4.7% 30-year, and long yields rising since the September 2024 jumbo cut, the risk is that short-rate cuts cannot reach mortgages, cards, and car loans while the curve bear-steepens toward eventual Twist/QE to cap the long end.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] The statement change to uncertainty diminished-but-elevated with dual-mandate tension admits administration policy pulls inflation and employment in opposite directions.
- [TAKE] Running 6%-plus deficits at 4.2% unemployment is pro-cyclical stimulus more suited to war or deep recession, so fiscal continuation is the upside inflation risk.
- [TAKE] Deglobalization and reshoring plus reduced illegal labor supply lift wages and prices together, which reverses the disinflationary globalization of the past four decades.
- [TAKE] Long yields bottomed on the first September 2024 cut and rose into cuts because the bond market prices growth, inflation, and credit risk rather than following the overnight rate.
- [TAKE] Real wages trailing GDP since 1960 means CPI near 5.0% with 10.0% wage growth would enrich workers, so the nominal inflation number alone misstates welfare.
