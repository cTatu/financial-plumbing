# The Consumer Debt Crisis

## 1. Thesis

He made this video to relocate the debt crisis from Washington to households: nominal personal interest payments hit $561 billion, an 11-fold rise since 1981 even though the policy rate is a fifth of its then-level, because falling rates induced ever-greater leveraging. Scaled to wages, the burden sits at an all-time high — 18 billion hours of work a year just to pay interest — with credit-card balances doubling to $1.2 trillion while the rate doubled to 21%, savings flat, and assets that look higher in dollars actually cheaper in gold. The twin-deficit economy therefore keeps accumulating debt at every level, leaving consumers exposed to structurally higher rates ahead.

## 2. Key Claims

- [00:08] Americans now pay $561 billion per year in personal interest on credit cards, auto loans, and mortgages. (as of May 2025)
- [00:12] Personal interest payments are up 11-fold since 1981 even though the federal funds rate is one-fifth of its then-level. (as of 1981-2025 comparison)
- [02:48] Americans now work 18 billion hours per year just to pay interest, up from 4 to 5 billion hours in the late 1960s. (as of May 2025)
- [03:51] The federal funds rate peaked at 19% in the early 1980s and has trended lower ever since. (as of 1981 peak)
- [04:59] Credit-card balances fell to about $650 billion after 2008 deleveraging before doubling to about $1.2 trillion. (as of 2008-2025 cycle)
- [06:11] The average credit-card interest rate rose from about 11.5% after the financial crisis to 21% today. (as of May 2025)
- [07:28] Personal interest payments are up about 3,385% since 1971 versus average hourly wages up about 760%, or roughly 5-fold faster. (as of May 2025)
- [17:11] It took about 670 ounces of gold to buy a house at the early-2000s peak versus about 130 ounces today. (as of 2000-2025 comparison)
- [18:26] Gold is up about 60 to 65% over the past 12 months because the dollar's purchasing power fell, not because gold changed. (as of May 2025)
- [19:22] M2 money supply grows about 7 to 8% per year, which is the real dilution rate of dollars. (as of May 2025)

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] [02:08] Personal interest relative to wages and to home prices sits at an all-time high, so the servicing burden matters more than the nominal $561 billion level.
- [TAKE] [03:27] Falling policy rates caused households to lever up, so the cost of debt exploded even as the price of money fell.
- [TAKE] [09:20] A twin-deficit nation consumes more value than it produces, so households and government alike can only accumulate debt rather than net wealth.
- [TAKE] [16:41] Houses did not get more valuable in real terms because the dollar price quadrupling from $80,000 to $500,000 mostly reflects denominator debasement to 130 from 670 ounces.
- [TAKE] [20:10] Interest rates move in 70- to 80-year lifetime cycles, so a move from 10 basis points to 4% has already worsened the burden before any structural rise to 5-9%.

## 4C Predicts

- [PREDICTS] [20:00] The burden of consumer debt goes higher as structurally higher rates lift the 4% funds rate toward 5-9% on record leverage. -> check: Fed funds plus average card rate and interest-to-wage ratio higher 12 months out versus reverting toward post-2008 lows.

## 7. Follow-ups

- Watch credit-card balances against the 21% rate with delinquencies: balances holding near $1.2T with rates above 20% confirms deepening leverage, while balances falling toward $650B says deleveraging has restarted.
- Watch personal interest relative to wages and home prices: holding at all-time highs with M2 growing 7-8% confirms debasement outruns income, while the ratio falling 10%+ says wages or hard assets are catching up.
- Watch funds rate with household stress into weak retail: funds rising toward 5%+ with interest-to-wage still climbing confirms the structural-rate squeeze, while funds cut to near zero with the ratio falling refutes it.
