# Is America Going to Strike Iran - What Markets Are Saying

## 1. Thesis

Across equities, currencies, volatility, oil, gold, and bonds, markets are pricing no imminent U.S. offensive entry into the Iran-Israel war: a shallow 0.5% S&P gap that faded, non-rallying yen/franc safe havens, a falling VIX into an FOMC day, oil back near Friday's close, lower gold, and softer long yields all point the same way. The one unrelated stress is in bills, where an August debt-ceiling hump inverts 1-month through 1-year yields and prices default risk, while Friday-Monday's 16bp rise in 10s shows how fast an oil-to-inflation scare can reprice duration.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] The shallow equity dip with Mag 7 leading and Dow flat signals low conviction that U.S. offensive operations are imminent.
- [TAKE] Yen and franc failing to rally while the dollar firms only 0.2% reads as no safe-haven rotation rather than hidden fear.
- [TAKE] A falling VIX into an FOMC meeting means investors are selling calls and puts instead of paying for hedges.
- [TAKE] Oil retracing Friday and gold falling two days in a row prices contained escalation rather than Hormuz closure.
- [TAKE] The bill-curve hump into August is debt-ceiling default pricing, separate from Iran, while softer long yields reflect fading oil-inflation premium plus weak retail sales.
