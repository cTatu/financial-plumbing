# The Dollar's Reserve Currency Curse - Triffin's Dilemma

## 1. Thesis

Reserve-currency status forces the United States to supply dollars through persistent trade and fiscal deficits, which overvalues the dollar, hollows out manufacturing and productivity, and leaves foreigners owning America — so the only durable exit is a Plaza-like reordering where all fiat devalues against gold while the dollar devalues most.

## 2. Key Claims

- [03:41] NBER data show United States gold near $20 billion backing about $60 billion of foreign dollar liabilities by 1970. (as of 1970 NBER)
- [06:15] Gold rose from $35 per ounce in 1968 to about $700 per ounce by 1980 as dollar purchasing power collapsed. (as of 1968-1980)
- [08:33] DXY surged from 86 to 130 within a couple years in the early 1980s on Volcker-era high rates. (as of early-1980s episode)
- [10:51] IMF 2023 purchasing-power-parity work puts dollar overvaluation at 90% versus 85% peaks in 1985 and the early 2000s and a 56% long-run average. (as of 2023 IMF)
- [14:31] The trade deficit runs $140 billion per month while the fiscal deficit runs about $2 trillion per year. (as of May 2025)
- [20:06] The current-account deficit prints $300 billion per quarter, or about $100 billion per month. (as of 2025-Q1 BEA)
- [28:20] Debt-to-GDP sits about 122% alongside a 6% of GDP full-employment deficit with no war. (as of May 2025)
- [31:54] Manufacturing employment fell from 26% of United States employment in 1953 to 7% today. (as of May 2025 BLS)
- [30:55] Manufacturing output fell from 13% of GDP to 10% of GDP over the past 20 years. (as of 2005-2025)
- [33:27] Manufacturing unit labor cost rose 53% from 86 in 2010 to 130 recently. (as of May 2025 BLS)

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] Attempting a balanced budget strengthens the dollar and blows out the trade deficit, so fiscal discipline alone deepens the Triffin trap.
- [TAKE] Financing the trade gap with Treasury issuance turns reserve demand into a structural bid for financial assets over plant and wages.
- [TAKE] The 2012-2013 QE peak marked the turn when foreigners stopped absorbing incremental debt and central banks rotated toward gold.
- [TAKE] Dollar strength plus offshoring lifted multinational margins while manufacturing productivity stagnated after the 2001 China shock.
- [TAKE] Nominal Treasuries must be devalued in real terms because nominal default will not occur and twin deficits cannot compound forever.
