# TIC Data: Foreign Purchases of US Securities is Grinding to a Halt

## 1. Thesis

July TIC shows headline inflows collapsing from $326 billion in May to $192 billion in June to $78.8 billion in July, and the mix is worse than the total: private Treasury buying shrank to $41 billion inside $72.4 billion total while 12-month official holdings fell $54 billion with Treasuries down $25.2 billion. With the UK as the second-largest holder at $900 billion, Cayman/Luxembourg fast money in the private share, and banks' dollar liabilities down $61 billion, the presenter argues de-dollarization is a slow, decade-long rotation from interest-bearing dollars into gold and euros rather than an overnight collapse.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] The buyer mix matters more than the headline because price-insensitive surplus holders like China and Japan are stepping back while twin-deficit Britain and 20-to-1 levered haven funds step in.
- [TAKE] De-dollarization is already a 10-to-20-year process because foreign-held share peaked 13 years ago and gold share of reserves bottomed near 10% after the GFC before rising from 2010.
- [TAKE] Dollar weakness plus gold doubling delevers Eurodollar debts instead of squeezing them because foreign incomes in euros buy more dollars while gold collateral appreciates.
- [TAKE] Falling bank dollar liabilities signal capital repatriation because foreigners pulling deposits and repaying dollar loans drains dollars from US banks.
- [TAKE] The post-cut bear steepener repeats September 2024 because 30-year yields rose after the Fed cut as developed sovereign pressure broadened to the UK, France, and Japan.
