# Job Losses Soar to Highest Level Since 2020 - Latest ADP Payrolls Report

## 1. Thesis

November ADP printed the weakest private-payrolls contraction in more than five years, but the composition matters more than the headline: small firms shed over 120,000 jobs while large firms still added payrolls, and losses concentrate in manufacturing, information, and professional-business services. The presenter frames this as demand for labor falling faster than a structurally shrinking supply — demographics plus negative net migration — so wage growth stays suppressed instead of rising, with AI-exposed white-collar sectors showing the persistent demand-side crack.

- [00:37] November ADP private payrolls lost 32,000 jobs versus a market expectation for a 10,000-job gain. (as of Nov 2025 ADP print)

- [03:43] The ISM manufacturing employment index has signaled contraction below 50 for basically three years. (as of Dec 2025 ISM history as cited)
- [04:09] Firms with 1 to 49 employees shed more than 120,000 jobs in November alone. (as of Nov 2025 ADP detail)
- [04:23] Firms with 500 or more employees added 39,000 private payrolls in November. (as of Nov 2025 ADP detail)
- [04:32] Construction lost 9,000 jobs, manufacturing lost 18,000 jobs, and information shed 20,000 jobs in November. (as of Nov 2025 ADP detail)
- [05:35] Education and health services added 33,000 jobs while leisure and hospitality added 13,000 jobs in November. (as of Nov 2025 ADP detail)

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] [02:34] Shrinking labor supply should lift wage growth if demand held steady, but demand is falling even faster so pay growth keeps trending down.
- [TAKE] [03:43] Manufacturing has effectively been in recession for three years, so the November goods-sector losses confirm a long contraction rather than a one-month shock.
- [TAKE] [05:06] Persistent weakness in information and professional-business services fits AI displacement, and the recurrence month after month should not be dismissed as coincidence.
- [TAKE] [05:59] Both parties previously grew labor supply faster than demand to suppress wage growth and protect bond-market borrowing capacity, and the current supply-shrink reverses that regime.
- [TAKE] [06:08] Falling labor demand from AI and productivity gains outrunning a shrinking supply produces poor wage growth and eventually elevated unemployment rather than a tight-market pay boom.

## 4C Predicts

- [PREDICTS] Falling labor demand outruns shrinking supply, keeping wage growth soft and pushing unemployment higher, potentially toward UBI-type policy discussion. -> check: job-stayer pay continuing down with ADP averages near zero and unemployment rising confirms it; wage reacceleration above 5% with payrolls rebounding refutes it.

## 7. Follow-ups

- Watch December ADP with size detail: another negative print led by 1-49 employee firms with large firms still adding confirms small-firm-led deterioration; a rebound above 100K with small firms positive says November was noise.
- Watch job-stayer vs job-changer pay with ISM employment: stayer pay falling further with ISM still below 50 confirms demand falling faster than supply; stayer pay reaccelerating toward 5% with ISM above 50 refutes it.
- Watch information and professional-business services hiring: continued losses in both sectors confirms AI-demand-crack persistence; renewed gains in both with construction rebounding says weakness broadened less than feared.
