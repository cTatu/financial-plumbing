# 30yr Bond Auction - Results Explained

## 1. Thesis

The $22 billion 30-year sale stopped through by about 2 basis points with 2.43x cover, backing up the prior day's strong 10-year with a second straight day of solid long-end demand, but the 30-year's 2-tails-in-4 record versus the 10-year's 4-stops-in-4 confirms structurally softer appetite for maximum convexity. With PPI cooling to 2.6% year over year yet still up from the summer-2023 bottom, the bond market is pricing fiscal supply and 30-year inflation risk rather than near-term producer-price relief.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] [05:51] The 30-year screens weaker than the 10-year because 2 tails in 4 auctions versus 4 stops in 4 shows investors demand extra convexity compensation at 30 years.
- [TAKE] [07:44] Lower primary-dealer take with steady 2.4x-type cover means end investors cleared the bonds rather than the buyer-of-last-resort backstop absorbing them.
- [TAKE] [05:12] Flat 2.4x to 2.5x cover across both days signals adequate depth, while the smaller stop size day-to-day tracks how far price must move to find the marginal buyer.
- [TAKE] [08:47] PPI falling almost 100 basis points since January helps near-term inflation optics but does not erase the uptrend from the summer-2023 bottom that keeps long-end term premium elevated.
- [TAKE] [04:53] A 1 to 2 basis-point stop is still bullish because Treasury funds below the market yield, which matters in dollars when $37 trillion of debt reprices by the basis point.
