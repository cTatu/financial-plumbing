# What Commodities Tell Us About the US Economy

## 1. Thesis

Measured against gold and money rather than dollars, the United States economy broke around 2000: real growth, commodity demand, wages, GDP, and manufacturing productivity all peaked versus monetary expansion, while fiscal deficits, financial assets, and top-1% wealth surged — evidence that post-financialization stimulus buys nominal GDP but no longer buys real productive growth.

## 2. Key Claims

- [03:19] Real GDP peaked at 4.5% in 2001, 4.3% in 2004, then fell to 3.2%, 3.6%, 3.1%, 2.9%, and about 3% on successive expansions. (as of Jun 2025 BEA)
- [08:52] From 1966 to 1990 commodities rose 250% while the SP500 rose 240%, but from 1994 to 2018 the SP500 rose 520% versus 67% for commodities. (as of 1966-2018)
- [08:52] Since 1925 the SP500 is up 71,000% while commodities are up only 1,400%. (as of 1925-2025)
- [09:15] SP500-over-commodities reached a 2.3-standard-deviation divergence at its peak since the mid-2010s versus 0.2 before the late 1990s. (as of Jun 2025)
- [16:54] The trade deficit widened from $8 billion before NAFTA to $28 billion before the China shock to $140 billion today. (as of 1993-2025)
- [20:06] One hour of work bought 0.06 ounces of gold in 2000 but only 0.01 ounces today, requiring a 6x wage rise to restore parity. (as of Jun 2025)
- [19:25] The inflation-adjusted Dow has gone sideways for 10 years without retesting its 2000 high. (as of Jun 2025)
- [03:38] Gold moved from about $1,700 to about $3,300 over the past two years. (as of 2023-2025)

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] Denominating wages, stocks, and commodities in gold or M2 exposes real purchasing-power loss hidden by nominal gains.
- [TAKE] Demand for commodities outpacing gold signals productive expansion, while gold outpacing commodities signals monetary hedging and stagnation.
- [TAKE] The fiscal deficit tracks real commodity weakness after the mid-1980s, so deficits now coincide with falling productive demand.
- [TAKE] Printing money lifts nominal GDP but leaves GDP/M2 and income/M2 falling, which means stimulus buys less real growth per dollar.
- [TAKE] Capital flows prefer elastic financial assets over inelastic real inputs, so financialization structurally favors the top 1% over wage earners.
