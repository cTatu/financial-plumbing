# Major Market Moves - Jerome Powell's Jackson Hole Speech

## 1. Thesis

Powell's Jackson Hole speech was mildly dovish, signaling a likely September cut on labor-market risk even with inflation still above target, and markets priced it instantly: stocks and Bitcoin bid, yields lower, dollar weaker. The substance is a Fed admitting its dual mandate is in tension — upside risks to both unemployment and inflation — while calling policy restrictive and abandoning the post-2008 makeup-inflation framework. The presenter's read is that cuts change little for households because mortgages price off the 10-year and 30-year, and cutting into 3.1% core with a 7% procyclical deficit and de-anchored expectations risks a bear steepener where long yields rise and the dollar sells off.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] [01:42] The dual mandate is in tension because unemployment risks rising while inflation risks rising, so the Fed's two tools push in opposite directions.
- [TAKE] [04:45] Shrinking labor supply via immigration restriction mechanically lifts wages for remaining workers, which the Fed reads as an inflation problem.
- [TAKE] [08:55] A tariff is a one-time tax-level shift, not monetary inflation, so sustained inflation requires the wage-price pass-through Powell called unlikely.
- [TAKE] [16:05] Calling policy restrictive signals room to cut because a 4.33% funds rate still sits ~200bp above 2.7% inflation.
- [TAKE] [22:31] Cutting too fast into a bear steepener lifts long yields and weakens the dollar, so mortgage and credit rates priced off the 10-year and 30-year barely improve.
