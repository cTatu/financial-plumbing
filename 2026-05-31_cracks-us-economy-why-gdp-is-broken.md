# Cracks in the US Economy - Why GDP is Broken

## 1. Thesis

Headline GDP growing at 1.6% hides a broken composition: consumption is 68% of GDP but over half of it is the top 10%, government outlays are 38% of the economy on a cash basis despite a falling NIPA G share, and private investment ex-AI is historically weak. With real income $32,000 per worker below the pre-2008 trend, savings down 51% and real income ex-transfers contracting at recession-like rates, only debt-funded hyperscaler AI capex keeps the print positive — so GDP will confirm recession long after the average household already lives it.

## 2. Key Claims

- [01:01] Q1 2026 real GDP grew at a 1.6% annualized rate versus a 3.0% long-run average, revised down from a 2.0% advance estimate. (as of Q1 2026 first revision)
- [02:43] Consumer spending rose from about 58.0% to about 68.0% of GDP while NIPA government consumption plus investment fell as a GDP share. (as of Q1 2026 BEA)
- [04:00] NIPA government consumption plus investment runs at $5.4 trillion versus $12.0 trillion in total federal outlays, with total spending up from 27.0% to 38.0% of GDP. (as of 2026)
- [06:34] Real inflation-adjusted consumer spending grew 2.1% over the past 12 months but shrank at a 0.4% rate over the past quarter. (as of Q1 2026 BEA)
- [07:35] The top 10.0% drive about 52.0% of consumer spending, equal to over one-third of US GDP. (as of 2026)
- [09:51] Excluding two AI capex subcomponents, Q1 GDP would be 0.7% and Q4 GDP would be negative 0.2%. (as of Q4 2025-Q1 2026)
- [11:02] Personal income per person trails the 1980-2008 trend by about $32,000 per worker per year since the 2008 break. (as of 2026)
- [12:44] The top 10.0% own 92.0% of household equity exposure while gross private investment drifts lower as a GDP share. (as of 2026)
- [16:24] Real personal income excluding transfers fell 1.76% on a 3-month annualized basis, a contraction seen only going into recessions. (as of May 2026)
- [17:35] Personal savings per worker fell to $3,600 from an $8,000 peak, with 12-month per-worker savings down 51.0%. (as of May 2026)

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] [03:52] NIPA government spending understates the fiscal footprint because it excludes transfers, Medicare, and interest, so the falling G share masks total outlays growing faster than GDP.
- [TAKE] [07:35] GDP is concentrated because one-third of the economy is the top 10% spending, so luxury softness plus bottom-90% stress leaves the print dependent on the wealth effect.
- [TAKE] [09:51] AI data-center capex from a handful of firms is the sole prop holding GDP positive, and that spending is increasingly debt-funded as free cash flow rolls over.
- [TAKE] [12:44] Corporate profits doubled as a GDP share while labor's share hit all-time lows, so profit growth and average-worker income compete for the same pie.
- [TAKE] [17:35] Households bridge negative real income growth only by dissaving and revolving credit, and that buffer is nearly exhausted at the second-lowest saving rate since 2008.
