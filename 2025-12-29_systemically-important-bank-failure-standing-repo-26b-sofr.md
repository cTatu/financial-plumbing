# Systemically Important Bank Failure? - Fed's Standing Repo Tapped for $26 Billion & SOFR Climbs

## 1. Thesis

Year-end funding stress is real but not a bank failure: the Standing Repo Facility drew $17.2 billion on Friday plus $26 billion on Monday while SOFR printed at 3.76%, one basis point above the Fed's 3.75% upper bound, because three years of QT plus a half-trillion-dollar TGA rebuild drained reserves from $4.2 trillion toward $2.9 trillion with the ON RRP buffer gone. Quarter-end window-dressing explains part of the spike, yet persistent SOFR-RRP widening and fresh reserve-management buying show a scarce-reserve regime the Fed already fears. The viral systemically-important-bank-blowup rumor traces to a ~3,000-subscriber Substack screenshot, and listed banks falling only 0.8-1.8% reject a market-priced failure.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] [03:40] QT destroys bank reserves while Treasury's $500 billion TGA rebuild removes a second liquidity leg, so the RRP shock absorber is gone when window-dressing hits.
- [TAKE] [08:43] SOFR reflects willing-and-able dollar supply rather than the Fed's posted range, which is why the 75th percentile can finance far above 3.75% while the median sits near the ceiling.
- [TAKE] [07:39] Reserve-management buying of short-dated bills prints reserves to cap short rates, whereas QE prints to buy long bonds and suppress long rates.
- [TAKE] [12:04] The bank-failure rumor fails market confirmation because a genuine UBS or money-center failure would reprice equity by far more than 1-2% with funding spreads blowing out.
