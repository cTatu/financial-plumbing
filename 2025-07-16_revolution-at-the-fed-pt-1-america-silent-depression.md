# Revolution at the Fed? - Pt 1: America's 'Silent Depression'

## 1. Thesis

Volcker-style rate hikes cannot work the way they did in the 1980s because the monetary plumbing has inverted: private bank credit creation has collapsed 71% as a share of base money since April 2008 and trails even GDP growth, so the government — running a 7%-of-GDP deficit at 125% debt-to-GDP versus 30-35% then — now creates the marginal dollar. That swap explains the silent depression: wages lagged GDP, M2, and gold for 25 years (an hour of work buys 80% less gold), while the trade deficit's mirror — about $150 billion per month of foreign capital surplus — plus QE inflated stocks, homes, corporate profits, and top-1% wealth far past output. The setup is why a fourth-turning reshaping of the Fed is on the table, and part two takes up the Malpass/Warsh proposals.

## 2. Key Claims

- [02:29] Federal debt-to-GDP ran only 30-35% into the Volcker hikes but stands near 125% today, a far more leveraged starting point. (as of Jul 2025)
- [10:06] The fiscal deficit ran about 1% of GDP in January 2008 but runs about 7% now, growing roughly 7 times faster than GDP. (as of Jul 2025 BEA/FRED)
- [05:26] The monetary base is only about $5 trillion while M2 money supply is about $22 trillion, about 4x leverage in the system. (as of Jul 2025 Fed)
- [07:57] Total bank loans have fallen 71% as a share of official government money since the April 2008 peak and have not kept pace with GDP since 2008. (as of Apr 2008-Jul 2025 Fed/FRED)
- [19:42] The current account deficit runs about $450 billion per quarter, requiring about $150 billion per month of foreign inflow into US financial assets. (as of Jul 2025 BEA)
- [15:50] One hour of average work bought about 0.06 ounces of gold in the early 2000s but buys about 0.01 ounces now, an 80% purchasing-power decline. (as of 2001-Jul 2025)
- [16:40] Personal interest payments per person are up 2,200% since 1968 while the average hourly wage is up only 969%. (as of 1968-Jul 2025)
- [22:10] The S&P 500 total return is up 5,000% since 1990 while the average hourly wage is up only 233%, a 20x outperformance. (as of 1990-Jul 2025)
- [22:47] The top 10% of earners own 93% of US equities with average holdings near $50,000, while corporate profits are up about 1,000% and top-1% net worth about 1,100%. (as of Jul 2025)
- [27:25] Buying one S&P 500 share took 15-17 hours of average work in the 1970s but takes about 190-200 hours now, about 10x more labor. (as of 1970s-Jul 2025)

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] [07:28] Volcker hikes restrained inflation because private bank credit created the marginal dollar then, while government money creates it now, so the same hikes land very differently.
- [TAKE] [19:42] A $450B-per-quarter trade deficit is arithmetically a $450B-per-quarter capital surplus that must inflate US financial assets and wreck home affordability.
- [TAKE] [21:20] QE was great for the top 1% and asset holders but punishing for wage earners because printed reserves flowed into bonds, stocks, and houses rather than paychecks.
- [TAKE] [28:29] The economy hyperfinancialized after 2001 as M2 velocity collapsed with the real productive economy, leaving deficits and the Fed to fill the private-credit void.
- [TAKE] [28:49] The S&P 500 effectively determines tax revenue because asset values underwrite receipts, so GDP is hostage to financial-asset prices.
