# Unraveling the Dollar: Its Power, Decline, and Role in the Global Economy

## 1. Thesis

The word dollar hides three different things — an exchange rate, domestic purchasing power, and a global funding network — and all three point the same way: the dollar is structurally overvalued on FX, steadily debased at home by money growth far above official inflation, and slowly losing its monopoly share of reserves and Treasury finance since the 2014/QE peak, which argues for coordinated devaluation against real assets rather than imminent collapse.

## 2. Key Claims

- [01:07] The DXY weights the euro at about 55%, the yen at about 15%, the pound at about 12%, and the Canadian dollar at about 10%. (as of May 2025)
- [03:49] The dollar has strengthened about 13% against advanced-economy currencies and about 34% against emerging-market currencies since 2005. (as of 2005-2025)
- [08:52] Currency in circulation is $2.3 trillion, the monetary base is $5.7 trillion, and M2 is $22 trillion. (as of May 2025)
- [09:12] M2 stands about 10 times levered to physical currency and about 4 times levered to the monetary base. (as of May 2025)
- [15:54] M2 money supply grew 172% faster than CPI over the past 30-35 years while gold grew 221% faster than CPI. (as of 1990-2025)
- [17:17] The United States must cover a current-account gap of $300 billion per quarter through capital inflow. (as of May 2025)
- [21:19] Foreigners hold about 23% of total United States Treasury debt, down from a 35% peak in 2014. (as of May 2025)
- [23:02] The dollar share of global FX reserves fell from 71% in 2000 to about 59% reported and 56% adjusted. (as of 2000-2024 IMF)
- [28:12] QE2 added about $600 billion and QE3 added about $1.2 trillion during 2012-2013 as foreign Treasury buying peaked. (as of 2012-2013)
- [30:32] The dollar is about 90% overvalued on IMF purchasing-power parity as of 2023 versus 85% peaks before the 1985 Plaza Accord and early-2000s top. (as of 2023 IMF)

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] A strong dollar makes American exports expensive abroad while making imports cheap at home, so demand for dollars directly suppresses domestic manufacturing.
- [TAKE] M2 at $22T against $2.3T of currency shows the spendable dollar supply is heavily levered to a small base of official money.
- [TAKE] CPI substitution lets the official index understate lived inflation, which is why M2 and gold outran CPI by 172% and 221%.
- [TAKE] The $300B quarterly current-account deficit must be matched by a capital-account surplus that pumps foreign savings into United States stocks, bonds, and real estate.
- [TAKE] Foreigners stepped back after 2014 while debt kept rising, so the Fed filled the gap with QE and central banks rotated toward gold.

## 4C Predicts

- [PREDICTS] A Mar-a-Lago-style reordering devalues the dollar against neutral assets like gold and Bitcoin while weaker foreign currencies strengthen more. -> check: coordinated FX language plus rising gold share of reserves and softer DXY by end-2025.
- [PREDICTS] Central-bank gold buying continues as Treasury foreign share keeps falling below 23%. -> check: IMF reserve composition and Treasury foreign-holdings share through 2025.
