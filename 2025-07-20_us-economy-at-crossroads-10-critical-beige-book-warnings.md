# U.S. Economy at Crossroads: 10 Critical 'Beige Book' Warnings for 2025

## 1. Thesis

The July Beige Book describes an economy losing momentum in real time: only 3 of 12 districts grow while 6 report slight-to-moderate declines, uncertainty reads pervasive, consumer spending growth collapses toward 0.5% quarter over quarter, manufacturing PMI sits at 48.5 in contraction since summer 2022, and revolving credit falls 3.2%. The bind is fiscal as much as cyclical: nominal growth peaked near 17% and now runs about 4.7% against a 5-year cost near that level and an average debt rate of 3.3% that no tenor can refinance, so $1 trillion-plus of interest at 20% of receipts (above defense) becomes the stimulus via a 75% spending propensity. Tariffs are framed as a 1.6%-on-a-$1,500-iPhone tax split three ways — too small and demand-destroying to explain inflation — while energy stands out as the lone bright spot with US output at 13.3 million barrels per day.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] [02:05] Nominal growth must stay above debt cost at 125% leverage, so growth sliding to 4.7% toward the 5-year yield turns debt dynamics from relief to burden.
- [TAKE] [03:26] Interest is someone else's income with a 75% spend rate, so $1 trillion of coupons acts as yearly stimulus checks that blunt rate hikes.
- [TAKE] [10:32] A 10% tariff on a $1,500 iPhone built on a $750 import cost adds $75 split three ways, so the consumer pays about $25 or 1.6%, which destroys demand rather than inflating.
- [TAKE] [11:28] Retailers cannot pass tariffs through because the store down the street undercuts them, so margin compression at names like Target extends instead.
- [TAKE] [27:18] Fed funds cuts cannot fix housing because mortgages price off the long end, which rises on fiscal risk when short rates are forced down.

## 4C Predicts
