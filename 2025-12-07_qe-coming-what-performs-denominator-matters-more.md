# QE's Coming: What to Do? What Performs Well? - Why Another Factor Matters EVEN MORE

## 1. Thesis

The outright size of the Fed's balance sheet barely explains asset returns — the DXY, gold, and the S&P 500 all show weak year-over-year correlation with its expansions and contractions — while two things dominate instead: inverted 10-year real yields, which track gold's annual change tightly until a 3-4 year breakdown, and the denominator itself. With bill finance plus bank leverage already monetizing debt and negative real yields described as mathematically required to service it, dollar-priced gains are mostly the denominator losing purchasing power: homes sit 83% cheaper in gold than in the early 2000s, so the unit of account matters more than the level of reserves.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] Balance-sheet size alone explains little because DXY, gold, and S&P year-over-year changes all show weak correlation with its expansions and contractions.
- [TAKE] Inverted 10-year real yields are the tighter signal because falling real compensation tracks gold's annual appreciation far better than reserve levels do.
- [TAKE] The recent 3-4 year breakdown of falling real yields with still-rising gold marks a regime break that balance-sheet arithmetic cannot explain.
- [TAKE] Bill finance already monetizes debt because banks lever money-like bills into new dollars while long-bond buyers cannot create money.
- [TAKE] Dollar pricing flatters assets because the denominator's purchasing-power collapse, not home or equity value, drives most of the nominal gain.

- [PREDICTS] The chosen denominator dominates outcomes, with gold-denominated homes and equities outperforming dollar readings. -> check: homes and S&P in gold terms continuing to fall in gold price confirms it; dollar readings keeping pace with gold readings refutes it.
