# Dollar's Biggest Drop Since 1973: Are We Headed for Crisis?

## 1. Thesis

The 10.8% first-half dollar fall is not a normal rate-differential move but a regime signal: Liberation Day broke the dollar's correlation with rising US-over-Europe yield spreads, the cutting cycle produced a bear steepener with 10-year yields up 65-90 basis points on 100 basis points of cuts while the 30-year crossed above nominal growth, and with debt at 124% of GDP the setup rhymes with both post-1947 yield-curve control and 1965-1982 stagflation — where pressure for a 300-basis-point politically driven cut plus activist short-end issuance points toward financial repression in which scarce assets outperform long bonds.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] [02:44] Liberation Day marked a correlation break because higher US-over-Europe spreads would normally lift the dollar, yet the dollar kept falling.
- [TAKE] [04:52] Rising 10-year, 20-year, and 30-year yields through 100.00 basis points of cuts form a bear steepener that prices fiscal and inflation risk rather than easier money.
- [TAKE] [08:00] The 10-year crossing above nominal growth turns debt-to-GDP dynamics adverse outside recession, which is the emerging-market-like yield-curve-control trigger.
- [TAKE] [26:36] Demanding a 300.00-basis-point funds cut while starving the long end and flooding bills is stealth easing that suppresses measured long yields while adding cash-like liquidity.
- [TAKE] [29:05] Long bonds lose in stagflation because 1965-1982 left the S and P 500 flat in dollars and down 70.00%-75.00% in gold, so scarcity with fixed or falling supply dominates.
