# Another Bond Auction Flop + Hot GDP Report

## 1. Thesis

Headline strength hides the split: Q2 real GDP printed 3.3% on a tariff-front-running import reversal that mechanically lifts growth arithmetic, pushing nominal growth near 6% and temporarily burying debt cost below growth — while the same session's $44B 7-year auction tailed with cover collapsing from 2.79x to 2.50x, extending a run where short auctions clear on cut hopes and belly-to-long duration demands concession. The curve message is a bear steepener driven by two different machines: the short end prices a September cut with core CPI at 3.1%, while the long end prices fiscal supply, sticky inflation, and activist bill funding that has already eroded Fed independence.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] The hot GDP print reflects tariff timing because front-running inflated Q1 imports and flattered Q2 when imports collapsed, so the 3.3% says little about final demand.
- [TAKE] Nominal growth near 6% temporarily puts the 10-year yield below growth and relieves debt arithmetic, but the relief lasts only as long as real plus inflation stay elevated.
- [TAKE] Real consumer spending tells the weaker story because nominal PCE rises with prices while inflation-adjusted PCE has made no new high since December 2024.
- [TAKE] Auction demand fades with duration because the 2-year cleared strong on cut pricing while the 5-year and 7-year tailed, showing buyers demand concession to absorb longer supply.
- [TAKE] The curve trades two regimes at once with the short end following expected funds cuts and the long end charging for inflation, supply, and independence risk, which is why mortgages price off the 10-year and 30-year rather than the funds rate.
