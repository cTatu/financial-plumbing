# Inflation Plummets, Is Kevin Warsh Going to Crash the Economy & Labor Market Update

## 1. Thesis

January CPI cooled to 2.4% year-over-year with core at its lowest since April 2021, but the disinflation is narrow — energy dragged while electricity and piped gas still run hot — and markets read it through a new Fed-chair lens. The episode argues Kevin Warsh cannot deliver the hawkish balance-sheet shrink markets fear because reserves are already near the dysfunction floor that protects the repo-financed basis trade, so fiscal dominance still forces front-end funding and rate cuts to contain $1.2T of interest; alongside a yen/JGB news-failure rally after Japan's populist landslide and a labor market where hiring dynamics look late-cycle, the tape is pricing productivity-led disinflation with sticky volatility rather than a clean all-clear.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] [07:43] Warsh hawkishness is misplaced because the Fed cannot shrink the balance sheet toward $3.5 trillion when RRP is already empty at $1 billion and currency grows steadily.
- [TAKE] [09:42] Bank reserves cannot fall much below $2.8-$2.9 trillion without repo dysfunction that endangers the basis trade funding about half of new Treasury issuance.
- [TAKE] [11:50] Fiscal dominance did not disappear with the Warsh nomination, so the Fed must keep cutting the overnight rate and funding in T-bills to arrest interest-expense growth.
- [TAKE] [07:12] Japan's post-landslide rally in bonds and yen is a news failure that may mark a 12- to 18-month turning point toward sustained yen strength.
- [TAKE] [18:21] The productivity boom is disinflationary but can go too far by cutting labor demand faster than supply, which explains record-low labor share and collapsing confidence despite ~5% GDP nowcasts.
