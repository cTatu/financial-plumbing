# Globalism: Who Really Benefited? - How Trump is Mirroring…. Pro-labor Democrats?

## 1. Thesis

The data verdict on hyperglobalization is that corporate profits doubled from 6% to 12% of GDP after NAFTA while the S&P, federal debt, and top-1% net worth all outran GDP and the average worker's wage fell relentlessly against GDP, gold, and M2. The video argues the mechanism is reserve-currency arithmetic: a $122-billion-per-month trade deficit forces a matching capital-account surplus into Treasuries, equities, and homes, creating a price-insensitive bid that multinationals, asset holders, and Washington capture while manufacturing employment collapses. Trump therefore mirrors the pro-labor Democrats of the 1990s and 2000s who warned NAFTA and WTO entry would sacrifice workers, and balancing trade over a decade becomes the bipartisan project to reverse wealth concentration even at the cost of lower equity multiples and higher yields.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] [03:19] Hyperglobalization enriched multinationals and Washington because wider deficits mechanically force larger capital-account inflows into profits, equities, debt, and elite net worth.
- [TAKE] [16:17] Relative charts expose the worker's loss because nominal wage gains reverse into relentless decline once divided by GDP, gold, or M2.
- [TAKE] [32:50] Manufacturing was never sweatshops but high-value domestic plants, so its falling employment share explains why wage purchasing power tracks industrial decline.
- [TAKE] [42:07] Foreign recycling is price-insensitive because surplus holders buy dollar assets to place funds rather than on valuation, which explains extreme equity multiples.
- [TAKE] [47:41] Ending reserve status is the stakes of balanced trade because eliminating the deficit also eliminates the dollar supply the world needs.
