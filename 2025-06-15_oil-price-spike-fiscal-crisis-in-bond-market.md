# Oil Price Spike = Fiscal Crisis in Bond Market?

## 1. Thesis

Escalating Israel-Iran strikes on refineries and depots threaten to double $65 oil toward the $120 JPMorgan tail, and that shock lands where the Jun 13 video left off but through the domestic fiscal channel: the 10-year already sits just above and the 20-year and 30-year well above fading nominal growth, with a 3.3% average debt coupon against nothing on the curve below 4%. A $30 replay of 2022's $90-to-$120 move added 140bp to the 10-year, so even the 65bp ex-Fed-hike remainder now pushes the 10-year over 5% — where recession widens a 6.5% deficit toward 15-18%, more supply lifts rates, and higher interest is funded with yet more deficit in a self-perpetuating loop the bill-heavy issuance schedule cannot absorb.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] Nominal growth above debt cost is sustainable but inverted cost-above-growth is not, and the 10-year through 30-year now sit on the wrong side.
- [TAKE] Oil lowers inflation-adjusted growth with a lag while lifting CPI within months, which is textbook stagflation when both move together.
- [TAKE] A scarred Fed that called inflation transitory leans toward hiking a 0.5%-plus monthly CPI print, and bill-concentrated issuance transmits any hike to interest expense within months.
- [TAKE] Recession does not rescue bonds here because a 6.5% deficit widening toward 15-18% means supply surges exactly when growth fails.
- [TAKE] More supply into flat or falling demand lifts yields, higher yields lift interest, and interest is funded with more deficit in a loop that feeds on itself.
