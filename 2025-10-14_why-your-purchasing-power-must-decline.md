# Why Your Purchasing Power MUST Decline

## 1. Thesis

Deflation is mathematically outlawed in a debt-based monetary system where ~90% of money is private bank credit, banks run ~10-to-1 leverage, and government debt sits at 124% of GDP: any sustained fall in prices collapses incomes and tax revenue while the nominal debt stock stays fixed, forcing defaults, bank failures, and a lending freeze that shrinks the money supply itself. Because a Great Depression-style 45% GDP fall would turn a $2 trillion deficit into an $8 trillion deficit near 50% of GDP, the state must keep nominal growth and M2 expanding at ~5-6% and monetize the gap — so the dollar's purchasing power asymptotically goes to zero by design, with the 1928-1934 episode of 37-38% purchasing-power gain possible only at 16% debt-to-GDP under a gold standard that allowed a 68% devaluation.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] Deflation raises the real debt burden because a $100,000 loan balance stays fixed while incomes and asset prices fall.
- [TAKE] Bank leverage near 10-to-1 turns income loss into defaults and forced selling because margin calls liquidate collateral into falling markets.
- [TAKE] Private banks must keep lending because roughly 90% of broad money is bank-created credit that vanishes when lending stops.
- [TAKE] The government absorbed the equity-to-housing bubble chain by doubling its own debt from 62% to 124% of GDP because no larger private bubble remained after 2008.
- [TAKE] Post-2008 M0 and Fed-balance-sheet expansion sterilized deleveraging because official money rose from 11% to 26% of M2 to prevent a deflationary cascade.
