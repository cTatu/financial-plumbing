# Liquidity Stress Re-Emerges in Highly Unusual Afternoon Repo Use

## 1. Thesis

Quarter-end calm was a one-day illusion — after a textbook Dec 31 window-dressing spike ($105B parked in RRP, SOFR 3.87% above the 3.75% bound on record $3.48T volume), Jan 2 flipped the presenter's RRP-minus-SRF spread from +$32B to its second-most-negative since mid-2020 as banks drew ~$23B from the standing facility. The tell is location and timing: ~$20B came in the afternoon window that runs near-zero 98-99% of the time, coinciding with a 30-year breakout to a multi-month high and the widest 30Y-2Y steepener since 2021. With reserves down to ~$2.8T on QT plus ~$0.5T of TGA drain and the RRP buffer empty, the Fed's short-bill reserve-management buying is bridge financing, not a fix — which is why Friday's SOFR print becomes the referendum.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] RRP and SRF must be read as a spread because double-entry mechanics make them opposite liquidity signals, and the flip from +$32B to deeply negative means Jan 2 stress is scarcity returning, not quarter-end noise.
- [TAKE] Afternoon SRF use is the smoking gun because that window almost never prints, so ~$20B past quarter-end shows borrowers missed morning funding or paid the penalty rate to meet late-day needs.
- [TAKE] QT plus TGA drain destroyed the RRP shock absorber, so shrinking reserves now transmit directly into SOFR spreads and SRF taps instead of being cushioned by idle cash.
- [TAKE] The Fed's bill buying prints reserves to buy short-term Treasuries, which suppresses short-term yields without the duration signal of QE, and its five-month reserve impulse tracks BTC more tightly than any other risk asset.
