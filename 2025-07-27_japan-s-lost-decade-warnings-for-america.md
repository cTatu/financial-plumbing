# Japan's Lost Decade: Warnings for America?

## 1. Thesis

Japan's 1980s bubble was a policy-made leverage cycle rather than an unavoidable bust: post-Plaza yen strength threatened its export-surplus model, so the Bank of Japan cut real rates to zero while deregulated banks lent beyond collateral value, driving stocks up 440% and land to extremes before a 2.5%-to-6% hike popped both. America rhymes on speculation and easy money but differs on the constraint that matters — Japan entered with 65% debt, a 4%-plus surplus, and 80%-plus creditor savings that could absorb deflation, while the US enters with 120%-plus debt, a 5% deficit, and negative 100% NIIP that cannot.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] [11:31] The 1985 Plaza Accord reversed an overvalued dollar, so the yen strengthened for a decade and directly threatened Japan's surplus-driven export model.
- [TAKE] [20:58] Cutting real rates to zero to weaken the yen while deregulated banks lent 1.5 million yen against 1 million yen homes fused central-bank stimulus with private credit creation into one bubble.
- [TAKE] [28:12] A return to a historically normal 3% real rate was enough to pop the bubble because leverage taken at zero had piled so high that collateral could not survive normalization.
- [TAKE] [29:53] Japan could endure decades of deflation because 65% starting debt and a surplus-fed creditor stock absorbed it, while 125% US debt in a 20-to-30-times levered system turns collateral decline into a debt doom loop.
- [TAKE] [30:02] The structural divide is surplus versus deficit: Japan accumulated national savings and over 1 trillion dollars of Treasuries, while America accumulated only debt and negative savings.
