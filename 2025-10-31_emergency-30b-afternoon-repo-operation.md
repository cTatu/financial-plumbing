# Emergency Update: ANOTHER $30B Tapped in Afternoon Repo Operation at Fed

## 1. Thesis

The afternoon Standing Repo Facility drew another $30 billion, taking October 31 to a $50.3 billion all-day record since the facility became standing, and the quantity leg now agrees with the price leg: SOFR sat above the Fed's ceiling on 6 of the past 13 days while the reverse-repo-minus-standing-repo buffer sits near zero after peaking near $2.5 trillion in 2022-2023. With bank reserves down from about $3.4 trillion to about $2.8 trillion and the SOFR-minus-RRP average vertical since July, the regime has shifted from abundant to scarce liquidity short of a 2019-style 900bp break, which is why Logan's if-not-temporary-then-buy-assets language frames reserve rebuilding as the next step and leaves only month-end window-dressing fade as near-term relief.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] The $50.3 billion all-day SRF total matters because price and quantity agree, with record emergency borrowing alongside SOFR above the ceiling rather than an isolated calendar print.
- [TAKE] A near-zero and briefly negative RRP-minus-SRF balance means the abundant-liquidity buffer is gone, so further drains must come directly from bank reserves.
- [TAKE] SOFR above the discount-window ceiling signals the Fed is losing control of the overnight cost of capital because free-market supply and demand is clearing above the administered ceiling.
- [TAKE] Fed funds drifting higher relative to the RRP floor while rate cuts land confirms the same scarcity on the smaller unsecured leg, not just secured repo.
- [TAKE] Logan's buy-assets language is the policy tell because the only durable fix for falling reserves is balance-sheet expansion, while rate cuts give only marginal relief.

- [PREDICTS] Without reserve relief, scarcity keeps building toward 2019-style dislocation rather than fading. -> check: reserves lower with the SOFR-RRP average still vertical confirms it; reserves stabilizing with spreads compressing refutes it.
