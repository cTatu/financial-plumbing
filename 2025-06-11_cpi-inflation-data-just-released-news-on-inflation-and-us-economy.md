# CPI Inflation Data Just Released - News on Inflation and US Economy

## 1. Thesis

May CPI extends the disinflation from the 9% 2022 peak to 2.4% headline and 2.8% core, but the relief is double-edged: falling inflation narrows the nominal-growth cushion while 10-year near 4.45% and 30-year near 5% sit above 4.3-4.4% nominal growth, so the debt burden tightens even as markets cheer lower prints, while food reaccelerating to 2.9%, Michigan expectations at 6.5%, and gold up 70% since the 2023 breakout warn that official CPI understates lived inflation and that asset-price inflation near 40% since 2019 is the real purchasing-power tax on wages.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] Disinflation still means prices rise, so 2.4% CPI compounds the 9%-peak base rather than restoring the pre-2020 price level.
- [TAKE] Falling inflation helps consumers but hurts the fiscal position because lower CPI with flat real growth drags nominal growth further below bond yields.
- [TAKE] Official 2.4% CPI coexists with 6.5% household expectations and a 70% gold repricing because substitution, owners-equivalent rent, and basket changes compress the index.
- [TAKE] Housing is not appreciating in real terms but providing levered exposure to debasement, which is why the dollar price tracks the 40% money expansion while the gold price sits near record affordability.
- [TAKE] The market reaction of lower 2-year yields, a softer dollar toward 98.60, and higher equities prices faster cuts, but the 30-year flat near 5% shows duration still charges for the deficit.

## 4C Predicts

- [PREDICTS] Continued disinflation with flat real growth widens the yield-minus-growth gap and tightens the debt burden. -> check: CPI lower with nominal GDP near 4% while 10-year/30-year hold above it; nominal reacceleration above yields refutes it.
- [PREDICTS] Gold keeps pricing purchasing-power loss ahead of CPI as central-bank and investor demand front-runs further debasement. -> check: gold holding above $3,000 with net central-bank buying; gold back below the $2,000 breakout refutes it.
- [PREDICTS] Asset prices track money rather than CPI, leaving wages further behind homes and equities. -> check: home prices and SPX versus M2 and wages over next year; wages outpacing both refutes it.
