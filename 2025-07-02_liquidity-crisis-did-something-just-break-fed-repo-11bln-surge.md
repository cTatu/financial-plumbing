# Liquidity Crisis? Did Something Just Break? - Fed's Repo $11bln Surge

## 1. Thesis

The $11.075 billion standing-repo take-up on June 30 is different from routine quarter-end noise because private SOFR spiked 11-12 basis points over fed funds at the same time the Fed's penalty-rate, stigma-bearing lender-of-last-resort window was tapped for the first time since summer 2020 — while the Fed has shrunk its balance sheet from $9 trillion to $6.6 trillion and drained bank reserves, which points to quantitative tightening approaching its end and a return toward balance-sheet expansion within 9-12 months.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] [02:40] Standing-repo use signals stress because borrowers pay a higher rate than the private market and accept stigma to bring the principal into the trade.
- [TAKE] [07:19] A positive SOFR-minus-fed-funds spread means private demand for dollars exceeds private supply, so overnight money reprices above the policy rate.
- [TAKE] [10:28] QT drains bank reserves and one-half of the M0 monetary base, reproducing the pre-September-2019 setup at much larger scale.
- [TAKE] [14:27] A falling TGA normally adds liquidity by spending locked-up cash into the economy, so stress persisting through a $456.00 billion TGA draw strengthens the reserves-too-low read.
- [TAKE] [20:01] Tapering QT does not force immediate QE because the Fed previously held flat with neither expansion nor contraction through the mid-2010s.
