# How America's Fiscal Crisis Could Cause A Liquidity Crisis, Thereby Blowing up the $1.8T Basis Trade

## 1. Thesis

The deficit is compounding faster than the economy while refinancing at higher rates, and because foreign demand has shifted away from the long bond toward bills, the Treasury must roll $550B every five days and hold a near-$900B cash buffer. That buffer was rebuilt from reserves because the reverse-repo tank was empty, so falling bank reserves are widening SOFR spreads — the exact funding strain that can force an unwind of the $1.8T repo-financed cash-futures basis trade and dump Treasuries the way March 2020 and early April did.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] [04:45] Shifting maturing coupons into bills creates a treadmill where the deficit compounds at 8% while the 5-day roll forces a permanently larger TGA buffer.
- [TAKE] [05:17] Rebuilding the TGA from $300B to $850B had to drain bank reserves because the reverse-repo tank held only about $150B at announcement.
- [TAKE] [06:01] Falling bank reserves widen SOFR spreads in a tight inverse correlation, so TGA growth directly tightens dollar liquidity.
- [TAKE] [08:39] The $1.80T basis trade is long cash Treasuries against short futures and is financed in repo, so wider SOFR stretches its carry and forces Treasury selling.
- [TAKE] [09:35] Rolling old low-coupon duration into higher-rate bills lifts interest expense and the deficit itself, reinforcing the loop.
