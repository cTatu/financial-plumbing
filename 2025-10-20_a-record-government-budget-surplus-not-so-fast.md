# A Record Government Budget Surplus? Not So Fast....

## 1. Thesis

September's $198 billion surplus is a calendar and accounting illusion, not a turn in the fiscal path: $88 billion of Labor Day-shifted payments plus a $130 billion one-time student-loan accounting gain flip a deficit month into a surplus headline, while the full-year picture is $5.2 trillion of revenue against $7.0 trillion of spending for a $1.77 trillion deficit essentially unchanged from fiscal 2024. The structure keeps deteriorating underneath — individual income tax up 6% while corporate receipts fall 14%, tariff revenue up 142% but total receipts up only 6-7%, and Medicare, Social Security, and interest each growing faster than revenue with spending doubling from $4.5 trillion pre-2020 to $7.1 trillion. With the average marketable-debt coupon at 3.3% below even the 2-year low of 3.46%, interest at 23% of receipts and $3 billion per day can only rise, which is why each new dollar of debt buys only $0.33 of GDP since 1971.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] The September surplus is a timing artifact because shifting $100 billion to $120 billion of military pay, VA, Social Security, and Medicare from September 1 into August mechanically depresses September outlays.
- [TAKE] Entitlements plus interest drive the deficit because Medicare at 24% of revenue and Social Security at 30% of revenue each grow faster than the 6-7% revenue base while defense is flat-to-down as a revenue share.
- [TAKE] Households carry the revenue load because individual receipts rise 6% while corporate receipts fall 14%, concentrating funding on wage and equity-driven taxes.
- [TAKE] Tariffs cannot close the gap because a 142% cumulative surge still leaves total receipts up only 6-7% against 3-4% spending growth on a $7 trillion base.
- [TAKE] Interest must keep rising because every maturity refinances above the 3.3% average coupon when even the cheapest 2-year point sits at 3.46%.
