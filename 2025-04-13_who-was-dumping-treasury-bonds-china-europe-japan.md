# Who Was Dumping US Treasury Bonds? China? Europe? Japan?

## 1. Thesis

After a week in which bonds sold off while the dollar fell, the video builds a forensic test for the "foreigners did it" question: compare each bloc's 10-year interest-rate differential against the US to its currency, since money should flow toward the higher yield. China, Europe, and Japan all break that relationship at the same moment — differentials fall while the yuan, euro, and yen strengthen — exactly as the US 10-year yield spikes. The argument is not definitive proof but a plausibility case that central banks, banks, or businesses in all three blocs rotated out of Treasuries into home currency, which domestic-only deleveraging cannot explain, with Treasury TIC data five to six weeks out as the arbiter.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] [03:13] The sell-then-convert mechanic explains the joint move because a foreign holder sells Treasuries for dollars, then sells those dollars for yuan, euros, or yen, pushing US yields up and the dollar down.
- [TAKE] [08:05] All three blocs show the same tell because falling rate differentials should have weakened their currencies, yet the yuan, euro, and yen strengthened while the US 10-year sold off.
- [TAKE] [20:50] A purely domestic hedge-fund and bank deleveraging cannot explain dollar weakness because a margin-call unwind typically bids the dollar higher, not lower.
- [TAKE] [16:17] The test is quick-and-dirty because rate differentials are only one driver among data releases, trade flows, and positioning in a multi-trillion-dollar FX market.
