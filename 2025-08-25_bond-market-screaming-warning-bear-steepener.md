# The Bond Market is Screaming a Warning - Is Anyone Listening?

## 1. Thesis

Every post-1950s Fed cutting cycle produced a bull steepener where all yields fell and shorts fell fastest — except today and 1980, where cuts met rising long yields in a bear steepener. In 1980 the bond market forced Volcker to reverse from 14.8% back to 19% and accept 11% unemployment; today the same signal is flashing with 100bp of cuts met by 100bp higher 30-year, 7% deficits, and de-anchored expectations. The presenter argues Powell's dovish Jackson Hole tilt ignores the warning, so without a Volcker-style reversal the release valve is a weaker dollar and higher inflation rather than a Volcker-style recession.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] A bull steepener means all yields fall with shorts falling fastest, while a bear steepener means bond values fall and yields rise despite cuts.
- [TAKE] The 10-year and 30-year price nominal growth, inflation, and fiscal supply rather than the funds rate, so cuts cannot force them lower.
- [TAKE] The 1980 bond selloff reflected trauma from 14% inflation plus wage reacceleration and oil doubling, so investors demanded compensation for easing risk.
- [TAKE] Raising rates worked in 1980 because private banks printed 85-90% of money, while today M0 government money dominates post-2008 and blunts the channel.
- [TAKE] Without a Volcker-style reversal the adjustment still arrives through a weaker dollar and higher inflation rather than through lower long yields.
