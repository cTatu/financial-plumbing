# National Debt to Surge by $1 Trillion - Will a Dollar Liquidity Squeeze Cause Cracks in the System?

## 1. Thesis

Treasury's plan to borrow about $1 trillion in one quarter while rebuilding the TGA cash buffer to $850 billion drains roughly half a trillion dollars of dollar liquidity at the exact moment QT continues and the RRP buffer sits near $150 billion. That supply shock argues for a near-term dollar squeeze and higher long-end yields, but it collides with a fiscal-dominance push for bill-heavy issuance and rate cuts that would steepen the curve and tighten mortgage, card, and auto rates for median households even as overnight rates fall.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] [03:22] A rising TGA mechanically removes dollars from circulation while a falling TGA spends them back in, so refilling to $850 billion is dollar-positive and a near-term squeeze force.
- [TAKE] [05:51] TGA, RRP, and QT all tighten at once, so $500 billion of TGA rebuild against only $150 billion of RRP buffer with $95 billion monthly QT is a net Fed-liquidity drain.
- [TAKE] [23:37] Pushing $1 trillion of new supply with flat demand lifts yields, although some TGA-refill supply may already be priced as a buy-the-rumor-sell-the-news dip first.
- [TAKE] [25:49] cutting the funds rate from 4.3% toward 1% with unemployment at 4.1% and core CPI near 3% reads as fiscal dominance that lifts 10-year, 20-year, and 30-year yields on inflation and solvency risk.
- [TAKE] [27:20] Overweighting bill issuance with deep funds-rate cuts engineers negative real bills that lever private credit creation, so the curve steepens and mortgage, card, and auto rates rise while overnight rates fall.
