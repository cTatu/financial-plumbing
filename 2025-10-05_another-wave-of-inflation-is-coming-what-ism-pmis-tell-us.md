# Another Wave of Inflation is Coming - What ISM PMIs Tell Us

## 1. Thesis

The September ISM prints point to reaccelerating nominal growth with inflation leading output: services at roughly 70% of GDP keep new orders in expansion while the prices components in both manufacturing and services sit deep in expansion — services prices at 69.4 and manufacturing prices at 61.9 — and the two-month lead of a smoothed ISM average over CPI has historically marked the path of headline inflation from its 3% perch. With core CPI at 3.1% and core PCE at 2.9% after three Fed cuts since September 2024, the surveys argue the next move is higher prices and firmer real GDP, not disinflation back to 2%.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] Services new orders lead real GDP by about 2 months because a smoothed 3-month average removes monthly noise at 70% GDP weight.
- [TAKE] Services and manufacturing prices jointly lead CPI by about 2 months because purchase-price surveys capture pipeline pressure before consumer prints.
- [TAKE] Averaging manufacturing with services prices sharpens the inflation signal because the combined 80% coverage beats either survey alone.
- [TAKE] Cutting into 3% inflation with assets at all-time highs reveals policy priorities because labor softness is cited while price pressure reaccelerates.
- [TAKE] Tariff pass-through plus hot PMIs lift nominal growth mechanically because real output and consumer prices rise together.
