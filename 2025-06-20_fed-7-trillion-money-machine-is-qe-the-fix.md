# The Fed's $7 Trillion Money Machine: Is QE the Fix for the Next Crisis?

## 1. Thesis

QE is an asset swap that prints bank reserves to buy bonds and MBS, lifting bond prices while suppressing yields so debt cost stays below nominal growth — but each cycle leaves a larger balance sheet, richer asset holders, and worse affordability. With $6.6-6.68T still on the books including $2T-plus MBS and $3.59T Treasuries, 10s up from 2.6% at the $9T peak, and S&P/gold down since QE began despite nominal records, the next stress will likely be met with balance-sheet expansion rather than deep rate cuts because cuts alone would lose the long end entirely.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] QE deposits reserves at major banks in exchange for bonds, removing duration collateral while adding dollars rather than printing unbacked cash.
- [TAKE] QE arrives in deflationary stress like 2008 and 2020 and QT restrains overheating, so balance-sheet direction reveals the regime better than any single CPI print.
- [TAKE] Fed MBS buying raises bond prices and lowers mortgage rates, which subsidizes housing risk and prices wage earners out of homes.
- [TAKE] Suppressing yields below nominal growth keeps 4.50% growth above debt cost, but 10s and 30s at or above 4.40% growth mark the limit of that arithmetic.
- [TAKE] Letting collateral fall to real value would mean ~80.00% lower stocks and ~60.00% lower housing, so the system is structured to choose inflation over deflation.

## 4C Predicts

- [PREDICTS] The next stress is solved with balance-sheet expansion to cap longs, not funds-rate cuts that steepen the curve. -> check: QT ends and securities holdings rise with 10-year and 30-year falling; aggressive cuts with QT continuing refutes it.
- [PREDICTS] Renewed QE lifts scarce assets with gold and Bitcoin front-running the balance-sheet turn. -> check: gold and BTC making highs into the next expansion; gold and BTC falling through expansion refutes it.
- [PREDICTS] More money printing widens the 54-to-200 hour financialization gap rather than closing it. -> check: S&P/wage and top-1%/GDP rising with next QE; ratios falling with broad wage catch-up refutes it.
