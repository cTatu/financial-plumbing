# Jobs Report Pt 2: Wages and Incomes + More Dollar Liquidity Stress?

## 1. Thesis

Nominal pay looks healthy only until it is divided by the money stock: M2 grows 5.0% per year against 2.7% headline inflation, yet wage growth has trailed M2 for 25 years, leaving total wages down from 88% to about 50% of M2 and personal income down from 182% to 117% of M2. The same debasement shows in gold, where an average hour buys 0.01 ounces versus 0.08-0.09 in the 1970s. Into this the Fed faces a funding market that will not normalize — SOFR sits at or above the 4.40% IORB rate days after the September 4 flag, a rare non-quarter-end print — while bonds price a September cut even as core CPI at 3.1% and PPI above 3% argue the inflation leg is re-accelerating.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] [02:54] Inflation means money-supply expansion with rising prices as the consequence, so wages must outgrow M2 to gain purchasing power.
- [TAKE] [06:10] Gold tracks monetary inflation across decades rather than days, which is why 0.01 ounces per hour diagnoses debasement rather than lost labor value.
- [TAKE] [07:40] SOFR failing to mean-revert after spiking while printing above IORB signals persistent funding tightness rather than quarter-end noise.
- [TAKE] [09:27] Twist steepening with long yields above the cut-cycle start while the 2-year falls prices cuts without validating duration, preserving the bear-steepener frame.
- [TAKE] [12:15] Cutting rates into 3%-plus inflation with new orders above 50 repeats the easing-into-reacceleration error that forces devaluation against gold.
