# HUGE Bond Auction + Tariff Dividend Check - How the Bond Market's Responding to Stimmy Check 2.0

## 1. Thesis

The short end still prices Fed cuts while the long end prices fiscal risk: a stellar $58 billion 3-year auction with a 1bp stop-through and 2.85 cover contrasts with the 30-year gapping to 4.73% after Trump's $2,000 tariff-dividend float. Rebating the full ~$350 billion of annualized tariff revenue would erase the deficit offset the bond market already priced in and force ~$350 billion of extra supply, which reads as Stimmy Check 2.0 — high-velocity checks to lower-K spenders that lift CPI and asset prices together, repeating the 2021-2022 pattern where the long end sold off about 80bp since cuts began.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] Short-end strength signals expected Fed cuts because 2-year and 3-year demand rises when the market prices lower overnight rates.
- [TAKE] Rebating all $350B of tariff revenue negates the deficit buffer the bond market priced in and forces $350B of extra bond supply at constant demand, so prices must fall and yields must rise.
- [TAKE] Helicopter checks are doubly inflationary because high-MPC lower-K spending lifts CPI velocity while surplus cash lifts housing, stocks, meme names, and crypto.
- [TAKE] The long end trades nominal growth plus fiscal term premium while the short end trades the Fed path, which explains the steepening mix with the 10-year minus Fed spread near 2-year highs.
- [TAKE] A 10.00% betting-market pass probability explains why spot long yields faded after the 4.73% gap, but any move toward passage would reprice supply higher.
