# US Tries to Sell $22 Billion in Long Bonds + A Chart That Should Scare Bond Bulls

## 1. Thesis

The $22B 30-year cleared decently at 4.77% with a 0.1bp stop-through, 2.36 cover, and only 11.1% dealer takedown the day after a dovish FOMC and return to bill-buying — but small size flatters it via activist issuance that starves duration. With the 30-year up from 3.9% to 4.8% since cuts began, no funding point below the 3.3% average debt cost, and TLT-to-gold still collapsing on a dividend-adjusted basis, one okay auction inside a global duration bear market does not make a bond bull case.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] Small size clears price because constant pension and insurer liability-matching demand meets artificially scarce duration, so B-grades overstate true duration appetite.
- [TAKE] Indirect strength overstates foreign demand because Cayman-based basis-trade hedge funds route through intermediaries and print as indirects.
- [TAKE] The curve keeps bear-steepening because long yields rise into cuts while bills follow funds down, leaving every funding tenor above the 3.3% average cost.
- [TAKE] Financial repression is the stated game because only nominal growth above the debt cost or negative real rates stabilizes debt-to-GDP without outright default.
- [TAKE] TLT divided by gold keeps falling on a dividend-adjusted basis because duration amplifies inflation devaluation over 20-30 years versus bills that reprice in months.

- [PREDICTS] Long yields stay elevated despite cuts as RMP bill-buying suppresses front rates only. -> check: 30Y holding above 4.5% with bills lower confirms it; 30Y back under 3.9% says duration bid returned.
