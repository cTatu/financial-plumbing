# Trump Sends Markets Crashing + More De-dollarization?

## 1. Thesis

Trump's threat of a large tariff hike after China pressed commodity exporters into restricting shipments to the US repriced trade war as a negative growth shock: the S&P fell about 1.5% with Nasdaq down 1.9% and speculative ARK down 3.5% while the VIX ripped and implied correlations jumped 26%. The bond market absorbed the shock differently than April's balance-of-payments scare, with Treasuries and most global sovereign yields falling as a haven bid instead of joint stock-bond-dollar selling. The dollar still sold off against the euro, yen, and peso on a risk-off day, while gold held a 0.5% gain and silver pressed toward the $50 psychological level. The de-dollarization leg rests on a longer arc — NY Fed custody holdings at a 13-year low and foreign share down from 35% to about one-quarter — which reflects both genuine reserve diversification into gold and custody shifts after the Russian seizure precedent rather than a single-day rupture.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] [01:14] Custody shifting away from the New York Fed reflects counterparty and confiscation fear after Russian reserves were seized because foreign holders still own Treasuries but avoid holding them in the US.
- [TAKE] [04:04] The 20-to-50-day VIX cross with S&P and VIX rising together warned that volatility risk was elevated because that joint move last reached extremes into Volmageddon.
- [TAKE] [06:25] Falling Treasury yields with a falling dollar and falling stocks differs from April because bonds absorbed the shock as a haven instead of joining a joint exit from dollar assets.
- [TAKE] [09:15] Global yields falling while Japan's 10-year edges higher isolates Japan because fiscal-dove repricing keeps JGB pressure even as worldwide duration catches a haven bid.
- [TAKE] [10:03] The single-day custody low looks less severe in context because the decade-long slide in foreign share and dollar reserve share already frames de-dollarization as a slow leak.
