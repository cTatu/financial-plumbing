# Fiscal Deficit Soars — Why YOU Will Be Buying a LOT More Treasury Debt to Fund the Government

## 1. Thesis

December printed the largest December deficit on record and a ~$600B first-quarter FY2026 shortfall (~$2.4T annualized), yet the real story is the collision of two deficits moving in opposite directions: the fiscal deficit stays near 6% of GDP at full employment while the trade deficit collapses toward $29B/month, which on balance-of-payments accounting mechanically shrinks the ~$1T/year of foreign capital that used to buy Treasuries. With Medicare growing 6.4x faster than GDP, interest at $1.2T/year (23 cents of every tax dollar), and a $10,000-per-worker funding gap worth 326 work hours, domestic households and firms must absorb the marginal bond — and the clearing price for that is a higher long-end yield even as the Fed is pushed toward cuts.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] A 6%-of-GDP deficit at full employment with no war or NBER recession is historically abnormal because deficits normally surge only when receipts collapse in recessions.
- [TAKE] Fiscal dominance is the forcing function for Fed cuts: unless Fed funds fall below ~3.3% and issuance shifts to sub-1-year bills, rolling debt refinances at ever-higher rates and debt service compounds.
- [TAKE] Fed cuts do not control the long end, so bill rates can follow the Fed down while 10-year and 30-year yields stay sticky or grind higher on fiscal, term-premium, and inflation fears.
- [TAKE] A shrinking trade deficit is, by accounting identity, shrinking foreign capital inflow into US financial assets, so households and firms at home must absorb more Treasury supply at lower prices and higher yields.
- [TAKE] The 326-hours-per-worker illustration is not a funding plan but proof the household sector cannot absorb $10,000 per worker per year, leaving yield as the adjustment valve.
