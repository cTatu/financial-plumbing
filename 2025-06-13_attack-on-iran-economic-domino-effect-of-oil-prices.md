# Attack on Iran - The Economic Domino Effect of Oil Prices

## 1. Thesis

Israel's overnight strike on Iran spiked crude about 14% to near $73 and broke oil out of its downtrend, and that single shock activates the full chain the series has built: oil lifts inflation within months, forces energy-short creditors Japan and the United Kingdom deeper into current-account deficits, compels those same top holders of Treasuries to slow buying or sell $40-300 billion chunks to buy dollars for fuel, and pushes the 10-year up 7bp and the 30-year up 8bp on the day — erasing two strong auctions — because with the SPR historically low and foreigners down to 25% of US debt, the bond market prices supply and inflation rather than seeking haven.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] Oil is the input to everything, so a rapid spike slows real growth while lifting CPI within a couple months toward stagflation.
- [TAKE] Energy-short creditors cannot run factories or trucks on Treasuries, so they sell their dollar savings to fund fuel and current-account deficits.
- [TAKE] The petrodollar still clears about 60% of oil in dollars, so an oil spike forces foreigners to buy dollars and pushes the dollar up against yen, euro, and won.
- [TAKE] Rising oil plus rising 10-year yield is not joint haven flow but duration selling, because haven demand would push yields down on geopolitical risk.
- [TAKE] The starting context is fragile with the SPR historically low since 2017 and long yields already above nominal growth, so incremental inflation or foreign selling tightens fast.
