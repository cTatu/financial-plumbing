# Credit Spreads are Flashing a Red Warning Light - What Are They? - Credit Spreads Explained

## 1. Thesis

Credit spreads measure the extra yield for bearing credit and liquidity risk, and Friday's sharp widening matters because spreads still sit near 3% at post-2007 tights where every prior turn wider marked equity drawdowns and pre-recession stress. The plumbing read is that mortgage spreads near 2.2 percentage points over the 10-year reflect MBB selling, quantitative tightening since late 2021, Treasury volatility, and 30-year-minus-2-year inversion shortening duration through prepayments. Widening normally tightens liquidity and lifts the dollar while bidding Treasuries lower in yield, but April's joint spread-widening with dollar selling broke the pattern, so one red day from tights signals watchlist risk rather than an imminent recession call.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] [00:43] A credit spread prices relative default and liquidity risk because lenders demand more yield from a zero-cash-flow startup than from Apple.
- [TAKE] [04:42] Quantitative tightening widens mortgage spreads mechanically because the Fed selling MBS pushes MBS prices down and mortgage yields up.
- [TAKE] [06:51] A flat or inverted 30-year-minus-2-year curve widens the mortgage spread because faster prepayments shorten duration toward short rates and demand extra premium.
- [TAKE] [08:50] Widening spreads normally tighten liquidity and lift the dollar because risk repricing drives haven demand for dollars and Treasuries.
- [TAKE] [10:49] Widening works through two legs at once because risky-bond yields rise on selling while Treasury yields fall on haven buying.
