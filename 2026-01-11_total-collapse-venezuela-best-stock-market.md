# Total Collapse: A Look at Venezuela - Really the Best Performing Stock Market IN THE WORLD?

## 1. Thesis

Venezuela is the limiting case of fiscal dominance funded by savings and the printing press: output fell from about $380B to about $111B, roughly a 70% collapse, while the budget deficit hit about 30% of GDP, foreign-exchange reserves fell from $43B to $5B, and gold reserves fell from 367 to 161 tons. Base money exploded until cumulative prices rose from an index of 100 in 2008 to about $29.1T today with the dollar up 6,750% against the bolivar in four years, so a $40K car costs $2.7M and the CPI peaked near 345,000% annually. The punchline is denominator illusion — the Caracas index is up 994% since August in bolivars and 230% in dollars yet down 6% in gold — which shows hyperinflation equities can nominally moon while still destroying purchasing power against the scarce bearer asset.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] Venezuelan data are thin and possibly unreliable, and multiple currency redenominations make level comparisons harder than in a single-currency system like the US.
- [TAKE] The budget gap was funded by depleting national savings because the trade surplus could not cover debt payments plus 30%-of-GDP spending.
- [TAKE] M0 outpacing M2 growth on the green M0-to-M2 line marks aggressive central-bank intervention rather than commercial-bank credit expansion.
- [TAKE] External-debt service means the current account, not the balance of trade, is the binding gauge because capital outflows for payments can offset a goods surplus.
- [TAKE] Hyperinflation equities moon only because the denominator collapses, so dollar or gold denomination is required before calling any return real.
