# Stealth Recession Exposed - 13 Charts Reveal a Hidden Economic Downturn

## 1. Thesis

Thirteen rate-of-change charts tell one post-recession story across labor, credit, output, and markets: JOLTS openings, separations, hires, and unemployment plus card charge-offs, M2, bank loans, ISM new orders and headline, sentiment, the 30-year-minus-2-year curve, construction spending, and the S&P all trace the same deceleration-then-turn signature seen coming out of 2001, 2008, and 2020. The implication is that a broad-based NBER-style downturn already ran its course under the surface around 2021-2022, leaving the economy in an ambiguous early-to-mid-cycle recovery where JOLTS leans early-cycle while the S&P leans mid-to-late-cycle.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] Rate-of-change of a moving average reveals the cycle because it smooths volatility and exposes the deceleration-to-recovery turn common to every NBER recession.
- [TAKE] Labor, credit, ISM, sentiment, curve, construction, and equities agree because broad-based weakness across unrelated metrics is the definition of recession.
- [TAKE] M2 going negative for the first time since the Great Depression marks this episode as a private-credit contraction rather than a normal inventory dip.
- [TAKE] Curve steepening since June 2023 fits the post-recession script because inversion flags the downturn and re-steepening tracks the early-cycle repair.
- [TAKE] Cycle timing stays ambiguous because JOLTS-based labor reads early-cycle while the S&P 12-month trend reads mid-to-late-cycle.
