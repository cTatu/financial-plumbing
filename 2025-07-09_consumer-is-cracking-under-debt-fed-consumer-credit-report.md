# Consumer is Cracking Under Debt - Fed's Latest Consumer Credit Report

## 1. Thesis

May G.19 shows revolving credit contracting 3.2% annualized — a rare move outside the GFC — because 500bp of hikes lifted floating card rates to 22% and doubled per-person interest to $3,470 per year. With private banks creating 70% of dollars and consumption at 65% of GDP, a sustained stall in the private-credit engine points toward disinflation tipping into terminal deflation. The Fed's $2.1 trillion of MBS shields termed-out homeowners while floating-rate borrowers absorb the shock, entrenching a generational split.

## 2. Key Claims

- [00:57] Revolving consumer credit contracted 3.2% on a monthly basis in the latest print. (as of May 2025 G.19)
- [10:31] Total consumer credit still grew only 1.2% annualized, far below 5.7% in 2021 and 7.7% in 2022. (as of May 2025)
- [11:41] Total consumer credit expanded only $61 billion annualized in 2025, far below the prior four years. (as of 2025 annualized)
- [12:39] Revolving balances fell $41 billion annualized while non-revolving balances rose $102 billion annualized. (as of May 2025)
- [13:17] Consumers hold about $5 trillion of debt, with $1.3 trillion in credit cards and $3.7 trillion non-revolving. (as of May 2025)
- [13:30] Commercial-bank rates average 7.7% for new cars, 22% for credit cards, and 11.5% for personal loans. (as of May 2025)
- [14:16] The average revolving rate rose from 13% after the GFC to 22% to 23% today, nearly doubling the cost. (as of Jul 2025)
- [14:48] Personal interest payments average $3,470 per person per year, doubling from $1,800 in January 2022. (as of Jul 2025)
- [15:39] The average worker labors 111 hours per year just to pay interest, matching pre-dot-com-bust and pre-GFC peaks. (as of Jul 2025)
- [18:18] Americans work 18 billion hours per year in aggregate just to service interest, not principal. (as of Jul 2025)

- [TAKE] [08:32] Five hundred basis points of hikes crushed releveled consumers because card balances had doubled while rates doubled from 13% to 22%.
- [TAKE] [16:18] The 111-hour interest burden sits at dot-com and GFC pre-recession peaks, marking a breaking-point threshold rather than normal cyclical strain.
