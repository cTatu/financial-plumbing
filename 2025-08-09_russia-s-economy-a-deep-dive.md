# Russia's Economy - A Deep Dive

## 1. Thesis

Russia funds its war from a position of low debt rather than strong growth: a 3%-of-GDP current-account surplus offsets a 1.7% fiscal deficit while debt sits at only 9.4% of GDP versus 125% in the US. The strain shows in prices instead of borrowing — 9.4% inflation, a 14.8% 10-year yield above 10.8% nominal growth, deeply positive real rates, and M2 doubling to 120 trillion rubles — with real growth rolling from 5.3% to 1.4% and the ruble down 63% since 2002. Markets now price relief: the curve uninverted in June-July 2025 in a bull steepening as short rates fell fastest, which the read ties to rising odds of an end to hostilities into the Trump summit.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] [02:22] Scaling by GDP matters because Russia's $20 billion quarterly surplus equals 3% of its economy while America's $450 billion quarterly deficit equals 6% of a far larger base.
- [TAKE] [11:05] Debt burden stays light at 9.4% debt-to-GDP even with the 10-year above nominal growth, which is why 14.8% yields pressure inflation rather than solvency.
- [TAKE] [16:55] Positive real yields across maturities show bondholders demanding compensation above 9.4% inflation instead of letting Russia inflate the debt away.
- [TAKE] [19:56] Sanctions failed to crush the currency because a trade-surplus exporter benefits from weakness the way Japan and China do, leaving the ruble only 6% below 2021.
- [TAKE] [18:47] The June-July 2025 un-inversion with short rates falling fastest is a bull steepening that prices lower inflation, lower policy rates, and higher odds of peace.
