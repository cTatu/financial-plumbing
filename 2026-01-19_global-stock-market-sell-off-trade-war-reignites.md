# Global Stock Market Sell-Off — Trade War Reignites and Rocks Markets

## 1. Thesis

A Greenland-triggered tariff escalation — a threatened 10% US duty on opposing EU states met by a reactivated €93B EU retaliation package — produced a textbook anti-haven session: S&P 500 futures gapped ~1% lower on MLK Day while the dollar fell instead of rallying and Treasuries sold off. The deeper signal is regime, not headline: European equities in dollar terms are up 22-39% since early 2025 and beating the S&P by 6-19.5pp, Japan's 30-year is melting up ~13bp in a day on fiscal fears, and gold, silver, and the franc are absorbing the bid that once went to dollars — consistent with a market starting to price reserve-status erosion rather than a one-weekend tariff scare.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] Tariffs should theoretically strengthen the dollar by shrinking the trade deficit and squeezing offshore dollar supply, so joint dollar, stock, and bond weakness is a regime break rather than normal risk-off.
- [TAKE] Europe's sustained dollar-terms outperformance since early 2025 reverses the post-2017 pattern where both European equities and the euro chronically underperformed the S&P and the dollar.
- [TAKE] Japan's long-end selloff alongside renewed yen weakness fits Plac—fiscal-driven bear steepening, but the absence of a Nikkei collapse in dollars argues against full emerging-market capital flight in Japan itself.
- [TAKE] The haven bid has migrated from dollars to gold, the Swiss franc, and to a lesser degree the euro and yen, which is why geopolitical escalation now coincides with dollar softness.
- [TAKE] Silver's outsized move is high-beta gold debasement exposure in a smaller market, not a growth signal, which is why copper's flat-to-soft tone alongside surging silver matters.
