# Is the Fed Secretly HIKING Rates? - What Fed Funds Indicates About SOFR Spreads and Liquidity

## 1. Thesis

The Federal funds rate drifting higher after the September cut is not a stealth hike but a market verdict on scarce dollars: the unsecured funds rate the Fed does not control has risen from 4.08% to 4.11% with its spread over the 4.00% reverse-repo floor widening from 3 to 11 basis points, mirroring the SOFR-over-RRP breakout since May-June 2025. The mechanism replays the 2018-2019 pre-crisis script where GSE lenders leave the funds market for repo, foreign banks withdraw as the borrow-lend spread compresses, and domestic banks bid more aggressively for a shrinking reserve pool — spillover from a secured market where $3 trillion funds nightly. With RRP-minus-SRF negative for the first time since 2020-2021 and SOFR still above interest on reserves, both the secured and unsecured legs now agree that the system has crossed from abundant to scarce liquidity, leaving balance-sheet expansion rather than rate cuts as the coherent fix.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] The Fed sets a 4.00%-to-4.25% corridor but does not control the funds print because the volume-weighted median clears where GSE supply meets bank demand.
- [TAKE] The fed-funds-over-RRP spread measures unsecured dollar scarcity against the floor because lenders demand more over the administered rate as reserves grow less abundant.
- [TAKE] GSE migration from fed funds to repo tightens both markets because it removes unsecured supply while adding secured demand, forcing domestic banks to bid higher for the remainder.
- [TAKE] SOFR and fed funds co-movement signals system-wide cash shortage rather than market-specific friction because secured and unsecured overnight dollars draw on the same reserve pool.
- [TAKE] Rate cuts cannot relieve this strain because the binding constraint is reserve quantity on the balance sheet, so slowing or ending QT with reserve creation is the historically effective tool.
