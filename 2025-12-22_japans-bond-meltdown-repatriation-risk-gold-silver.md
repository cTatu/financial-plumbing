# Japan's Bond Market Meltdown - What is it Signaling? Why are Gold & Silver Soaring? Dollar Meltdown

## 1. Thesis

Japan's bond selloff is normalization, not insolvency: the belly of the curve (2-year through 10-year) is repricing a telegraphed climb toward a ~2% terminal rate while the long end stays contained, which is the opposite of a fiscal-crisis blowout. The real US risk is not a surprise yen-carry unwind but slow repatriation — Japanese capital that bought Treasuries and dollars only because JGBs yielded nothing now has a reason to sell both and go home, dragging global yields higher. Metals screaming to new highs with monthly RSIs above 90 while real yields sit positive is the market's verdict: more participants are selling dollars for gold than the reverse, front-running the negative-real-rate regime Washington's run-hot policy makes mathematically likely.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] [02:28] A telegraphed BoJ climb lets bond and currency markets price the path, so this episode lacks the surprise repricing that made last summer's sudden yen spike violent.
- [TAKE] [05:25] Co-rising JGB and Treasury yields are the repatriation signature because a wealthy Japanese holder sells Treasuries, then sells dollars for yen to buy JGBs.
- [TAKE] [07:53] A belly-led selloff with a flattening 30-year minus 10-year spread signals rate normalization rather than the long-end blowout a solvency or hyperinflation scare would produce.
- [TAKE] [11:17] Gold's surge against positive 1.47% real yields breaks a 50-to-60-year correlation, which means markets are pricing future negative real rates required by US fiscal math.
- [TAKE] [11:56] Homes are depreciating assets, so record dollar home prices reflect dollar purchasing-power loss rather than houses becoming more valuable.
