# Jobs Report - Current State of the US Labor Market

## 1. Thesis

The May payroll beat keeps the headline story in a cooling-but-still-positive labor market, yet the composition tells the weaker tale: government hiring has stalled, hours worked are falling, youth and broad U6 unemployment are rising from 2022 lows, and job growth concentrates in low-wage services while manufacturing and mining drift down — so nominal 3.9% wage gains look like real gains only if official 2.3% CPI is believed, while gold, S&P, M2, and top-1% wealth denominators show the average hour buying relentlessly less.

- [01:50] Average hourly earnings grew 3.9% year over year against a 3.7% expectation. (as of May 2025 BLS)

- [14:47] Average hourly earnings grow 3.9% year over year while CPI runs 2.3% year over year, printing as official real wage gains. (as of May 2025 BLS/CPI)

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] A 139,000 headline beat does not reverse the five-year cooling trend that Powell frames as normalization from stretched post-2020 tightness.
- [TAKE] Falling government payrolls matter because the public sector grew almost without interruption, so sustained shrinkage would break the cycle pattern.
- [TAKE] Rising headline, youth, and U6 unemployment from 2022 lows fits the historical sequence where bottoms that turn higher usually go nonlinear into recession.
- [TAKE] Shorter hours and part-time-for-economic-reasons mean added jobs overstate worker strength because fewer paid hours buy less even at higher hourly rates.
- [TAKE] Wage gains concentrated in finance, professional services, and information while leisure, retail, and hospitality add low-wage volume explain why averages rise as median purchasing power versus gold, S&P, M2, and top-1% wealth keeps falling.

## 4C Predicts

- [PREDICTS] Unemployment keeps drifting higher from the summer-2022 bottom and turns nonlinear if a recession arrives. -> check: headline, U6, and youth rates over next 3-6 BLS prints; sustained decline back to lows refutes it.
- [PREDICTS] Government employment rolls over and drags aggregate payrolls as federal cuts spread to state and local hiring. -> check: total and federal employment in next BLS reports; renewed government gains refute it.
- [PREDICTS] Construction employment rolls over before the broader cycle as it did into 2008. -> check: construction payrolls and housing starts over next two quarters; renewed construction expansion refutes it.
