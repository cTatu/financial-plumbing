# Cayman Island Basis Trade Chaos Pt 2: What's the Basis Trade & How is it Already Under Stress?

## 1. Thesis

Part two turns the Cayman undercount into mechanics: the basis is a 0.1% futures-over-cash premium harvested by going long cash Treasuries and short futures into quarterly convergence, made lucrative only by 50-to-1 to 100-to-1 overnight repo leverage. That structure splits risk into convergence profit versus daily carry cost, so a 30-basis-point rise in SOFR wipes out the 10-basis-point edge many times over — the worked example turns a $100-per-year gain on $1,000 of equity into a $200 net loss, or a 20% drawdown before mark-to-market widening. With SOFR already up 18 basis points in about two weeks for a 13% hit, printing above the 4.25% discount-window ceiling, and the RRP-minus-SRF spread negative in a scarce-liquidity regime, the trade is stressed but not yet in forced unwind — and the cited Brookings remedy is for the Fed to warehouse the $1.1 trillion to $1.2 trillion position itself by buying cash bonds while shorting futures.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] The basis exists because cheapest-to-deliver optionality plus futures preference from mutual funds, insurers, and pensions for liquidity and leverage accounting keeps futures slightly rich to cash.
- [TAKE] Convergence into quarterly expiration is the profit engine because the 0.1% futures premium decays to the cash price for a short-futures, long-cash holder.
- [TAKE] Daily repo refinancing is the fragility because the entire 100-to-1 notional must be rolled every night at whatever SOFR prints.
- [TAKE] Funding stress hits both legs at once because higher SOFR raises carry cost while Dallas Fed evidence shows the basis itself widens 5 to 10 basis points wrong-way.
- [TAKE] Regional-bank deposit outflow and collateral-quality fears can tighten repo further because money-fund lenders demand higher SOFR when counterparty confidence erodes.
