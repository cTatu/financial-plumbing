# Recession = Emerging Market-like Debt Crisis

## 1. Thesis

A garden-variety recession turns a 7%-of-GDP procyclical deficit into a sovereign funding crisis: revenue falls about 22% while spending rises about 27% and GDP shrinks about 4%, producing roughly $3.9T of receipts against $9.1T of outlays for a $5.2T gap near 17.9% of GDP. Because each new dollar of debt already buys only about $0.37 of GDP and $30T of equity wealth would evaporate in a typical 46% drawdown, surging supply would meet falling absorption capacity — the emerging-market pattern where yields rise and the currency falls into weakness, forcing Fed monetization on a scale beyond 2020.

- [04:09] Tax revenue falls about 22% in modern recessions, taking $5.0 trillion of receipts down to about $3.9 trillion in a garden-variety downturn. (as of Sep 2025 historical average)
- [04:40] The S&P 500 averages a 46% drawdown across the past three recessions, cutting about $65 trillion of market cap to $35 trillion for $30 trillion of destroyed wealth. (as of Sep 2025 historical average)
- [07:26] Spending rises 27% from $7.1 trillion to $9.1 trillion while revenue falls to $3.9 trillion, producing a $5.2 trillion deficit versus about $2.0 trillion today. (as of Sep 2025 model)
- [08:31] GDP falls about 4% from $30.3 trillion to $29.0 trillion, so the $5.2 trillion gap equals a 17.9% of GDP deficit, or 15% on NBER timing and 17% peak-to-trough. (as of Sep 2025 model)
- [11:41] Each $1.00 of new debt yields $0.82 of GDP, only $0.37 over the past 25 years, so $5.2 trillion of issuance returns about $1.9 trillion of GDP at a roughly $0.30 marginal return. (as of Sep 2025 history)
- [15:21] Each 1 percentage point rise in yields adds $370 billion of annual interest to the $5.2 trillion deficit, and an Italy-style 500 basis point surge would push the gap toward $7.0 trillion and over 20% of GDP. (as of Sep 2025 model)

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] [01:14] A 7% deficit at full employment is procyclical fragility because the next recession must fund the 800-to-1000bp historical widening from already-exhausted capacity.
- [TAKE] [03:11] Mandatory-plus-interest dominance removes adjustment because 79% of receipts pre-committed to Social Security, Medicare, and interest leaves only deficits to fund everything else.
- [TAKE] [11:41] Debt productivity collapse confirms over-borrowing because $0.37 of GDP per $1 of debt means each new issue buys less growth through crowding out.
- [TAKE] [08:31] GDP is the absorption constraint because the deficit numerator surges while the output denominator shrinks, so supply must clear into falling demand and yields must rise.
- [TAKE] [14:26] Early April previewed the regime because stocks, the dollar, and bonds sold jointly with the dollar down over 1% for three straight days, which is balance-of-payments behavior.
