# Powell Admits: Repo Market Cracks - Is QE Coming?

## 1. Thesis

Powell's admission that repo rates are firming with money-market strains echoing 2019 confirms the funding thesis: quantitative tightening plus a ~7%-of-GDP deficit, a ~$0.5 trillion TGA rebuild, and an empty $3.5 billion reverse-repo buffer have left the $3 trillion daily repo market with no shock absorber. Because rate cuts cannot fix a reserve shortage, the end of QT and a return to reserve-management buying — QE in plumbing if not in name — is the only tool that worked in September 2019, and back then gold topped about 18 months before QE stopped while equities and long bonds repriced with the balance sheet.

## 2. Key Claims

- [00:29] The Fed balance sheet stands at $6.59 trillion with QT destroying bank reserves. (as of Oct 2025)
- [01:24] The federal deficit runs about 7% of GDP, worsening by 9 percentage points since 2000 at similar economic conditions. (as of 2025)
- [02:27] The Treasury rebuilt the TGA checking account by about $0.5 trillion, removing that cash from the system. (as of 2025)
- [02:47] The reverse-repo facility sits at $3.5 billion, leaving the excess-dollar storage tank effectively empty. (as of Oct 2025)
- [03:18] About $3 trillion is financed each day in the overnight repo market. (as of Oct 2025)

- [12:15] It takes about 200 hours of average work to buy one unit of the S&P 500 versus 15-20 hours in the parents' generation. (as of 2025)

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] QT plus deficits plus TGA plus empty RRP all drain dollar liquidity because every new Treasury must be bought with dollars while QT destroys reserves.
- [TAKE] The SOFR-minus-RRP spread measures free-market dollar scarcity against the Fed's floor because repo cost above the administered rate means dollars command a premium.
- [TAKE] Interest-rate cuts cannot fix repo stress because overnight reserve shortage is a balance-sheet quantity problem, so ending QT is the coherent response.
- [TAKE] QE shows little CPI link but lifts asset prices because reserves buy bonds from banks while the top 1% holds most financial assets.
- [TAKE] The Fed avoids selling long bonds under QT because 10-year, 20-year, and 30-year demand is already weak and added supply would press yields.
