# Repo Market Cracks - Fed's Emergency Credit Line Soars - Liquidity Crisis?

## 1. Thesis

What looked like quarter-end noise now reads as a scarce-reserves regime change: the Standing Repo Facility drew tens of billions across three straight sessions while both median SOFR and the 75th percentile sat over the Fed's 4.00% ceiling, with the RRP-minus-SRF gauge negative for the first time since mid-2020. With the reverse-repo buffer empty and a $300B-to-$900B TGA rebuild funded out of bank reserves, the presenter argues the Fed is losing its grip on the overnight price of dollars in the $3.20T secured market — and that persistent over-ceiling funding, not calendar effects, is the signal.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] Three straight multi-billion SRF draws prove scarcity because an emergency backstop unused since 2020 does not get tapped for calendar window-dressing.
- [TAKE] An empty reverse repo removes the shock absorber, so the $600.00 billion TGA rebuild had to drain straight out of bank reserves and the monetary base.
- [TAKE] Persistent 75th-percentile prints over the ceiling matter more than median because $800.00 billion funding above the backstop rate shows stress is broad, not a thin tail.
- [TAKE] Rising Fed funds alongside SOFR confirms the squeeze spans secured and unsecured dollars rather than a repo-collateral quirk.
- [TAKE] Stigma and collateral haircuts explain why firms pay over 4.00% in the market instead of using the facility, which keeps the ceiling from clearing stress.
