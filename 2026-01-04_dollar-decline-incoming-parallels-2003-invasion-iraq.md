# Dollar Decline Incoming? - Parallels to 2003 Invasion of Iraq

## 1. Thesis

The macro rhyme with March 2003 is unusually tight — Fed cutting, 3% inflation, ~2% real GDP, a dollar already falling, an oil-rich authoritarian target (Venezuela's ~300B barrels and 1.1M bpd vs Iraq's 112B and 2.5M bpd) seized amid an oil glut — except fiscal strain is worse (5.5% of GDP vs 2-2.5%) and the curve is bear-steepening instead of bull-steepening. Post-2003 history says the consensus "more supply means lower oil" was wrong: oil dipped from ~$37 to ~$25 then ran toward $115-145, the dollar fell ~25%, and Chevron (+220%), Halliburton (+30%), the S&P 500 (doubling), and gold all ran. With the dollar now sliding from ~105 to ~98.5 even as US yields pay more than Europe and Japan, plus TIC showing foreigners halting UST buying after the Russian FX-reserve seizure, the Venezuela action reads as dollar-negative and bullish for oil, energy equity, and gold.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] Bear-steepening today inverts the 2003 signal because long-end yields rise while the Fed cuts, so easing no longer loosens real-economy borrowing tied to the 10-year.
- [TAKE] The dollar keeps falling against the euro even as US paper pays more than European and Japanese paper, which breaks the carry logic that capital flows to the highest yield.
- [TAKE] Seizing Russian FX reserves and defaulting on Treasuries owed to Russia told surplus countries that USTs are conditional collateral, and the Venezuela seizure extends that de-dollarization signal.
- [TAKE] Invading an oil-rich producer amid a glut did not lower oil after 2003 because above-ground risk, spending, and dollar debasement dominated the supply arithmetic.
