# The US Economy is a Pressure Cooker ...and the Pressure is Building

## 1. Thesis

The Q3 BLS productivity release crystallizes a decades-long split where output per hour compounds to owners while hourly pay stagnates: productivity rose 5.0% annualized against 2.9% nominal and negative 0.2% real wage growth, extending a post-1987 pattern where market cap outran GDP by 323%, debt by 160%, and profits by 200% while total labor share fell 23% and per-capita share fell 47%. Ownership explains the wedge because the top 1% hold about $20T of equities against barely $1T for the bottom 40% and 93% sits with the top 10%, so the same productivity that lifts margins lifts top-1% net worth from 80% to 167% of GDP while the bottom half stays near 14%. Record AI capex near 2% of GDP, larger than all postwar megaprojects combined, is explicitly aimed at replacing humans, which means further productivity gains will widen the hours-to-buy-a-share and populist gap until participation and pay break.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] Productivity gains accrue to corporations and equity owners rather than wage earners because equity ownership is concentrated in the top 10% of earners.
- [TAKE] Per-capita wage share matters more than total wage share because labor-force expansion dilutes the average worker even when aggregate wages look stable.
- [TAKE] AI capital spending aims to raise output from the same one human and one hour, so further efficiency gains will widen the productivity-wage split by design.
- [TAKE] GDP growth headlines mislead workers because 5% output growth means little when the average wage rises only 1% and asset access decides who compounds.
- [TAKE] Immigration-led labor-supply growth explains part of the per-capita dilution because more workers share output while equity stays narrowly held.
