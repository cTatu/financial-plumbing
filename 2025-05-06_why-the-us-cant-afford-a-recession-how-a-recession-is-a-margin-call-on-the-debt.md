# Why the US Can't Afford a Recession - How a Recession is a Margin Call on the Debt

## 1. Thesis

He made this video to show why even a garden-variety recession would be a fiscal catastrophe from a 7%-of-GDP starting deficit: spending historically rises about 11% while receipts fall about 28%, so a $2T deficit on $27T GDP becomes roughly a $4T deficit on $25T GDP, or nearly 18% of GDP. Because debt (+8,500%) and interest (+5,500%) have outrun GDP (+2,300%) since 1971 with ever-lower productivity per borrowed dollar, that widening forces soaring supply into a shrinking balance-sheet pie, higher yields, higher interest, and eventual QE-infinity and double-digit inflation.

- [21:10] The recession math implies about a $4 trillion deficit on about $25 trillion GDP, or nearly 18% of GDP. (as of May 2025 scenario)

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] [04:07] Government spending rises in recessions through automatic stabilizers, unemployment benefits, and stimulus, so the expenditure leg reliably widens the deficit.
- [TAKE] [07:27] Government revenue collapses in recessions because the taxable base shrinks and hyperfinancialized capital-gains receipts fall with the S&P 500.
- [TAKE] [13:03] Each borrowed dollar now buys less GDP as debt-to-GDP climbs, with Reinhart-Rogoff and Swedish government-share research both pointing to a 0.1% GDP drag per point of state expansion.
- [TAKE] [23:20] A 20%-of-GDP deficit means shoving more bond supply into a shrinking GDP pie while demand falls, which is the mechanical case for secularly higher yields, not lower ones.
- [TAKE] [27:15] Higher yields raise interest that is itself deficit-financed, so a 17%-of-GDP gap can spiral very quickly toward 25% through this vicious feedback loop.
