# TACO Is Back — Major Volatility Returns & US Sells $230 BILLION of US Debt Amidst EU Trade War

## 1. Thesis

The Greenland tariff shock was a TACO headline round-trip — S&P futures gapped, then ripped vertical on a "framework of a future deal" before fading on Danish pushback to close up about 1.2% — but the funding test underneath passed: the Treasury sold roughly $230B in two days, $216B of it bills plus a $13B 20-year that stopped through 1bp with 64% indirects. Japan explains why the week still matters more than the headline: a 35-45bp two-day long-end blowout on populist fiscal talk (sales-tax cut plus snap election) that then snapped back shows a thin, illiquid JGB market where repatriation and carry-unwind, not Tokyo solvency, is the channel into US duration — while gold refusing to sell off keeps signaling the reserve-currency transition underneath the volatility.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] Japan is not an imminent implosion because a large current-account surplus, 50% BoJ ownership, 12% external ownership, and short-dated issuance insulate Tokyo funding even when duration gaps 35-45bp.
- [TAKE] The long end is a bond-vigilante referendum on populist fiscal-monetary ease: dovish policy with 3% inflation and nascent wage growth demands higher term compensation, unlike the deflation era when it was benign.
- [TAKE] Bill demand is cash-market demand for interest-bearing dollars with minimal duration risk, while duration auctions are the marginal test of fiscal credibility, which is why $216B of bills plus a tiny $13B 20-year can both clear yet prove little about the long end.
- [TAKE] Gold ripping while refusing to sell off through TACO volatility is the primary signal that the shrinking trade deficit is being read as reserve-status erosion rather than a one-off tariff scare.
- [TAKE] TACO headline risk defines 2026 sequencing: tariff threats gap markets, framework-of-a-deal headlines squeeze them back, and Danish-style pushback leaves an ugly daily wick with trade tensions unresolved.
