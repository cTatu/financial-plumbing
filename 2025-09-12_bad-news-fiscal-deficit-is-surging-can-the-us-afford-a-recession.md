# Bad News: Fiscal Deficit is Surging - Can the US Afford a Recession?

## 1. Thesis

August's $345 billion monthly deficit on $690 billion of spending leaves the 12-month deficit at 7% of GDP with no recession or war to explain it, and the structure keeps deteriorating: outlays run 22% of GDP near WWII levels, $93 billion of monthly interest annualizes past $1.1 trillion, and Social Security plus Medicare plus interest already absorb 79% of receipts before Medicaid. Because recessions widen the deficit about 800 basis points while shrinking GDP, today's procyclical gap implies a 15% deficit in the next downturn — the emerging-market dynamic where falling capacity meets surging supply and yields rise into weakness, already previewed in April's tariff-growth shock.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] [01:20] A 7% deficit at 4.3% unemployment is procyclical fragility because the next 800bp recession widening must fund 15% of GDP in new debt into falling output.
- [TAKE] [15:39] Mandatory plus interest dominance removes discretion because 79% of receipts pre-committed to Social Security, Medicare, and interest leaves cuts symbolic without reform.
- [TAKE] [12:20] Interest compounds the spiral because $3B per day is funded with new issuance, so old-debt cost becomes new-debt supply.
- [TAKE] [07:05] Tariffs cannot close the gap because $350B annualized barely dents $7T-plus spending even before court reversal risk.
- [TAKE] [18:24] Debt productivity collapse confirms over-borrowing because $0.40 GDP per $1 debt with falling velocity means each issue buys less growth.
