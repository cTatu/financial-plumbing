# Negative Real Rates - The Solution to US Debt Crisis?

## 1. Thesis

The United States can only escape 122% debt-to-GDP the way it did after World War II: by capping Treasury yields below inflation and nominal growth so the denominator outgrows the numerator, which means today's breakout to positive real yields must reverse into yield-curve control, QE, and financial repression — a regime where scarce inelastic assets like gold and Bitcoin preserve purchasing power while stocks and housing rise nominally but fall in real terms.

## 2. Key Claims

- [01:29] The deficit was $880 billion heading into 2020 and recovered only to a $1.4 trillion deficit afterward, with each crisis settling at a worse level. (as of 2020-2025)
- [02:21] The deficit runs about $2 trillion, or 6% of GDP, at full employment with no war, a level previously seen only in World War II, 2008, and 2020. (as of Jun 2025)
- [03:07] The current tax bill would push the deficit to about 7% to 7.5% of GDP. (as of Jun 2025)
- [03:34] Debt to GDP stands at about 122%, above the World War II peak. (as of Jun 2025)
- [07:51] Federal interest payments run about 4% of GDP, the highest in data reaching back to the 1800s. (as of Jun 2025)
- [11:03] From 1941 to about 1950 the 10-year yield was capped at 2.5% while inflation ran 12% to 20%, producing deeply negative real yields. (as of 1941-1950 episode)
- [15:35] Real yields broke above zero after about six negative years, exiting a roughly 45-year downtrend dating to the 1980 peaks near 14% CPI and 15% 10-year. (as of Jun 2025)
- [17:09] Nominal growth has fallen for four straight years while the 10-year yield rises, leaving the cost of debt above nominal growth. (as of Jun 2025)
- [22:23] The real 10-year return rose from negative 6% in 2022 to positive 2% today, an 8-point move, while gold rose from $1,700 to $3,400. (as of 2022-2025)
- [26:13] About 40% of all dollars ever created were created from 2021 to 2023, and the average single-family home price rose 40%. (as of 2021-2023 Fed M2)

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] Debt to GDP falls only when nominal growth exceeds the Treasury yield, so capping yields below growth mechanically delevers the state.
- [TAKE] Positive real yields raise the debt-service burden while negative real yields quietly transfer purchasing power from bondholders to the Treasury.
- [TAKE] The 45-year fall in real yields subsidized ever-larger deficits, which is why the feared 1970s-style debt crisis kept not arriving.
- [TAKE] Gold rising alongside real yields shows the market pricing eventual yield caps, money printing, and currency debasement to resolve the debt.
- [TAKE] Under repression, scarce inelastic assets preserve purchasing power while nominal stocks and housing go sideways to down in gold or Bitcoin terms.

- [PREDICTS] The SP500 and housing rise nominally but fall in gold and Bitcoin terms as in the 1970s episode. -> check: SPX/gold and home-price/gold down over 12-24 months; new highs in gold terms refute it.
