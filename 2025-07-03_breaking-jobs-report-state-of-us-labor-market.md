# Breaking: Jobs Report - State of the US Labor Market

## 1. Thesis

The June 147,000 payroll beat and steady 4.2% unemployment sustain the cooling-not-cracking narrative markets trade, but the internals — 62.4% participation last seen in the 1970s, 7.8% U6, 1.96 million continuing claims accelerating from 1.85 million in March, 34.2-hour weeks, 4.5 million involuntary part-time plus 6 million labor-force wanters plus 1.8 million marginally attached, and hiring led by government, health care, and social assistance — show a two-tier market where Microsoft can cut 2,300 Washington workers including 817 engineers while seeking 6,000 H-1Bs, and where the trade deficit narrowing from $138 billion to $71 billion per month still needs quarters to prove tariffs are reordering anything.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] [01:20] Markets reward the beat-versus-expectation gap more than the absolute 147,000 level, which remains soft against history back to the 1960s.
- [TAKE] [03:05] U6 near double the headline rate plus 12.00 million across involuntary part-time, want-a-job, and marginally attached workers captures labor slack the 4.20% rate hides.
- [TAKE] [07:12] Continuing claims bottoming in 2022 and accelerating higher fit the pre-recession pattern where bottoms that turn rarely stabilize without going nonlinear.
- [TAKE] [10:48] Hiring led by government, health care, and social assistance reads as weakness because strong private markets do not need 19,000 more social-assistance workers.
- [TAKE] [21:24] The trade deficit, the dollar index, and wages versus GDP form the objective scoreboard for reindustrialization, while one month of China narrowing proves little.
