# Horrible 7yr Bond Auction - Weakest Foreign Demand in 4+ Years

## 1. Thesis

The $44 billion 7-year auction tailed 0.6bp with a 2.4 bid-to-cover and 56.4% indirects — the weakest foreign bid since February 2021 — because a hot morning (Q2 GDP at 3.8%, Q3 tracking 3.25%, strong claims, durables, and trade) repriced the Fed path only a week after a 25bp cut. Yields rose across the curve in a bear flattener led by the 2-year (3.59% to 3.66%), the dollar firmed, and the S&P fell for a third day as the easy-money bid deflated — with the foreign-demand fade matching the recent TIC warning that the marginal Treasury buyer is stepping back.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] [04:08] Hot GDP, claims, durables, and trade prints forced the bond market to admit yields had fallen too far on misplaced growth fear.
- [TAKE] [06:47] Stronger growth means fewer Fed cuts, so the short end reprices fastest and the curve bear-flattens with 2-year-led selling.
- [TAKE] [08:15] Stocks fall on strong data because the rally is priced on cheaper capital, and fewer expected cuts remove that fuel.
- [TAKE] [10:20] The 56.4% indirect print extends the TIC theme that foreign demand for US debt is waning at the margin.
- [TAKE] [05:40] Yields rising the week after a Fed cut show the long end tracks nominal-growth expectations, not the policy rate alone.
