# Has the Fed Lost Control? - Inflation Roars Back to 3%

## 1. Thesis

September CPI coming in cool versus expectations changes nothing about the trend: headline and core sit at 3.0% with core PCE at 2.9%, up 70bp in 5 months for the fastest 6-month pace since June 2022, led by services and a re-accelerating 4.1% monthly gasoline plus 1.5% energy turn. With M2 growing 5.0% against 5.0% nominal GDP while debt grows 6.1% and the deficit 10-11%, the presenter frames 3% into a 96%-priced October cut as fiscal dominance — run nominal hot with bill issuance and sub-inflation short rates to engineer negative real yields as in WWII — where ISM services prices vertical over 2 quarters warn the next CPI leg is higher and the release valve is all-time-high stocks, homes, and gold.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] A cooler-than-expected 3% print is still re-acceleration because the 6-month rate of change runs the hottest since June 2022.
- [TAKE] Consumers experience cumulative price level gains rather than rates of change, so 3% on top of prior years keeps pressure rising even before acceleration.
- [TAKE] Cutting into 3% inflation with stocks, homes, and gold at highs admits fiscal dominance because debt at 6.1% and deficits at 10-11% outrun 5% nominal GDP.
- [TAKE] Negative real yields are the stated escape because holding bill and bond rates below inflation plus nominal growth shrinks debt-to-GDP as after WWII.
- [TAKE] Bills fund the strategy twice over because short-dated issuance is more money-like and more leverable while cuts push bill rates below inflation.
- [TAKE] Vertical ISM services prices foreshadow higher CPI three months forward because services dominate the index and manufacturing prices confirm the push.
