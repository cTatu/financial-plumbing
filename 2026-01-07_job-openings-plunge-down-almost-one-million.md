# Job Openings PLUNGE - Down Almost ONE MILLION Since Last Year

## 1. Thesis

November JOLTS plus December ADP show a labor market that is weaker than headlines but frozen rather than collapsing: openings fell to 7.1M, down 885K year over year and 500K below the 7.6M consensus in a three-sigma miss, dropping back below the unemployed count to negative 685K. Hiring stays pinned near 5M while layoffs stay historically low, so the market is low-hire, low-fire with the worst pain in professional and business services where AI substitution fits. The offsets are narrow but real — small and mid-size firms re-accelerating versus large-firm weakness, quits up 208K with layoffs down 107K in accommodation and food services, job-changer wage growth re-bouncing to 6.6% versus 4.4% for stayers, and ISM services employment back above 50 — which is why the market is starting to price a broadening from Mag-7 into equal-weight and small-cap rather than outright recession.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] The market is in a low-hire, low-fire freeze where hires sit near 5M without bouncing while layoffs stay very low versus history, so stagnation rather than mass firing is the binding constraint.
- [TAKE] Openings falling back below unemployment is normalization because openings above unemployment was the historical anomaly, as the 2004 example of 8.4M unemployed versus 3.7M openings shows.
- [TAKE] Government openings keep trending down from 2022-2023 levels, which means prior payroll strength flattered by government hiring is reversing under DOGE effects.
- [TAKE] Weakness concentrated in professional and business services fits AI displacement of white-collar spreadsheet and cubicle work, a pattern repeating for many months.
- [TAKE] The ISM services bounce matters because services are about 90% of the economy and soft PMI data can lead hard payroll data by a couple of months if the move above 50.0 holds.
