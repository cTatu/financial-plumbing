# Another $38 Billion of Debt Just Auctioned Off - With France/UK Selling, Who's Buying?

## 1. Thesis

The $39B 10-year at 4.17% — highest since August — cleared on the screws with 2.55 cover above the 2.51 average, only 8.8% dealer takedown, and 70.2% indirects, a B-minus-solid result that answers who buys when France and the UK sell: price-insensitive demand still absorbs duration even with yields ripping to 3-month highs. The auction is the calm inside a global long-end storm — Germany, France, the UK, Japan, and the US all pushing higher — while 10-year breakevens trend up against falling WTI, ISM services prices telegraph hotter CPI two months out, and 10Y-minus-funds sits at 11-month wides in a Hassett-led bear steepener. Suppression levers from activist issuance to SLR relief can still cap the long end, but the direction is fiscal-dominance steepening.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] Size clears price because the auction, not the off-the-run screen, is where big capital actually trades tens of billions and sets the true yield.
- [TAKE] Foreign selling does not equal failed auctions because 70.2% indirects with below-average dealer takedown shows other price-insensitive buyers step in at 4.17%.
- [TAKE] Breakevens rising against falling oil is a warning because the usual tight correlation breaking means bond-implied inflation is pricing fiscal risk rather than energy.
- [TAKE] Services PMI is the CPI telescope because a near-one-to-one pass-through two months later with 6-9 months of hot prints points to margin compression ending.
- [TAKE] Global long ends move as one because twin-deficit interest doom loops — higher rates into higher deficits into more supply — repeat across the US, UK, France, and Japan ex-China.
