# Japanese Bond Yields Surge - Bond Market Implosion?

## 1. Thesis

Japan's record 40-year yield near 3.57% is framed as normalization rather than implosion: after lost decades of deflation and stagnant real GDP, wages, nominal growth, and inflation are finally moving together, so higher yields are the expected price of escaping 0% YCC and negative rates. The binding constraint is balance-sheet, not growth — 236% debt-to-GDP plus a $5T BoJ balance sheet means every further rise crushes bank, pension, and insurer collateral the way 0.4% Treasuries crushed US regionals in 2023. Japan therefore faces a choice between letting yields clear (with yen strength and repatriation of its $24B/month surplus savings doing the buying) or returning to QE/YCC and letting the yen take the adjustment, and the currency plus rate differentials so far say orderly: yen up over eight days and since January 2025, not the yields-up-plus-currency-down of a balance-of-payments break.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] [01:52] Bond yields should approximate nominal growth, so rising JGB yields alongside wage and economic growth indicate normalization rather than automatic catastrophe.
- [TAKE] [23:55] Japan runs no twin deficit while America does, so direct US-Japan debt-to-GDP comparisons without balance-of-payments context mislead.
- [TAKE] [36:49] Yen strength over the past eight days shows higher JGB yields are attracting global capital instead of triggering disorderly flight.
- [TAKE] [41:23] Japan must either let yields keep rising and impair financial-institution collateral or resume QE and yield curve control and let the yen absorb the adjustment.
- [TAKE] [40:06] The dangerous combination is bond yields rising while the currency falls, which described America in early April but does not describe Japan over the past week.

## 7. Follow-ups

- Watch USD/JPY with 30-year and 40-year JGB yields: renewed yen weakness to new lows while long JGB yields make new highs confirms disorderly stress, while continued yen firmness with yields elevated confirms repatriation-demand absorption.
- Watch Japanese wage growth near 2.1% with inflation near 4.5% and nominal GDP: wages holding above prior deflation ranges with positive nominal growth confirms normalization, while wages rolling over with inflation still rising says stagflationary squeeze on collateral holders.
- Watch BoJ balance sheet with policy language on QE and yield curve control: renewed balance-sheet expansion with yield caps confirms the yen-release-valve path, while continued runoff with higher yields confirms tolerance for collateral impairment and possible bank or pension failures.
