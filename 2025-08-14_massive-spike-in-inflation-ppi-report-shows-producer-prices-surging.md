# Massive Spike in Inflation: PPI Report Shows Producer Prices Surging

## 1. Thesis

July PPI surged 0.9% month over month versus 0.2% expected with 3.3% headline and 3.7% core year over year, the hottest since summer 2022, yet the composition repeats the CPI story: services drove it via a 1.1% final-demand services jump led by a 2.0% widening in trade margins, not broad goods inflation. The PPI-core-CPI spread of about 0.8 points and the history of 3-5 point swings plus April-2023's 6.6-point PPI-over-core gap mean producers likely absorb the shock because tapped-out consumers meet pass-through with demand destruction. Yields repriced higher on the print while September cut odds stayed firm, so the market treats it as a one-off margin event rather than a new goods-inflation regime.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] [02:50] PPI, CPI, and PCE are three separate inflation gauges so a producer surge does not map one-to-one onto what households feel.
- [TAKE] [11:01] PPI behaves like a volatile penny stock versus core CPI because 3-5 point spreads are normal, as April 2023's 6.6-point gap shows.
- [TAKE] [10:47] Tariff-driven goods inflation is still missing because goods supply only ~20bp of core CPI with services supplying the rest.
- [TAKE] [19:15] Retailers cannot pass margin compression to tapped-out consumers without triggering demand destruction, so producers and importers absorb it.
- [TAKE] [14:56] Hyperfinancialization concentrates the PPI spike in portfolio management and deposit services, the Dutch-disease signature of reserve-currency finance.

## 7. Follow-ups

- Watch August PPI with trade margins and machinery wholesaling: services above 1.0% again with margins above 2.0% confirms persistence, while margins reverting toward 0% says July was one-off.
- Watch CPI goods vs PPI goods with retail sales: PPI goods elevated with CPI goods muted confirms absorption, while CPI goods jumping with sales falling confirms pass-through plus demand destruction.
- Watch 2Y/10Y/30Y with September cut odds: yields fading with firm cut odds confirms look-through, while 10Y above 4.40% with odds collapsing says the bond market repriced inflation.
