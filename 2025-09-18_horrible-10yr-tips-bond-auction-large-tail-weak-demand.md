# Horrible 10yr TIPS Bond Auction: Large Tail, Weak Demand

## 1. Thesis

The $19 billion 10-year TIPS sale tailed 5bp with only 2.2x cover, and because TIPS are inflation insurance, weak demand inverts the usual interpretation: the market is pricing growth scare over inflation scare even with core inflation near 3% and a fresh Fed cut. The presenter reads the print as backward — breakevens grinding to 2.38% while oil falls, record-low crude longs, and nominal retail strength masking real weakness all argue inflation risk is underpriced, not gone.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] [03:25] Weak TIPS demand means growth fear dominates inflation fear because investors shun inflation insurance when they expect disinflation or cooling growth.
- [TAKE] [06:01] A Fed cut with inflation at 3% looks dovish because every headline and core gauge sits nearer 3% than the 2% target.
- [TAKE] [07:49] Breakevens rising to 2.38% while oil trends lower is a notable divergence because energy is the main force holding headline CPI at 2.7% below 3% core.
- [TAKE] [09:35] Nominal retail strength overstates the consumer because higher prices inflate dollars spent, leaving a much weaker inflation-adjusted picture.
- [TAKE] [12:05] The market has it backward to fear growth over inflation when inflation runs 3% into rate cuts with record-low oil longs primed for a pop.
