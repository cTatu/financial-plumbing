# Pt 1: Recession Causes: What Sparks One and What It Says About the 2025 Economy

## 1. Thesis

Recessions share a repeatable sequence — oil spikes destroy demand, confidence falls, PPI-led inflation squeezes spending that is 65% of GDP, equities peak first, the curve inverts as shorts chase coming cuts, and banks withdraw credit because borrow-short-lend-long turns unprofitable — and with confidence near 51-52, the top 10% funding half of spending, two negative GDP quarters and an inverted curve already seen in 2022 without an NBER call, plus an August-2019 inversion that preceded the repo seizure, the video frames 2025 as late-cycle on classic triggers even before Part 2 argues the private-credit engine itself broke after 2008.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] [03:00] Oil causes recessions through demand destruction because energy prices enter making, shipping, commuting and retailing at every step.
- [TAKE] [05:09] Confidence leads spending and spending is 65.00% of GDP, so a 51.00-to-52.00 confidence low matters even when top-10% outlays mask it.
- [TAKE] [17:13] Inversion is causal because banks lose the borrow-short-lend-long spread near 2.75%, stop creating loans and leases, and tighten financial conditions.
- [TAKE] [16:54] The bond market front-runs the Fed because trillions pile into 2-year and short paper for the cutting-cycle price jump, which inverts then un-inverts the curve.
- [TAKE] [23:32] The Fed follows the 2-year rather than leading it, with the 2-year-minus-funds spread turning negative before cuts and positive before hikes.
