# Breaking: Banks Request a RECORD $75 BILLION from the Fed's Standing Repo Facility

## 1. Thesis

The $74.6 billion year-end Standing Repo draw is the largest since the facility became permanent in 2021, but it confirms a predicted seasonal crunch layered on structural scarcity rather than a sudden seizure: QT destroyed $1.3 trillion of reserves from a $4.3 trillion peak toward $3 trillion while Treasury's checking balance absorbed another $500 billion, leaving the reverse-repo buffer empty into window-dressing. SOFR breaching 3.75% for two days with 25% of $3.2 trillion financing at 3.79-3.86% shows stress is broad but still year-end-typical, which is why the verdict depends on the first week of January. The Fed's shift to reserve-management bill buying already concedes the regime changed from excess to scarce reserves.

- [03:11] Mortgage-backed securities trade down 15% on price alone even as the total-return index ticked above its February 2021 high. (as of Dec 31, 2025 session)

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] QT plus the TGA rebuild removed roughly $1.8 trillion of combined liquidity, which flipped the system from the post-2021 excess-reserve regime to a scarce-reserve regime.
- [TAKE] Window-dressing explains the record size because leverage, liquidity-coverage, and supplementary-leverage reporting force dealers to hoard dollars into Dec 31.
- [TAKE] SOFR measures willing-and-able dollar supply rather than the Fed's posted range, so scarcity lets the lone cash holder set a high price for pledged Treasuries and MBS.
- [TAKE] MBS-heavy pledges reflect underwater collateral because a 15% price loss leaves banks holding duration they prefer to finance at 3.75% rather than sell.
- [TAKE] Reserve-management purchases admit the Fed lost upper-bound control because printing reserves to buy short bills is the mechanical offset to QT-driven scarcity.

- [PREDICTS] The RRP-minus-SRF spread staying at its most negative since 2020 signals continued reserve drain. -> check: reserves sliding with TGA near $1T confirms it; reserves rebuilding on reserve-management buys refutes it.
