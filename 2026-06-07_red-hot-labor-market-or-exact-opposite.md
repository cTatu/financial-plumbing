# Red-Hot Labor Market, or the Exact Opposite?

## 1. Thesis

The hot headline — 7.6M openings and +172K payrolls at double consensus — dissolves on composition: hiring fell to 5.12M with hires-per-opening at a 2-year low, quits near 2020 lows, and all net growth concentrated in temporary World Cup leisure, lagging local government, and low-paid education-health care that alone exceeded 100% of 12-month payroll growth. With 79K full-time jobs lost, real earnings negative, savings near all-time lows, and revolving credit spiking, the report is a supply-shocked, consumption-funded late-cycle print that the bond market correctly repriced toward hikes rather than strength.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] Openings without hires signal looseness, not tightness, because fake or unfilled postings cost nothing while the hiring rate captures actual employer intent.
- [TAKE] The low-hire, low-fire regime plus separations outgrowing hires since 2021 and quits near 2020 lows shows workers lack confidence to switch jobs.
- [TAKE] Payroll strength is narrow and low-quality because temporary World Cup accommodation-food, lagging local government, and care work for an aging population do not signal cyclical expansion.
- [TAKE] Negative real earnings with spending beating income forces households into dissaving and 28-30% credit-card debt, which is contractionary once buffers exhaust.
- [TAKE] A 6% PPI versus CPI blowout leaves firms choosing margin compression or demand destruction, and a hike response to supply-driven energy inflation would cut consumption that is 68% of GDP.

## 4C Predicts
