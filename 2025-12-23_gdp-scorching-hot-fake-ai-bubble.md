# Just In: GDP Comes in SCORCHING HOT - But, Is America's GDP all Fake? A Giant AI Bubble?

## 1. Thesis

A 4.3% real GDP print against 3.3% expected looks scorching until it is decomposed: much of the snapback is the tariff front-run normalizing, as monthly imports round-tripped from $344 billion to $420 billion and back to $340 billion, leaving a five-year-low deficit that flatters the net-exports math. Underneath, consumption ($16.5 trillion, 70% of GDP) leans on healthcare and services while the average worker's $31.76 hourly wage keeps losing purchasing power in gold, M2-share, and per-capita-spending terms — which is why sentiment sits at record lows. The residual engine is AI capex: data-center investment up 300%+ contributes roughly a point (over 60%) of meager first-half growth, so a technology built to replace labor is the only thing keeping the economy out of recession — and it must accelerate endlessly to keep nominal growth above the cost of the debt.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] [01:21] Net exports mechanically swing GDP because America consumes more value than it produces, so tariff front-running imports created the earlier negative print and normalization flatters this one.
- [TAKE] [04:52] GDP acceleration requires growing a component faster than the economy, so holding consumption, investment, or government spending flat cannot lift the growth rate.
- [TAKE] [06:01] Debt sustainability is nominal growth versus the 10-year borrowing cost, so running nominal growth hot above the debt cost is the administration's stated strategy.
- [TAKE] [07:35] CPI-deflated wage gains mislead because CPI undercounts lived inflation, which is why gold-, M2-, and per-capita-spending-denominated wages keep plumbing new lows.
- [TAKE] [13:08] AI capex is the economy's growth anchor and its job-replacement engine at once, so the technology propping up GDP is designed to displace the workers GDP supposedly measures.
