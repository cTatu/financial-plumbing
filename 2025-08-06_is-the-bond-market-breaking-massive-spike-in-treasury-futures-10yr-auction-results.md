# Is the Bond Market Breaking? - Massive Spike in Treasury Futures + 10yr Auction Results

## 1. Thesis

A $42 billion 10-year auction tailing 1.1bp with bid-to-cover collapsing to 2.35 ends six months of stop-throughs and marks the weakest long-end demand since last summer. Minutes earlier, about 75,000 contracts concentrated in long-duration ZB futures dumped roughly $8.6 billion notional and bear-steepened 30Y-2Y by about 4bp — read as the long end repricing fiscal-dominance risk around a rumored dovish Fed-chair leak, not a plumbing glitch, with the $25 billion 30-year auction as the immediate confirmation test.

- [07:56] About 75,000 ZB contracts representing 30-year Treasuries traded in about 10 minutes. (as of Aug 6, 2025 session)

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] [05:57] Ending six straight stop-throughs with a 1.1bp tail plus bid-to-cover at 2.35 signals genuine long-end demand fade rather than noise.
- [TAKE] [08:29] Concentrating the 75,000-contract dump in ZB while 2-year and 5-year futures barely lifted shows sellers targeting duration where fiscal-dominance losses hurt most.
- [TAKE] [12:03] Running a bear steepener since the Fed started cutting means rising long yields with cuts, which inverts the normal bull-steepening relief governments get from easing.
- [TAKE] [07:22] Primary dealers absorbing more while indirect buyers step back means the buyer of last resort is being relied on more over recent months.
- [TAKE] [10:21] A leaked dovish/puppet Fed-chair rumor fits the price action because politicized easing expectations punish 30-year value through convexity first.
