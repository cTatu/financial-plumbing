# Japanese Bond Yields Soar to 17-yr Highs + $135 Billion in US Treasury Debt Auctioned Today

## 1. Thesis

Washington keeps funding itself where the demand is — $50B of 1-year plus $85B of 6-week bills in a single day with a record $105B 4-week bill already announced — because short, money-like paper levers easily and suppresses long-end yields, at the cost of faster TGA-refill stimulus, a weaker dollar, and a Fed that cannot hike without blowing up rollover interest. Japan is the mirror: its weakest 2-year auction since 2009 with 1-2 year yields at 17-year highs reflects BoJ-hike timing, not insolvency, since inflation finally runs positive with rising wages after the lost decades — which is why higher JGB yields are reflation first and a repatriation risk for Treasuries second.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] Short bills are money-like because low volatility paper levers readily into new dollars, which makes front-end supply stimulatory and inflationary while suppressing long-end yields by about 50 basis points.
- [TAKE] Concentrating issuance in 1-month to 6-month paper erodes Fed independence because rolling the stock every few months means any hike immediately blows out interest expense and the deficit.
- [TAKE] The soft 1-year cover of 2.9 against 3.1-3.2 averages shows demand is strongest at the very front, which is why Treasury sizes $85B of 6-week bills against only about $15B for a 30-year bond.
- [TAKE] The weak 2-year JGB reflects hike timing because buyers wait when the Bank of Japan may lift 0.5% tomorrow, so price falls and yield spikes without proving fiscal distress.
- [TAKE] Japan's move is partly healthy reflation because inflation turned positive from end-2021 into early 2022 with wages finally growing after 15-20 years of deflation, so higher short yields track growth rather than default risk.
