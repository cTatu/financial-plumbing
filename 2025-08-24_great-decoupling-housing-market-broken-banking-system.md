# Great Decoupling: Housing Market Exposes a Broken Banking System & Structural Shifts

## 1. Thesis

Housing looks broken because the monetary engine underneath it broke in 2008: private-bank real-estate credit never resumed its postwar growth trend, leaving a $15 trillion loan gap and a $13.8 trillion total-credit gap that government deficits and Fed balance-sheet money had to fill. Prices still rose because scarce-asset demand from K-shaped wealth, $150 billion per month of foreign inflows, and all-cash buyers replaced marginal mortgage borrowing — average mortgage size fell from $457,000 to $372,000 even as prices rose. The call is that national nominal prices likely grind higher if ISM reacceleration lifts anemic 1-2% loan growth, while real purchasing-power returns stay flat to negative.
