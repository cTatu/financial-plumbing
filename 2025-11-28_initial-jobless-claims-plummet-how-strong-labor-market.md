# JUST IN: Initial Jobless Claims Plummet - But, How Strong is the Labor Market, Really?

## 1. Thesis

Headline layoffs look calm as the post-shutdown data restarts, but breadth is deteriorating underneath: continuing claims, U3, and especially leading Black and youth unemployment are curling higher while ISM employment has been contracting for years, even as the rate-of-change in openings hints at early-cycle stabilization. The presenter frames this as the central labor-market tension — levels that look late-cycle and recession-adjacent versus momentum that looks like emerging from a silent recession — with AI displacement as the structural wedge between a vertical stock market and flat hiring.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] Low headline claims mask narrow breadth because leading Black and youth series are rising vertically while U3 and continuing claims grind higher.
- [TAKE] U6 at 8.00% is the better labor lens than 4.40% U3 because it captures marginally attached and part-time-for-economic-reasons workers the headline excludes.
- [TAKE] AI ranking as the second-most cited layoff driver with the S&P 500 vertical and openings flat points to displacement rather than a simple cyclical freeze.
- [TAKE] Years of sub-50 ISM employment against low claims creates the conflicting read where levels warn into recession while openings momentum hints at early-cycle exit from a silent recession.
- [TAKE] Weekly claims need 3-week to 6-week averaging because single-week spikes mislead and only a sustained moving-average turn marks regime change.
