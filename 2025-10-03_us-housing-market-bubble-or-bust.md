# US Housing Market - Bubble, or Bust?

## 1. Thesis

Housing keeps grinding sideways-to-higher in nominal dollars not because families can afford it but because the denominator broke: Case-Shiller's 1.7-2.3% yearly gains with a 0.1% monthly dip still leave prices up 80% since 2006 and 147% since 2012, while annual interest on the average home tripled from $10,000 to $26,000 and homes cost 160% more than real wage purchasing power versus 2007. The marginal bid comes from cash — average mortgage size falling since 2022 against a $512,000 price, $40 trillion plus $79 trillion of top-quintile wealth against $11 trillion for the bottom 40%, $100 billion per month of recycled trade-deficit inflows absorbing roughly 800,000 homes, and the Fed's $2 trillion of MBS suppressing rates — so policy defends nominal prices as a boomer pension fund even as CPI-adjusted and gold-denominated prices show the real break.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] [03:30] Mortgage rates lead prices by about nine months because falling inverted rates lift year-over-year appreciation and rising rates suppress it.
- [TAKE] [05:50] Nominal strength hides real weakness because CPI-adjusted prices have pulled back for three years while gold-denominated prices sit near record affordability.
- [TAKE] [08:02] Falling average mortgage size against flat-to-rising prices proves cash buyers dominate because financed demand cannot explain the level.
- [TAKE] [08:57] Recycled trade deficits plus concentrated wealth supply price-insensitive bids because $100B per month must buy US assets including houses.
- [TAKE] [11:41] Fed MBS buying suppresses mortgage rates because printing reserves to lift bond prices lowers yields and invites bidding wars.

## 7. Follow-ups

- Watch Case-Shiller near 1.7-2.3% YoY with month-over-month near -0.1%: yearly gains holding above CPI with flat months confirms sideways-to-higher grind, while YoY turning negative with volume rising says the cash bid failed.
- Watch average price near $512,000 against average mortgage size: price flat-to-up with mortgage size still falling confirms cash-buyer dominance, while mortgage size rising with price says financed buyers returned.
- Watch mortgage-to-10Y spread near 2.3% with MOVE: spread narrowing below 2.3% on lower volatility confirms rate relief without 10Y help, while spread back toward 2.9% on higher volatility says affordability worsens.
