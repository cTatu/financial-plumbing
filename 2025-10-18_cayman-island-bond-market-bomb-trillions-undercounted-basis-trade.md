# Cayman Island Bond Market BOMB: Trillions Undercounted—How Basis Trades Could Crash Amid Repo Stress

## 1. Thesis

A Federal Reserve report reveals the United States' largest creditor is not Japan or China but Cayman-domiciled hedge funds running the Treasury cash-futures basis trade: a $1.4 trillion TIC undercount lifts Cayman holdings to about $1.8 trillion, with those funds absorbing roughly 40% of all new Treasury issuance since January 2022. The trade harvests only 10 to 30 basis points of convergence between cash bonds and futures, so it is levered 50-to-1 up to 100-to-1 through overnight repo pledging, making it delta-neutral to direction but lethal to funding cost and basis widening. With SOFR printing above the 4.25% discount-window ceiling outside month-end, quarter-end, or tax day, the October stress echoes the March 2020 unwind where the basis went 400 basis points the wrong way — forced bond selling that pushes yields vertically, which a 125%-of-GDP, 7%-deficit sovereign paying 23% of receipts in interest cannot afford.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] The basis trade is delta-neutral to bond direction because the long cash bond gains when yields fall while the short future gains when yields rise, leaving only futures-cash convergence as profit.
- [TAKE] The 10-to-30-basis-point edge becomes economic only at 50-to-1 to 100-to-1 repo leverage because the position must be refinanced every night in overnight repo.
- [TAKE] Volatility-driven haircut increases plus SOFR elevation jointly force unwinds because higher funding cost raises carry while mark-to-market basis widening hits equity at the same time.
- [TAKE] Unwinding requires selling the long bond and buying back the short future, so widespread exits push bond prices down, futures up, the basis further wrong-way, and yields sharply higher.
- [TAKE] A Cayman-concentrated, highly levered holder of nearly half of recent issuance is a fragile funding base for a 125%-of-GDP sovereign running a 7% procyclical deficit with 23% of receipts to interest.
