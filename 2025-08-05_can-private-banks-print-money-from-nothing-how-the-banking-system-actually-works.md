# Can Private Banks Print Money from Nothing? - How the Banking System Actually Works

## 1. Thesis

Private banks create most dollars out of nothing, and Richard Werner's live €200,000 loan experiment proves the credit-creation theory while rejecting intermediation and fractional-reserve accounts. The scale makes the point before the theory: $5.7 trillion of base money supports $18.5 trillion of bank credit and $22 trillion of M2 — about 4x leverage — while $40 trillion of real estate, $62 trillion of equities, and the national debt stack roughly $139 trillion of dollar claims on that base. The US reserve requirement is 0%, loan officers never checked reserves, no offsetting account fell by €200,000, and the balance sheet simply expanded — so Basel-style regulation built on intermediation misreads the system, and the part-two agenda is disaggregated credit (C&I versus real-estate versus consumer) mapped to GDP, home prices, and consumption.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] [07:20] Intermediation already fails arithmetically because $5.7 trillion of base cannot fund $22 trillion of M2 by shuffling deposits.
- [TAKE] [19:54] Fractional-reserve theory fails experimentally because credit officers signed the €200,000 loan without checking reserves or deposits.
- [TAKE] [17:26] Credit creation fits because customer deposits sit on the balance sheet as the bank's loans, so new lending expands both assets and liabilities.
- [TAKE] [08:29] A 0% requirement with all-three-theories Bank of England guidance leaves banks structurally exposed to runs and Basel guardrails aimed at the wrong model.
- [TAKE] [29:31] Disaggregation is the forecasting edge because C&I, real-estate, and consumer loan growth should map separately to GDP, home prices, and consumption rather than one M2 blob.
