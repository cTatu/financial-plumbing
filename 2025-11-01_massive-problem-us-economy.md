# We Have a *Massive* Problem with the US Economy

## 1. Thesis

Deaths of despair have roughly tripled since 2000 to about 240,000 per year, now three times the Great Depression rate, and the break coincides with the post-2000 globalization surge that took the current-account deficit from about $120 billion to about $1 trillion per year. That deficit must arithmetically return as roughly $1 trillion per year of foreign buying of US stocks, bonds, and real estate, lifting the top 1% share of wealth and an 8,290% S&P run against 814% wage growth while personal income-to-M2, wages-to-GDP, wages-to-homes, and labor participation all peaked around 2000, so the social collapse is framed as the human counterpart of hyperfinancialization rather than a cyclical downturn.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] A $1 trillion current-account deficit must return as $1 trillion of capital inflows, so foreign buying of real estate, Treasuries, and stocks is arithmetic rather than optional.
- [TAKE] The top-1% net-worth-to-GDP line rises because inflows capitalize into financial assets faster than GDP, which defines the K-shaped and hyperfinancialized economy.
- [TAKE] The S&P growing 10 times faster than wages proves the gains accrued to capital while labor lost share, with QE2 and QE3 widening the early-2010s break.
- [TAKE] Every real-economy denominator peaked in 2000, including S&P-in-gold, income-to-M2, participation, and wages-in-gold, so 2000 marks the structural turn rather than a cycle.
- [TAKE] Balancing trade is only a first step because the deficit-recycling machine simultaneously drives asset bubbles, housing exclusion, and despair mortality.
