# Just In: Report Shows Consumers Drowning in Record $5.07 Trillion - Credit Card Debt Soars to Record

## 1. Thesis

September's G.19 shows the consumer funding stagnation with leverage: $5.08 trillion outstanding growing 3.10% annualized ($157 billion flow, $130 billion 12-month average pace) while assessed card rates sit at 22.83% even as the 10-year fell, stretching the card-minus-10-year spread to 18.70% from ~10-11% in the early 2010s. That prices a $280-billion annual card-interest transfer and $571 billion of total personal interest — 106 hours of average work per worker just for interest, back at the 105-hour line that preceded prior recessions — which the presenter frames as the K-economy's lower leg: asset owners clip the spread while the bottom 80% work ~13 days a year to service debt on credit growing 324% faster than wages since 1971.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] Rate-of-change of private-bank-created credit matters because banks print ~90% of dollars when they lend, so revolving re-leveraging is a GDP input.
- [TAKE] Card spreads widening to 18.70% while the 10-year falls proves pricing power sits with banks rather than funding costs.
- [TAKE] The $280.00 billion card-interest and $571.00 billion total-interest tallies define the K economy because asset owners receive the spread that indebted labor pays.
- [TAKE] Consumer credit decoupling from real GDP since the 1990s shows debt funding consumption that output no longer validates.
- [TAKE] Commercial-and-industrial credit falling from 26.00% to 14.00% of bank credit shows total bank credit growing through household leverage rather than productive lending.
