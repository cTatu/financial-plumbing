# $80 Billion Liquidity Shock to Stock Market? Repo Stress Worsens + Very Ugly 20yr Bond Auction

## 1. Thesis

Liquidity stress is back in the same form as Q3-Q4: about a quarter of a $3.2T repo market printed above the Fed's 3.75% cap for two straight days while the 20-year auction tailed 2bp with the second-weakest foreign bid on record. With RRP exhausted, $216B of bill settlements must now fund from risk-asset sales — the $80B settlement shock running into April — so long-end weakness and equity drawdowns on settlement days are plumbing-driven, even as the bond bid elsewhere hints growth, not just supply, is starting to matter.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] Upper-quartile SOFR above the cap for hundreds of billions proves demand exceeds lendable supply and the Fed is losing the overnight price outside quarter-end.
- [TAKE] A 2bp tail with 2.36 cover and 17.6% dealer take is weak demand, because price had to fall to clear and the buyer of last resort filled the gap.
- [TAKE] With RRP at zero, $216B of bill settlements must come from selling risk assets, which is why settlement days skew equity-negative into April.
- [TAKE] The Fed restarted bill buying in mid-December precisely because SOFR dislocation threatened the basis trade that absorbs about half of issuance.
- [TAKE] Bull-flattening with a 2022-low 2Y says bonds now fear growth and AI-driven deflation, which is the steelman for owning duration against consensus long-end bearishness.
