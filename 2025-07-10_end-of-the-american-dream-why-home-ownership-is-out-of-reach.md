# End of the American Dream - Why Home Ownership is Out of Reach

## 1. Thesis

Homes are unaffordable not because houses got better but because the denominator broke: 42% base-money expansion lifted nominal prices 42-44% while wages in gold fell 82%, and the $100B-per-month capital-account surplus plus $2T of Fed MBS buying financialized the stock into a boomer retirement fund that policy cannot let reprice. Rate shock tripled annual interest to $3,200 and pushed hours-to-buy from 7,500 to 16,000, so the fix is not lower short rates — 300bp of cuts would steepen longs higher — but restoring scarce-money wages and removing subsidized demand.

- [03:44] Monetary base M0 rose 42% over ten years while average home prices rose 42% to 44% over the same span. (as of 2015-2025)
- [04:53] Home prices fell 81% in gold terms from 800 ounces to 150 ounces over the past 20 years. (as of 2005-2025 comparison)
- [09:03] Buying the average home requires about 16,000 hours of work versus about 7,500 hours at the end of the gold standard. (as of 1971-2025 comparison)
- [12:08] The Fed holds about $2 trillion of mortgage-backed securities equal to about 11% of all US mortgages. (as of Jul 2025)
- [16:39] The US current-account deficit runs about $311 billion per quarter, or about $100 billion per month. (as of Q1 2025)
- [19:43] The average hourly wage fell 82% in gold terms from 0.06 ounces per hour to 0.01 ounces per hour over 20 years. (as of 2005-2025 comparison)
- [23:12] The average home price rose 200% faster than the real purchasing power of the average hourly wage since 2008. (as of 2008-2025 comparison)

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] [04:38] Houses did not get more valuable because the fall from 800 to 150 ounces proves the dollar lost purchasing power while the wooden box stayed the same.
- [TAKE] [09:03] Wages confirm the squeeze because hours-to-buy doubled from 7,500 to 16,000 while nominal GDP growth accrued to asset holders rather than labor.
- [TAKE] [11:08] Fed MBS buying subsidizes risk because printing reserves to lift bond values suppresses mortgage rates and invites bidding wars that lift prices.
- [TAKE] [17:47] Trade deficits fund housing demand because $100B per month of capital-account surplus must buy US assets and single-family homes absorb part of that flow.
- [TAKE] [27:15] Cutting funds 300 basis points lifts mortgage rates because longs price fiscal dominance and inflation while shorts follow funds, so the curve bear-steepens.
