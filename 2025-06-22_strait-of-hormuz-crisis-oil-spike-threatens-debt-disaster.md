# Strait of Hormuz Crisis: Oil Spike Threatens Debt Disaster

## 1. Thesis

The U.S. strikes on Iran's nuclear program plus an Iranian parliamentary vote to close Hormuz put 20-25% of world oil at risk of a 50-95% spike toward the $90-$120 JPMorgan-Goldman tail, and the video traces that shock through the stagflationary chain rather than the creditor-selling channel covered June 13. Oil lifts PPI almost in real time and CPI within months, crushes real growth, widens credit spreads, rolls financial assets that fund half of consumption and capital-gains receipts, and leaves a Fed scarred by transitory unable to cut — so with the 10-year at and the 20-year and 30-year above fading nominal growth on a 6.5% deficit, recession widens borrowing toward 15-17% and higher supply lifts rates that are paid for with yet more deficit.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] Every material oil spike on the log chart precedes NBER recession because energy is the foundation input to making and moving everything.
- [TAKE] PPI follows oil in real time and consumer spending inversely follows PPI, so $100-plus oil slows 65%-of-GDP consumption with a short lag.
- [TAKE] Equities lead oil tops because K-shaped reliance on asset-funded spending breaks the wealth effect before crude peaks.
- [TAKE] The scarred Fed cannot cut into spiking PPI and CPI, so short rates stay flat while inflation and supply push long yields higher.
- [TAKE] Recession is a margin call because 6.50% borrowing gaps toward 15.00%-17.00% exactly when supply surges, which lifts rates and funds interest with more deficit.
