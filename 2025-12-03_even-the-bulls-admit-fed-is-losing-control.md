# Even the Bulls Admit: The Fed is Losing Control

## 1. Thesis

Five straight days of median SOFR over the 4.00% ceiling — before year-end even arrives — plus a 75th percentile running 10-25bp over the bound for six weeks shows the Fed losing both edges of its corridor: the ceiling leaks under scarcity just as the floor leaked under the 2022 flood. With QT having ended December 1 into only mechanical reserve-management buying, a $900B TGA buffer that must survive $650B of five-day bill rolls, and the SOFR-RRP average trending tight since August, the presenter argues the durable fix is outright reserve creation — printed bank reserves exchanged for securities — before year-end pressure layers another 20-30bp onto an already-elevated spread.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] The corridor is leaking at the top under scarcity just as it leaked at the bottom under the 2022 flood, so the tools are failing in both directions.
- [TAKE] The median understates the squeeze because $800 billion in the 75th percentile has paid 10-25bp over the backstop for six weeks.
- [TAKE] Base money and broad money are different constraints because M2 can sit at all-time highs on private credit while Fed-created reserves keep draining.
- [TAKE] The coming TGA drawdown cannot be counted as relief because $650 billion of five-day bill rolls plus monthly coupons force a permanently large buffer.
- [TAKE] Ending QT into mechanical reserve-management buying is too small because the 10-day SOFR-RRP average has tightened since August, so only outright reserve creation rebuilds the tank.

## 4C Predicts

- [PREDICTS] Year-end pressure adds another 20-30bp to SOFR spreads on top of the already-elevated base into quarter-end and year-end. -> check: SOFR-RRP and 75th-percentile premium widening further through December confirms it; spreads collapsing back toward the floor refutes it.
- [PREDICTS] The Fed announces a reserve-management program that creates bank reserves by buying securities from banks. -> check: balance-sheet expansion with reserve growth and narrower spreads confirms it; book flat-to-shrinking with spreads wide refutes it.
