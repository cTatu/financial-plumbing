# Major Overnight News: Global Bond Yields Surge as Trump Fires Fed Governor Lisa Cook

## 1. Thesis

Overnight markets treated Trump's firing of Governor Cook as expected erosion of Fed independence: S&P futures dipped 0.4% then recovered, 10-year and 30-year yields added 4-5bp with the 30-year near 4.92%, and the dollar faded without disorder. The durable story is global, with French, British, Japanese, and Chinese 30-year yields all pressing higher on fiscal and nominal-growth repricing rather than a US-only shock. The presenter reads Japan's record 3.22% 30-year as healthy reacceleration with repatriation risk for Treasuries, while France and Britain look like genuine fiscal distress — and US housing keeps eroding in real terms at 1.9% nominal versus 2.7% CPI with 3.1% core behind it.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] Nominal durable-goods strength is an aircraft-and-inflation illusion because real orders have fallen for 30 years and ex-transport growth is only ~1-2%.
- [TAKE] Housing is not crashing in nominal dollars at 1.9% gains, but it is eroding in real terms against 2.7% CPI and 3.1% core.
- [TAKE] Firing a governor lifts long yields and weakens the dollar through independence erosion, yet muted moves show much of the tension was already priced.
- [TAKE] Japan's record 30-year yield reflects nominal reacceleration after lost decades rather than implosion because the yen is not collapsing.
- [TAKE] French and British 30-year spreads above Italy-crisis levels mark genuine fiscal distress rather than healthy growth repricing.
