# Breaking: Stocks Plunge on Lowest Consumer Sentiment in 72yrs (On Record)

## 1. Thesis

A record-weak Michigan survey — current conditions at an all-time low and headline sentiment 0.30 points from the all-time low — collided with the prior day's 22-year-high layoff print to extend an ~10-day equity drawdown, with the Nasdaq down ~5.30% on the week. The presenter reads the mix as late-cycle stagflationary de-dollarization risk rather than ordinary weak-data bullishness: CPI already carries a 3-handle with ISM-services prices vertical, bonds are only tactically bid while the 10-year minus Fed funds spread re-widens, and simultaneous European-session selling in bonds, dollars and equity futures reprised the January-April capital-rotation footprint, now with gold and franc/euro as the receivers.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] Record-low current conditions with sentiment 0.30 points from the all-time low matters because it lands one day after 22-year-high layoffs, linking household fear to labor loosening.
- [TAKE] Weak data is not cleanly bullish for bonds because CPI already has a 3-handle with ISM-services prices vertical, so cuts into rising inflation steepen rather than relieve the long end.
- [TAKE] The 213-hour cost of one S&P share explains youth discontent with equity records because wages trailing the market by 914% excludes labor from asset gains.
- [TAKE] Joint European-session selling in bonds, dollars and equity futures repeats the January-April de-dollarization footprint because foreigners exit dollar assets rather than rotating within them.
- [TAKE] Populism is bearish for stocks and bonds but not gold because the last populist era left the S&P sideways in dollars while down 90% in gold terms.
