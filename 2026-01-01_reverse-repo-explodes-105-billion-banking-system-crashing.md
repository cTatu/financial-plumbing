# Breaking: Reverse Repo Explodes to $105 Billion - Is the Banking System Really Crashing?

## 1. Thesis

The $106B reverse-repo spike is year-end window-dressing, not a banking collapse — banks hoarded cash at the Fed's 3.5% floor to flatter regulatory ratios even though SOFR paid ~3.71%. The real stress signal was the opposite facility: a record $74.6B tapped from the standing repo at 3.75% shows cash was scarce and SOFR had broken above the upper bound. On the presenter's RRP-minus-SRF spread, liquidity is now more ample than in Oct-Dec, but with reserves down to ~$3T on QT plus TGA drain the system remains fragile, which is why the Fed restarted reserve-management bill buying — a flow that correlates with SPX and especially BTC.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] Reverse repo and standing repo are opposite facilities that must be read as a spread: RRP use signals excess cash parked at the Fed, while SRF use signals cash shortage that pays the penalty 3.75% rate.
- [TAKE] Parking at 3.5% RRP while SOFR pays 3.71% is uneconomic unless banks are hoarding for window-dressing into liquidity-coverage and year-end regulatory snapshots, with the Fed as the risk-free counterparty.
- [TAKE] SOFR breaking above the 3.75% upper bound plus record SRF taps means pricing power sat with the few lenders holding cash, forcing the Fed to print reserves to buy short-term bills and push short-term yields back down.
- [TAKE] The five-month rate of change in bank reserves tracks the five-month rate of change in SPX weakly and BTC tightly, while gold front-runs Fed action rather than tracking contemporaneous printing.
