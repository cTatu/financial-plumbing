# Hyperfinancialization - Pt 2 - Solutions

## 1. Thesis

This is the solutions companion to the hyperfinancialization diagnosis: average labor's share peaked around China's 2001 WTO entry and then lost for two decades on every denominator — wages versus gold, income versus M2, wages versus GDP — while debt, corporate profits, and the S&P 500 all rose against GDP. The cause is framed as mechanical rather than cultural: a roughly $130-billion-per-month trade deficit must return as a capital-account surplus bid for FIRE assets, amplified by a dollar at least 50% overvalued that pays foreigners both ways through positive carry. The fix is therefore two-part — shrink the trade deficit toward balance and restore a fairly valued, much weaker dollar to make American manufacturing saleable again — accepting pricier imports in exchange for higher real wages, over intense opposition from asset holders and a process measured in a decade, not quarters.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] [34:15] A trade deficit is arithmetically a capital-account surplus, so about $130 billion per month must flow into stocks, bonds, farmland, single-family homes, and Treasuries.
- [TAKE] [27:10] Positive carry pays foreigners twice because dollar appreciation plus dollar-asset appreciation combine into an 11% to 25% unhedged return that turns the dollar into the funding currency.
- [TAKE] [31:29] An overvalued dollar produces American Dutch disease where finance, insurance, and real estate absorb talent and capital while manufacturing employment share and real wages fall together.
- [TAKE] [19:02] The marginal productivity of debt and money has declined since the mid-1990s because GDP divided by federal debt and GDP divided by M2 both trend down.
- [TAKE] [50:27] A fairly valued weaker dollar raises import prices but restores manufacturing competitiveness so real purchasing-power wages rise, which is why financial-asset holders and government will fight it.
