# Global Bond Market Meltdown - How Iran Engineered a Global Liquidity Crisis

## 1. Thesis

Friday's global bond rout was not primarily an inflation repricing but a balance-of-payments liquidity squeeze engineered through Hormuz: 15% to 20% of oil supply cut forces importers into wider current-account deficits that demand more foreign financing just as the GCC flips from $1.5 billion per day creditor to debtor and Europe plus advanced Asia are pushed the same way. With only 25 of the 65 basis point rise in the US 10-year from breakevens, the rest from supply and repatriation pressure, Japan and the UK selling reserves, and RMP liquidity tapering, higher yields abroad force Treasury selling that lifts US rates, deficits, and debt costs into a synchronized sovereign funding crisis.

- [TAKE] [01:56] Supply must equal consumption for physical oil, so a 20% supply cut must reduce consumption through higher prices that drag PPI and then CPI.
- [TAKE] [06:14] Higher gilt yields pull US yields higher because investors sell lower-yielding Treasuries to buy higher-yielding gilts.
- [TAKE] [08:04] The importer problem is a requirement for more foreign financing as trade deficits widen, not merely higher CPI to compensate bondholders.
