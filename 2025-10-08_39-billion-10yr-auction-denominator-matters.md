# $39 Billion 10yr Treasury Bond Auction + Why Your Denominator Matters So Much

## 1. Thesis

A soft $39 billion 10-year auction — a 0.3-basis-point tail with 2.48 cover and foreign share sliding to 66% — matters less than the denominator it is priced in: spot gold firmly above $4,000 with silver up 70% and gold up 55% year-to-date against only 14% for the S&P leaves the S&P down 26% in gold terms and flat in euros after an 11% dollar slide. The run-it-hot read is that 1965-1982 is the rhyme, when the S&P went sideways for 17 years in dollars while losing 93% in gold and 60-70% in M2 terms, so rising 10-year, 30-year, 5-year, and gold since Fed cuts signal monetary reordering rather than a growth scare.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] The 0.3bp tail with 2.48 cover and 66% indirect marks softening demand because two of the last three auctions tailed after a year of stop-throughs.
- [TAKE] Record 24% direct takedown offsets fading foreign sponsorship mechanically because domestic bidders absorb the duration foreigners leave behind.
- [TAKE] Pricing the S&P in gold or euros exposes the denominator illusion because 14% dollar gains become minus 26% in gold and flat in euros after the 11% dollar slide.
- [TAKE] The 1965-1982 rhyme warns that sideways dollars can still mean 93% debasement because gold tracks purchasing-power loss that nominal indexes hide.
- [TAKE] Yields and gold rising together after Fed cuts confirm the reordering signal because easing into loose policy lifts both term premium and bearer-money demand.
