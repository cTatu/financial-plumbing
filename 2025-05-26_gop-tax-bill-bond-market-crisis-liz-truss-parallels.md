# Could GOP Tax Bill Trigger a Bond Market Crisis? - 'Liz Truss Moment' Parallels

## 1. Thesis

The House-passed reconciliation bill is framed as America's Liz Truss test: starting from a 6.3% deficit with interest already near 20% of receipts, adding roughly 1% of GDP to the deficit — half the UK's 2% shock that spiked gilts 130bp in five days — prices roughly 65 to 75bp higher on the US 10-year toward 5.3% and about $280B per year of extra interest that must be borrowed at ever higher rates. With US 10-year already 20bp above 4.3% nominal growth, UK-US deficits correlated since 1990, foreign net Treasury buying gone for a decade, and debt-to-GDP paths toward 165% to 225% where every 50bp costs $185B per year, the bond vigilantes are expected to force either fiscal retreat or QE and yield curve control well before the extremes print.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] [03:41] Speed kills as much as size because a 130bp five-day spike collapses bond collateral and forces insolvencies faster than balance sheets can adjust.
- [TAKE] [05:34] Stocks down plus bonds down plus currency down together marks capital flight and an emerging-market-style loss of confidence rather than normal risk-off.
- [TAKE] [18:47] Borrowing new debt at higher rates to pay old interest makes debt-to-GDP nonlinear once yields exceed nominal growth.
- [TAKE] [35:09] Growing nominal GDP faster than the debt is the only non-default exit because CBO-style numerator-only scoring ignores the denominator channel.
- [TAKE] [37:43] Once bond math binds near 150% to 160% debt-to-GDP, the resolution is QE or yield curve control imposed by vigilantes rather than chosen by policymakers.
