# Are We Living in a Silent Depression? - Truth about Wages

## 1. Thesis

Record GDP, stocks, and corporate profits coexist with a multi-decade wage collapse when priced in scarce denominators: GDP per capita, M2, homes, equities, and gold have all compounded several times faster than average hourly pay since 1970, with the sharpest leg after 2008 ZERP plus record deficits. The result is a K-shaped economy where asset owners capture debasement through leveraged housing and equities while wage-only households face 7,000-to-16,000 hours for a home, 20-to-190 hours for one S&P share, and social markers consistent with prior depressions.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] [02:44] Relative charts compare rates of change, so GDP per capita growing 3% per year requires wages growing 3% per year to hold the line flat.
- [TAKE] [09:10] The most violent leg of equity-versus-wage divergence arrived after 2008 on ZERP, ultra-low rates, easy money, and record deficits via Cantillon effects.
- [TAKE] [13:21] GDP divided by M2 and GDP divided by debt show diminishing returns from printing, with debt-financed growth overwhelming real output since 1981 and money velocity collapsing alongside.
- [TAKE] [17:45] Housing is leveraged exposure to debasement because a 10% down payment captures price gains on the full home value as more dollars chase relatively scarce shelter, unlike unlevered wages or equities.
- [TAKE] [18:34] The economy splits into holders of stocks and homes who capture the 5-times money and 7-times equity compounding versus debt-only households who absorb it as higher living costs.
