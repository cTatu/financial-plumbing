# The Great Dollar Unwind: The $60 Trillion Dollar Trade Unwind... It Already Started

## 1. Thesis

The decades-long dollar carry trade — foreigners borrowing in depreciating local currencies to buy dollar assets while the dollar structurally appreciates — has begun to reverse: post-Liberation Day the dollar now falls even as US yields rise, and with gross foreign holdings near $60 trillion and over $9 trillion of US equities unhedged, even a gradual rotation home means a structurally weaker dollar, persistent pressure on US stocks, and a slow headwind rather than a single crash day.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] [07:57] Selling Treasuries pushes US yields up because price and yield move inversely, so foreign liquidation raises the US leg of the rate spread.
- [TAKE] [08:45] Selling the dollar proceeds to buy francs, yen, or euros pushes the dollar down, which is why yields can rise while the currency falls.
- [TAKE] [26:32] A $140B monthly trade deficit must be financed by a matching capital-account surplus plus fiscal-debt issuance sold to foreigners.
- [TAKE] [31:02] Exchange-rate strength and domestic purchasing power are distinct, since DXY rose during five years of 20-25% cumulative inflation.
- [TAKE] [32:05] Tariffs alone cannot close the current-account gap, so the administration needs a weaker dollar to restore competitiveness.
