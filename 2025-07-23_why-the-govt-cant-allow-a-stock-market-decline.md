# Why the Govt Can't Allow a Stock Market Decline

## 1. Thesis

Washington is structurally long equities because the tax base now is: wages grow at one-fifth the pace of stocks and trail M2 while corporate profits and the S&P outrun GDP, so record 2021 cap-gains realizations at 8.8% of GDP and 15% of receipts filled a 7%-of-GDP deficit that headline 4.1% unemployment cannot. With half of stocks held by the top 1% and 93% by the top 10%, a deep drawdown collapses receipts, widens the deficit, forces bond supply higher, and reprices yields — which is why both parties protect the $345-billion-a-year preferential cap-gains regime and why a 70-80% crash would mean EM-style money printing, not austerity.

## 2. Key Claims

- [03:46] The S&P 500 delivered about 20% returns in each of two back-to-back years going into the analysis window. (as of 2023-2024 run-up)
- [06:48] The federal deficit widened from 1% of GDP going into 2008 to 7% of GDP today while headline unemployment is lower now than then. (as of Jul 2025)
- [08:24] Realized capital gains hit 8.8% of GDP in 2021, a record above the 5.7% to 6.4% peaks around prior bull-market tops. (as of 2021 IRS/CBO)
- [08:49] Capital gains supplied about 15% of total tax revenue in 2021-2022 versus about 10% to 11% around the 2007-2008 top. (as of 2021-2022 IRS)
- [09:54] The S&P 500 tracked M0 base-money growth since the early 1970s while total wages grew at about one-fifth the S&P pace and about one-quarter the M0 pace. (as of 1971-Jul 2025 chart)
- [11:24] Buying one S&P 500 share requires about 200 hours of average work today versus about 20 hours when boomers entered the labor force. (as of 1971-Jul 2025 comparison)
- [17:43] Capital-gains realizations exceeded $2 trillion in 2021 to reach a 40-year high in income and taxes paid by high-income groups. (as of 2021 IRS via Tax Foundation)
- [20:08] Investment income was nearly $2.6 trillion in 2021, including about $2 trillion of net capital gains, versus about $9 trillion of wages and salaries. (as of 2021 IRS)
- [25:00] Preferential capital-gains treatment reduces federal collections by about $345 billion per year, split into $240 billion of rate preference plus $62 billion of death-transfer exclusion plus $43 billion of primary-residence exclusion. (as of 2024 Peterson/CBO estimate)
- [28:42] The top 1% own about half of US equities and the top 10% own about 93% of US equities. (as of Jul 2025 Fed DFA as cited)

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] [05:52] Stocks hedge debasement because the S&P compounds near 8% per year while post-2008 M2 also grows near 8%, leaving real equity returns flat without forward repricing.
- [TAKE] [11:42] Financial assets fill the GDP gap because wages as a share of GDP fall while corporate-profit share doubles and S&P-to-GDP marches up and to the right.
- [TAKE] [16:50] Receipts track stocks rather than wages because total wages underperform tax-receipt growth while the S&P outperforms it, especially after 2009.
- [TAKE] [24:16] The tax code subsidizes hoarding over working because long-held gains face a much lower rate than a one-week gain or wage income, concentrating the benefit in seven-figure earners.
- [TAKE] [32:11] A 70-80% crash is fiscally impossible without printing because the deficit would blow toward 20% of GDP as cap-gains revenue vanished and bond supply repriced yields.
