# 20yr Bond Auction - Results Explained

## 1. Thesis

The $13 billion 20-year sale printed exactly on the when-issued with only 13% dealer take, which reads as strong until size and context are added: $13 billion is small enough to avoid a tail, the tenor has tailed more often than not since summer 2024, and the bond still rose 4 basis points on the day after rising 8 basis points on Friday's Iran-Israel shock. With the 20-year persistently yielding above the 30-year on TLT-driven technicals, equities rallying on de-escalation headlines while long yields climb, the dollar probing three-year lows near 97.70, and bill-heavy issuance leaving refinancing exposed to any Fed hike, price action says duration demand is thinner than three straight good auctions suggest.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] The even print is flattered by a $13 billion size that needs little price concession to clear, so scaling to $100 billion would demand a tail and higher yield.
- [TAKE] The 20-year-over-30-year inversion reflects TLT short-expression flows and new-tenor illiquidity rather than a pure credit view that 20-year risk exceeds 30-year risk.
- [TAKE] Yields rising into geopolitical shock inverts the haven pattern and signals that supply plus foreign-duration selling is overpowering safety demand.
- [TAKE] Shifting issuance to 1-month to 6-month bills defers duration pain but transmits any Fed hike to interest expense within months through constant refinancing.
- [TAKE] Three strong auctions in five sessions with long yields still higher means clearing mechanics are fine while marginal price discovery keeps demanding more compensation.
