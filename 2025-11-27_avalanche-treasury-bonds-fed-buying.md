# An Avalanche of Treasury Bonds Hit the Market - Why is the Fed Buying so Many Bonds?

## 1. Thesis

A $183B three-auction week split cleanly by maturity: the $69B 2-year cleared on the screws with the strongest cover since August, while the $70B 5-year and $44B 7-year both tailed with soft foreign bidding and heavier dealer takes. The Fed's ~$21B combined add-on is reserve-management reinvestment inside a still-shrinking balance sheet, not QE — and the more telling split is collateral scarcity pulling bond prices up at the same time dollar scarcity pushes repo rates over the ceiling.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] [01:41] The short end still clears cleanly because 2-year pricing keys off the overnight policy path the market thinks it understands.
- [TAKE] [02:48] A rising dealer take with falling indirects on 5s and 7s means the buyer of last resort is absorbing what foreigners skip.
- [TAKE] [04:58] Fed add-ons are duration-matching reinvestment to avoid over-tightening, which is mechanics rather than renewed QE while the balance sheet shrinks.
- [TAKE] [06:08] Bonds can rally on collateral scarcity while dollars stay scarce in repo, so falling yields and over-ceiling SOFR describe two different shortages.
- [TAKE] [06:54] A bull steepener into cuts with the 30-year above its at-first-cut level keeps the abnormal post-cut selloff pattern intact.
