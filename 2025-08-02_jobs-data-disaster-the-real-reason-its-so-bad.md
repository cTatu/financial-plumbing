# Jobs Data Disaster - The Real Reason it's So Bad

## 1. Thesis

Trump fired the BLS commissioner claiming the weak July report was manipulated, but the real story is methodology, not malice: the monthly payrolls print is a noisy estimate from a voluntary survey covering 26% of establishments with only ~73% responding by deadline, modeled through a birth-death adjustment that misses turning points. Both administrations show large two-way revisions — 258,000 cut under Trump this month, 110,000 and 149,000 cuts plus a 93,000 upward revision under Biden — and the 2024 annual benchmark missed by 4x the 10-year average. Headline U3 at 4.2% remains very low versus 45 years of history, so the honest fix is less-frequent but complete data, with real-time ADP payrolls as the better high-frequency cross-check.

## 2. Key Claims

- [00:02] July added only 73,000 jobs, far below expectations with massive prior-month downward revisions, and Trump responded by firing the BLS commissioner for alleged manipulation. (as of Aug 2025 BLS)

- [07:59] The CES surveys about 120,000 businesses and government agencies covering about 630,000 worksites, only 26% of US establishments. (as of 2025 BLS)
- [09:11] The first monthly print rests on about a 73% response rate (68% when the BLS methodology video was made), with late responses concentrated in small businesses. (as of Jul 2025 BLS)
- [04:05] Recent Trump-administration revisions ran 258,000 lower combined, then 16,000 higher, 95,000 lower, 58,000 lower, and 48,000 lower across successive months. (as of 2025 BLS)
- [04:46] Biden-era revisions ran 49,000 lower, 110,000 lower, 93,000 higher, and 149,000 lower, with every 2020-2023 print revised lower in sequence. (as of 2023-2024 BLS)
- [13:13] The 2024 annual benchmark revision was 0.4% off versus a 0.1% 10-year average, or four times the normal miss. (as of 2024 BLS benchmark)
- [14:20] The absolute March benchmark revision overestimated by 220,000 jobs versus a 62,000 long-run average. (as of 2024 BLS benchmark)
- [12:03] The birth-death model imputes firm births and deaths from historical trends with a five-year lookback rather than real-time data, missing sudden economic changes. (as of 2025 BLS)
- [18:53] ADP, the largest US payroll processor with data since 2010, printed a 23,000-job contraction last month as a more direct real-time read. (as of Jul 2025 ADP)

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] [07:46] Reporting the monthly print as absolute certainty misleads because the first release is an estimate built on partial responses.
- [TAKE] [08:29] A 26% establishment sample cannot represent the whole economy, like two basin weather stations forecasting LA mountains and desert.
- [TAKE] [09:27] Voluntary response with small businesses answering late means revisions systematically reflect the firms missed in the first print.
- [TAKE] [15:03] The birth-death model fails most exactly when it matters, imputing from a five-year lookback through turning points like the post-2020 gig-work surge.
- [TAKE] [22:13] Large two-way revisions under both administrations point to gross incompetence in collection and modeling rather than a grand manipulation conspiracy.
