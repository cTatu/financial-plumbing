# Trump Trade Deal with China - Inflation & Treasury Report Released, and More Issues with Bond Market

## 1. Thesis

He made this video to argue the China headlines changed little: the S&P rally back above Liberation Day pricing is overbought hope on an agreement-to-talk while the effective tariff still sits near 18.5% versus 2% for decades, and the bond market is already voting fiscal, not inflation. The dollar is still 2.5% weaker, oil is 11.5% weaker, breakevens are lower, and the 10-year is back at 4.50% because the real yield rose 37bp. With the deficit 23% wider than 2024 and a $4T debt-ceiling ask in Congress, the message is that long-end pressure now disciplines trade and fiscal policy alike.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] [06:13] The 10-year is higher because the inflation-adjusted real yield rose 37bp while breakevens fell, so the market charges fiscal and duration premium rather than tariff inflation.
- [TAKE] [13:06] More 30-year supply to fund a wider deficit against flat-to-falling foreign demand from capital-surplus targeting means structurally higher long-end rates.
- [TAKE] [15:54] Tariffs cannot close the twin-deficit gap without currency adjustment, since China offsets duties and restructuring either economy takes many years rather than weeks.
- [TAKE] [04:42] The S&P pricing above Liberation Day on no signed deals with an 18.5% tariff wall still in place is overbought hope that overshoots fundamentals in both directions.
- [TAKE] [21:43] The dollar staying 2.5% weaker while stocks recover makes it the cleanest scorecard for whether reindustrialization policy actually succeeds.
