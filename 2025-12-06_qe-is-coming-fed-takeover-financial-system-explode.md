# QE Is Coming: The Fed's Takeover of the Financial System (And Why It Might Explode)

## 1. Thesis

Private banks stopped creating credit after 2008 as regulation and IORB incentivized reserve hoarding, breaking the link between private credit and GDP and forcing the Fed's base money to fill a $40T gap. With the TGA drained up by $600B, SOFR spreads signaling scarce reserves, and Japan's rising yields threatening to reverse a yen-carry bid worth trillions, funding stress can no longer be eased by rate cuts. The way out is reserve-management bill buying — functionally QE without the name — which monetizes bill-heavy deficits through banks that lever reserves into new dollars and blows out asset prices rather than restoring lending.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] Private banks withdrew after 2008 because GFC scarring plus Basel III, Dodd-Frank, and IORB made hoarding reserves more attractive than lending, so base money replaced private credit and the GDP correlation broke.
- [TAKE] Bill finance is quasi-monetization because banks buy leverable low-volatility bills and create new dollars against them, while insurers and pensions buying long bonds cannot create money.
- [TAKE] The TGA rebuild from $300B to $900B plus shrinking reserves flipped the ample-minus-SRF gauge negative, so SOFR pressure reflects scarce reserves rather than a quarter-end blip.
- [TAKE] A narrowing US-Japan rate gap plus rising JGB yields makes the yen-carry funding of trillions of US asset purchases less attractive and adds a second liquidity drain.
- [TAKE] Rate cuts cannot fix SOFR spreads because only reserve creation adds cash to the plumbing, which forces functionally-identical-to-QE bill buying even with positive policy rates.
