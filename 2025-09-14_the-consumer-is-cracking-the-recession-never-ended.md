# The Consumer is Cracking - The Recession Never Ended

## 1. Thesis

Headline 4.3% unemployment hides a K-shaped consumer break: sentiment has detached from GDP since 2021, job-loss fear sits at recession levels, and purchasing power keeps eroding even as nominal wages, savings, and home values print highs. Real spending grows only 0.4% on a six-month basis, credit growth funds the gap at 22.5% card rates, and hours needed to cover yearly interest jumped from 60 to 104 — a level seen only before 2001 and 2008 — while wages fell from 87% to 44% of M2 and from 0.06 to 0.01 ounces of gold per hour.

- [06:45] Revolving credit grew 9.6% month over month with six- and 12-month averages near 3.0% while card rates run about 22.5%. (as of Sep 2025 Fed)
- [07:51] Money-market balances hit a $7.5 trillion all-time high near $7.0 trillion ex-RRP, growing 14.2% per year. (as of Sep 2025 Fed)

- [16:53] Hours of average wages needed to pay yearly interest rose from 60.0 to 104.0, versus about 110.0 before 2001 and 2008 and a 114.0 to 115.0 peak recently. (as of 2024 data)

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] [03:33] Sentiment detached from GDP after 2021 because the average experience diverged from aggregates, so headline growth no longer predicts how consumers feel or spend.
- [TAKE] [11:04] U3 understates slack because Black, youth, and U6 rates deteriorate first, so marginal groups lead the headline cycle.
- [TAKE] [13:24] Nominal wage records mislead because wages halved against M2 and lost 80% in gold, so purchasing power fell while dollar pay rose.
- [TAKE] [16:53] Interest burden signals retrenchment risk because 104 hours matches pre-2001 and pre-2008 stress, when squeezed households cut spending and defaults rose.
- [TAKE] [09:23] Home-equity tapping extends the cycle because flat $35.7T equity with rising $271B revolving balances shows tapped-out owners borrowing against debasement gains.

## 4C Predicts

- [PREDICTS] Charge-offs rise through a downturn rather than peaking early, so continued elevation without a spike means stress is early-stage. -> check: Fed charge-off rate higher over next two quarters with delinquencies rising; rate falling back toward post-2010 lows refutes it.
- [PREDICTS] Real spending keeps fading while credit funds the gap until hours-to-pay-interest forces pullback. -> check: real PCE six-month under 1% with revolving balances rising; real PCE reaccelerating above 2% with credit slowing refutes it.
