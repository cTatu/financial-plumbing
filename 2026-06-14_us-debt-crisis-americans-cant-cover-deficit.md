# The US Debt Crisis: Americans Can't Cover the Deficit - So Who Does?

## 1. Thesis

The deficit is not a future risk but a present funding identity: at $293B in May alone and $1.6T on a trailing basis against 1.6% real growth, Medicare, Medicaid, and interest compounding at 8-12% force the Treasury to sell the gap to someone every month. With revenue flat as a GDP share for 75 years while spending ratchets from 27% to 38% of the economy, a shrinking trade deficit means less foreign surplus to absorb the 5%-of-GDP fiscal deficit — so the burden shifts onto domestic private savings via higher rates, negative front-end real yields, and an inflation tax that debases the 7,000 hours of work each worker owes for their share of $39T in debt.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] Debt crises are growth crises because Medicare, Medicaid, and interest compounding at 8-12% against 1.6% real growth widen the deficit by arithmetic, not politics.
- [TAKE] The deficit is net new debt that someone must fund from excess savings, so the sectoral identity of 5% fiscal deficit equals 3% private surplus plus 2% foreign surplus determines who pays.
- [TAKE] Revenue holds a flat GDP share over 75 years while spending climbs from 27% to 38%, so the gap is a spending-growth problem amplified by a pro-cyclical 5% deficit at 4.3% unemployment.
- [TAKE] A narrowing trade deficit helps growth optics but hurts funding because less foreign surplus forces more of the 5% fiscal deficit onto domestic savers.
- [TAKE] The exit is negative front-end real rates because cutting Fed funds below 4.2% CPI while issuing in bills lets the Treasury inflate away debt at bondholders' expense.
