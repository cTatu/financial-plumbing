# Breaking: Bond Yields Soar on Surprise Announcement & ADP Report Shows Labor Rebound

## 1. Thesis

Bonds sold off because the November quarterly refunding announcement broke the market's bill-heavy consensus: Treasury flagged possible 2026 increases in nominal coupon and FRN auction sizes to fund a 7%-of-GDP pro-cyclical deficit, repricing duration supply just as a hawkish Powell had already lifted the long end above the Fed funds rate. A firmer-than-expected ADP print (+42k) added a growth excuse, but the move — 5-7bp across 2s/5s/7s/30s — is framed as supply and policy-path repricing, not a labor breakout, with activist bill issuance (23% vs 20% TBAC target) now colliding with its money-like inflation limits.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] The sentence flagging 2026 coupon and FRN increases is what sold bonds because the market had priced continued bill-heavy activist issuance.
- [TAKE] Bills are cash-like and leverable into new dollars, so funding the deficit short is stimulatory in a way 10-, 20- and 30-year bonds are not.
- [TAKE] A 7%-of-GDP deficit at flat unemployment is pro-cyclical and historically only seen in recession, war or catastrophe.
- [TAKE] Powell's hawkish cut — 25bp with fewer future cuts signaled — had already bottomed yields, and the QRA extended that repricing.
- [TAKE] With the shutdown blocking official data, the bond market overweights the ADP beat because it is the only labor print available.
