# Middle Class is Extinct: 70% of America is Below the Poverty Line - What Happened?

## 1. Thesis

Applying the prior video's $136,000 true-minimum line to the income distribution leaves roughly 70% of households below it, so the middle class reads as extinct rather than squeezed. New cuts show the break starting around 1990-2000: home prices outran wage purchasing power by 54% in six years and 176% since 2007, personal income fell $99T short of its 1957-1990 trend, and financial claims plus top wealth compounded faster than GDP while labor's share fell — leaving homeowners and asset holders carried by the denominator while wage-only households pay market prices.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] [01:42] Homes are 84% cheaper in gold, so the affordability crisis is a dollar-denominator failure rather than houses gaining real value.
- [TAKE] [06:34] Real output, velocity, participation, and income purchasing power all broke around 2000 and were replaced by debt, profits, and financial-asset growth.
- [TAKE] [07:20] Wage share falling from 40% to 32% while top-1% net worth doubles as a GDP share is the distributional signature of that break.
- [TAKE] [07:41] Subsidies cushion the bottom and wealth insulates the top, so the middle pays market price and ends poorer than the lower bracket after cliffs.
- [TAKE] [08:52] Stocks compounding 7x wages and GDP 3.8x wages explains why record aggregates coexist with a 70% below-the-line population.
