# Tariffs Bite Canadian Economy - Will They Dump US Treasuries Again?

## 1. Thesis

US tariffs are visibly biting Canada into a twin-deficit bind: monthly exports collapsed from C$74 billion to C$61 billion, the annualized trade deficit hit a record C$87 billion, and quarterly GDP printed a 1.6% contraction while youth unemployment runs at 14.6%. The irony is structural — Canada holds about $438 billion of US Treasuries as the fifth-largest creditor, the exact type of twin-deficit holder that must sell reserves to fund its own government when the current account blows out. Just as in April's Liberation Day episode, when Canada cut $56 billion from $426 billion to $370 billion in a month, tariff pressure that widens the deficit is also what forces the Treasury sales that push US yields higher.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] [02:35] A twin deficit forces reserve sales because funding medical, social, and government payments requires constant foreign capital once imports exceed exports.
- [TAKE] [09:33] Canada makes the worst kind of Treasury holder because twin-deficit debtors sell what they can rather than what they want when their own funding strains.
- [TAKE] [26:02] Tariff irony is mechanical: US duties widen Canada's current-account gap, and the wider gap is what forces liquidation of US Treasuries into higher US yields.
- [TAKE] [11:28] GDP per capita falls 1.4% because 3.7% population growth expands the denominator faster than output, masking contraction behind headcount.
- [TAKE] [22:52] Canadian wages and home prices sit at all-time lows in gold and M2 terms, so the silent-depression arithmetic applies north of the border too.
