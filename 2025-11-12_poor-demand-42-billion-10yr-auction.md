# Poor Demand at Today's $42 Billion 10yr Bond Auction

## 1. Thesis

Duration is repricing fiscal risk while the front end still prices cuts: the first long-duration auction since the $2,000 tariff-rebate float tailed 0.6bp with 2.43 cover, the second tail in a row and second-weakest cover in 13-14 auctions, as primary dealers absorbed the most since August and foreign share fell well below its 70.2% average. Set against a strong $58 billion 3-year two days earlier, the split confirms the curve logic — 1-year and 2-year yields lead the Fed lower while the 10-year and 30-year charge a fiscal plus nominal-growth premium — so extra $350 billion rebate supply would steepen the curve further just as Rick Rieder floats QE to cap the same long end.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] A tail taxes the taxpayer because Treasury must pay a higher yield than the when-issued market to clear $42B of supply.
- [TAKE] Longer duration punishes inflation expectations disproportionately, which explains why the 10-year tails while the 3-year stops through in the same week.
- [TAKE] Front-end yields lead the Fed with a multi-month lag in both cuts and hikes, so strong 3-year demand reads as cut pricing rather than fiscal comfort.
- [TAKE] Foreign current-account-surplus buyers are preferred strong hands over 100-to-1 levered Cayman hedge funds because forced unwinds turn funding stress into Treasury selling.
- [TAKE] The Fed lacks control of the long end because a 150bp funds cut coincided with higher 10-year and 30-year yields, which motivates Rieder's QE prescription.
