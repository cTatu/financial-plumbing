# Moody's Downgrades US Credit Rating - Looming National Debt Crisis?

## 1. Thesis

He made this video to use the Moody's downgrade to AA as the occasion for a full balance-sheet diagnosis: receipts have held near 17% of GDP while spending climbed to 23%, so outlays compounding at 3,350% since 1971 versus receipts at 2,200% produce a 7%-of-GDP peacetime deficit with interest at 23-25% of revenue — past the 20% line the IMF and World Bank literature treats as an emerging-market distress marker. With debt productivity collapsing since the early 1980s and the curve now bear-steepening on fiscal fear rather than bull-steepening on Fed-cut hopes, the downgrade is framed as arithmetic catching up rather than a single agency's opinion.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] [02:16] The level of $37T debt matters less than its path and servicing capacity, with debt growing three to four times faster than GDP and interest twice as fast.
- [TAKE] [08:21] The deficit comes from spending outrunning a roughly constant 17% receipts share, since outlays at 3,350% since 1971 beat receipts at 2,200%.
- [TAKE] [13:11] Each borrowed dollar buys less growth as debt-to-GDP rises while a larger government share drags about 0.1% of GDP per point, so issuance and expansion both suppress income.
- [TAKE] [17:26] Interest at 23-25% of revenue past the 20% emerging-market line means the constraint binds on actual collections, not on potential GDP, and cannot be taxed away without shrinking GDP.
- [TAKE] [26:08] A bear steepener with rising real yields differs from 2000 and 2008 bull steepeners because long bonds sell fastest on fiscal and inflation fear rather than rallying on expected Fed cuts.
