# Did the Fed Just Crash the Dollar?! — Currency Intervention Soon? Mar-a-Lago Accord?

## 1. Thesis

Friday's dollar collapse was not just yen strength but a possible coordinated intervention signal: after the Bank of Japan held at 0.75% into a JGB meltdown and 160 USD/JPY, a ~1% five-minute yen spike flagged a BoJ rate check, then a second leg lower into the New York session followed Financial Times / Brent Donnelly reporting of a New York Fed rate check done at Treasury direction. The mechanics matter because yen intervention is funded by selling dollar reserves — chiefly Treasuries — so it pressures US long-end yields even as the dollar falls, and with DXY near 97 still overvalued on PPP versus 1985 Plaza levels, the episode reads as early innings of a Mar-a-Lago-style reordering toward reindustrialization rather than a one-day FX squall.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] A rate check lets a central bank move FX without spending reserves because dealers front-run the quoted $10B yen-buy and spread the rumor themselves.
- [TAKE] Currency policy is set by the US Treasury and executed by the New York Fed desk, so a Fed rate check implies Treasury direction, likely via the Exchange Stabilization Fund.
- [TAKE] Yen intervention is inherently US-duration-negative because Japan must first sell Treasuries to raise dollars before selling dollars to buy yen.
- [TAKE] The joint stock-down, bond-down, dollar-down price action extends the sell-America regime where geopolitical stress no longer produces a haven dollar bid.
- [TAKE] DXY near 97 being fairly-valued would require much more downside because PPP still shows overvaluation versus the 1985 extreme.
