# Jobless Claims Hit Near-Record Low, $22 Billion in US Debt Sold, Silver SOARS

## 1. Thesis

The first 30-year auction since the DOJ-Powell probe stopped through 0.8bp at 4.825% with 66.8% indirect takedown and only 12% to dealers — the largest stop-through since early 2025 — extending a 10-day streak of strong auctions that defies the fiscal-dominance boycott narrative. Simultaneously the labor market printed near-record tightness on initial claims (198K versus 215K expected, all-time low per labor-force member) even as continuing claims sat flat at 1.8M, while silver refused to sell off (8% intraday range resolving back above $91) on an $11-13 Shanghai premium that has tracked every leg higher since October 2025. The bond market's message was hawkish anyway: 3-month through 2-year yields rose and flattened the 30Y-2Y spread as January 28 cut odds sat at just 5%.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] A record stop-through with two-thirds indirect takedown days after a DOJ probe of the Fed chair shows foreign buyers discounting institutional risk rather than boycotting duration.
- [TAKE] Record-low initial claims alongside flat 1.8M continuing claims means layoffs are historically rare while hiring remains stagnant, a low-churn freeze rather than a layoff wave.
- [TAKE] Short-end yields rising into a 5%-cut-odds meeting while the long end holds near 4.8% is the bond market pricing out cuts, which flattens the curve against the steepener thesis near term.
- [TAKE] Silver absorbing a 5-8% flush and reclaiming highs with an $11 Shanghai premium intact is textbook cannot-sell-off bullishness led by Eastern physical demand.
- [TAKE] US long-end underperformance versus France, the UK, and Japan traces to a decade-plus of QE beach-ball suppression now releasing globally rather than a US-only fiscal story.
