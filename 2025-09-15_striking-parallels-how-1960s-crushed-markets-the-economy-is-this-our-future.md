# Striking Parallels: How 1960s Crushed Markets & the Economy — Is This Our Future?

## 1. Thesis

Today rhymes with 1965-1982: populism plus Vietnam/Great Society spending plus supply shocks plus a loose Burns Fed turned 1.5% inflation into 14.5% with 7% average inflation, drove 3-month yields from 1.1% to 16%, crushed bonds through an up-and-to-the-right 10-year, and left the S&P sideways in dollars and down about 80% in gold while gold returned 706%. With a 7% procyclical deficit, 3% inflation, all-time-high assets, and talk of cuts echoing Burns, the analog warns of a bond bear market, weak real growth, a softer dollar, and scarcity assets outperforming for a decade or more.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] Cash at 4.5% in money funds misleads because inflation-adjusted T-bill returns were deeply negative through the 1970s artifact of loose policy.
- [TAKE] The long end answers nominal growth rather than the Fed because the 10-year rose while the funds rate fell, both in the 1960s analog and since September 2024.
- [TAKE] Deficits plus deglobalization plus immigration restriction create structural inflation that AI productivity must overcome before real returns recover.
- [TAKE] Reserve-link abandonment amplifies debasement because leaving gold in 1971 let spending widen without convertibility discipline, the condition gold priced next.
- [TAKE] Cutting with equities, homes, and gold at highs with 3% inflation repeats the Burns error of accommodation into asset and price pressure.
