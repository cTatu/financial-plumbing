# JUST IN: Fiscal Deficit is Surging - Is the US Headed for a Debt Crisis?

## 1. Thesis

The July Monthly Treasury Statement shows a $291 billion monthly deficit on $629 billion of spending, putting the fiscal year on pace for a $1.88 trillion gap funded by $1.9 trillion of new debt — a 7-7.5% procyclical deficit at low unemployment that is 6x the pre-GFC share. The structure is entitlements plus interest: Social Security at 32% of revenue and Medicare at 24% together absorb half of all receipts, while interest at $1 trillion fiscal-year-to-date toward $1.2 trillion runs 20% of revenue and 3.5-4% of GDP. Tariff revenue at $28 billion in July and $135 billion fiscal-year-to-date annualizes to about $300 billion, enough in theory to zero the bottom 75% of income tax but not to close the gap, and with the 30-year at 4.9% now above 4.7% nominal growth the Treasury is pushed toward bill-heavy funding that is pro-liquidity, inflationary, and dollar-negative.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] [02:40] The 7-7.5% deficit at a lower unemployment rate than pre-2008 is procyclical and 6x larger as a GDP share than the last expansion.
- [TAKE] [04:13] Social Security, Medicare, and fast-growing interest drive the gap while defense grew slower than revenue and GDP even with black-budget inclusion.
- [TAKE] [13:49] Individuals pay more income tax than last year while corporate receipts fall, shifting the funding burden onto households and equity-driven receipts.
- [TAKE] [14:12] Tariff pass-through to consumers is minimal so far because US importers and foreign suppliers absorb the duty via margins, with demand destruction likely if pushed further.
- [TAKE] [22:52] Funding in 1-to-6-month bills to chase negative real rates is pro-liquidity and inflationary because bills are cash-like and rehypothecate.
