# PCE Inflation - Higher than Expected

## 1. Thesis

June PCE printed hot — core 2.8% versus 2.7% expected with headline 2.6% versus 2.5% — confirming the call that inflation bottomed after 2022 and set a higher low near 2.2-2.3% before reaccelerating. Headline is flattered by falling oil while bond-market breakevens rise against oil since September 2024, wage pressure builds as immigration restriction shrinks labor supply, and the long end keeps bear-steepening even as the Fed cut 100 basis points. The layer-deeper story matches the labor data: headline personal income rises while M2-deflated real and real-disposable income deteriorate, leaving roughly a $10 trillion gap to the nightly-news number.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] [02:52] Headline income misleads because PCE-deflated real income and M2-deflated purchasing power both deteriorate underneath the up-and-to-the-right nominal line.
- [TAKE] [04:19] Shrinking labor supply through restrictive immigration lifts wages by supply and demand, so the wage is the release valve and the employment cost index reaccelerates.
- [TAKE] [06:03] Powell sounded more hawkish than expected because he had already seen the hot PCE print the market had not yet seen.
- [TAKE] [06:59] Core matters more than headline because a 5% one-day oil move on an OPEC cut whips headline while core tracks the persistent trend near 3%.
- [TAKE] [14:53] The post-cut bear steepener shows bond displeasure with monetary-plus-fiscal direction because the Fed controls only overnight to 1-year rates while 10-year to 30-year yields price inflation and solvency risk.
