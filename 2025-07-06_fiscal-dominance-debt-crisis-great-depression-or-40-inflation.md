# Fiscal Dominance: Debt Crisis to Cause a Great Depression or 40% Inflation — Already There?

## 1. Thesis

Walking through Charles Calamis's Q4-2023 St. Louis Fed paper line by line, the argument is that America has reached the fiscal-dominance ceiling where deficits and debt strip the Fed of inflation control: debt is projected toward 566% of GDP by 2097 on current policy, the 10-year real yield has risen from 0.70% at writing to about 2% that the paper flags as the trigger, and interest already absorbs 23% of receipts with debt near 120% of GDP. Because only $2.39T of currency is non-interest-bearing against $3.20T of interest-paying reserves, the paper's arithmetic says funding the gap requires either forcing banks onto zero-interest reserves — a 1930s-style deflationary banking shock — or accepting roughly 8% to 41% sustained inflation, and the Fed's $233B loss plus 15%-of-GDP Treasury holdings say monetization has already started.

- [04:00] Interest-bearing federal debt totals $36.00 trillion against $21.90 trillion of M2, a $5.60 trillion monetary base, and only $2.39 trillion of non-interest-bearing currency. (as of 2025)
- [04:52] Gold and the monetary base each rose about 8,000% since the gold standard while CPI rose only 834% over the same span. (as of 1971-2025)
- [06:50] Market value of Treasury debt totals $26.70 trillion and fell from 108% of GDP in 2020 to about 85% of GDP on post-2021 inflation surprise. (as of 2020-2025)
- [15:05] CBO-style projections imply 200% debt-to-GDP by 2050 even assuming 40-to-60-basis-point real yields versus 200 basis points today with deficits above 5% of GDP. (as of 2023 paper assumptions)
- [19:45] Fed holdings of government debt rose from 4% to 5% of GDP historically to 15% now while the balance sheet rose from 5% to 6% to 23% of GDP. (as of Jul 2025)
- [31:40] Marketable-debt interest totals $1.11 trillion per year, equal to 23% of tax receipts with debt-to-GDP near 120% versus 30% to 40% in the late 1970s. (as of 2025)
- [34:40] Interest on reserves at 4.40% on $3.20 trillion pays banks $141.00 billion per year while the Fed carries a $233.00 billion cumulative loss as a deferred asset. (as of 2025)

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] [02:30] Fiscal dominance means debt service displaces inflation control so the government funds deficits with the inflation tax rather than real surpluses.
- [TAKE] [07:40] Currency alone is too small a tax base at $2.39T, so lasting inflation taxation requires pulling $3.20T of reserves into zero-interest status.
- [TAKE] [12:20] Ending interest on reserves would crush bank profits and lending the way Mexico's 1970s-1980s nationalizations crushed allocation and growth.
- [TAKE] [26:20] Rising real yields near 2% are self-fulfilling because the inflation premium slows growth and forces still more inflation taxation later.
- [TAKE] [33:00] Paying 4.40% on reserves above bill yields sterilizes reserves and reveals the Fed already fears upside inflation risk.

## 7. Follow-ups

- Watch 10-year TIPS real yield against 2%: holding at or above 2% with debt-to-GDP rising confirms the trigger condition; falling back near 0.70% argues the scare was cyclical.
- Watch IORB policy with reserve-requirement language: keeping 4.40% on $3.20T with zero requirements confirms banks stay compensated and inflation runs hot; cutting to zero with a 40% requirement confirms the deflationary-bank-crisis path.
- Watch CPI-adjusted TLT with KRE about 50 weeks later: renewed real TLT lows followed by KRE breakdown confirms the balance-sheet contagion loop; both stabilizing together refutes it.
