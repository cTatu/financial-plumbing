# The US Economy Has a MAJOR Problem... and it's Getting Worse

## 1. Thesis

December payrolls added just 50K jobs versus 70K expected, with a three-month average of only 333 jobs per month and a nine-month average of 55K against a 170K-220K post-GFC norm, while October was cut further to negative 173K. The headline 4.4% unemployment rate hides a K-shaped breakdown where youth unemployment runs 15.7%, native-born employment lost ground after zero net gains from 2018-2024, and manufacturing's share of the labor force hit a new 7% low. The structural claim is that labor's share of GDP sits at an all-time low while corporate-profit share has doubled since 1990 and productivity is up 224% since 1964 against 21% for real wages, so record AI capex at the scale of all postwar megaprojects combined will widen the capital-labor split until participation, wages, and populist pressure break.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] Comparing today to 170K-220K post-GFC hiring is the right benchmark because 2021-2024 prints were distorted by fiscal, monetary, and immigration policy.
- [TAKE] Zero net jobs for native-born Americans from 2018-2024 with all gains to foreign-born workers reframes headline growth as compositional rather than broad.
- [TAKE] Persistent downward revisions back to January, as under the prior administration, mean first prints overstate strength and markets should trade revisions over headlines.
- [TAKE] Labor share at a new low with profit share doubled means GDP growth accrues to capital and equity holders rather than wage earners.
- [TAKE] Record AI capex at 1930s-public-works-plus-Manhattan-plus-Apollo-plus-Interstate-plus-broadband scale aims to replace humans, so productivity gains will further split output from per-capita pay.
