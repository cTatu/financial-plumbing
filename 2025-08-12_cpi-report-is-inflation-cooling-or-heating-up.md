# CPI Report: Is Inflation Cooling or Heating Up?

## 1. Thesis

July headline CPI at 2.7% looks cool only because falling oil suppresses it, while core at 3.1% and supercore reaccelerating off a bottom 3-4 months ago show sticky services and wage pressure underneath. The read is monetary plus labor-supply: roughly 40% of all dollars were created in the last five years leaving workers in a purchasing-power hole, and shrinking labor supply via restrictive immigration mechanically lifts the wage release valve in a services-heavy economy. With the 30-year at 4.90% now above nominal growth and 5-year breakevens rising as crude falls, the bond market prices persistent inflation that complicates Fed cuts.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] [03:13] Services drive the vast majority of headline and core while goods contribute only a sliver, so tariff goods pass-through is not the inflation story.
- [TAKE] [09:34] Shrinking labor supply via restrictive immigration mechanically lifts wages because demand is relatively constant and the wage is the release valve.
- [TAKE] [10:16] Official real wage gains dissolve once deflated by M2 and GDP because money and output outran hourly pay since the 1980s.
- [TAKE] [15:52] The post-cut bear steepener with long yields rising as the Fed cuts is abnormal for the US and prices fiscal dominance.
- [TAKE] [21:04] Five-year breakevens climbing while oil falls since April 2024 warn that bond-implied inflation diverges from headline disinflation.

## 7. Follow-ups

- Watch next 3 CPI prints with supercore and ECI: core holding above 3.1% with supercore rising confirms reacceleration, while core falling toward 2.5% with ECI cooling refutes it.
- Watch 5-year breakeven versus WTI: breakeven rising with oil near $71 and lower confirms bond-led inflation, while breakeven fading toward 2.1% says disinflation resumed.
- Watch 30Y against nominal growth with NFIB labor quality: 30Y holding above 4.90% with 21% labor-shortage share rising confirms wage-driven persistence, while 30Y falling with hiring ease refutes it.
