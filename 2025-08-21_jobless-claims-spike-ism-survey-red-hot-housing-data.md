# Jobless Claims Spike & ISM Survey Red Hot? + Housing Market Data

## 1. Thesis

One data dump captures both halves of the stagflationary squeeze: labor prints soften at the margin — 235K initial claims versus 224K expected with the 12-week average near post-2021 highs, and 1.97M continuing claims still climbing — while output and price surveys run hot, with S&P composite PMI at 55.4 (8-month high, ~2.5% real GDP) and manufacturing at 53.3 (39-month high) but selling prices rising at the sharpest pace since August 2022. The housing leg shows why cuts will not fix it: existing sales beat at +2.0% month-over-month yet sit near GFC lows while prices hold all-time highs, because the 30-year-based mortgage channel broke — $9K of average annual interest in 2020 is $27K today — and fiscal-dominance funding (bill-heavy, negative real rates) would steepen the bear curve further, so asset holders gain while wage purchasing power keeps falling.

- [TAKE] [22:04] Claims describe normalization rather than collapse because 1.95-million continuing claims sit near historical lows after the 2020-2021 disruption rather than at crisis highs.
- [TAKE] [07:20] ISMs lead the business cycle because PMI rollovers precede real-GDP turns by months and unemployment spikes by a further few months, as in 2004 and April 2007.
- [TAKE] [08:52] Nominal growth reaccelerates because tariff pass-through plus hot PMI and PPI lift both real output and inflation, matching the house base case held since May.
- [TAKE] [11:33] National home prices will not materially fall because bill-heavy fiscal dominance with negative real rates debases the dollar and pushes scarce-asset prices higher in nominal terms.
