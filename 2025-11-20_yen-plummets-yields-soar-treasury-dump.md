# Yen Plummets, Yields Soar- Will They Dump US Treasuries to Defend the Yen? Yen Carry Trade Unwind?

## 1. Thesis

Japan's 30-year yield jumping ~35bp since early November alongside a sliding yen has already bled into a soft $16B 20-year US auction, and the presenter frames two distinct US-negative transmissions: repatriation where higher JGB yields pull Japanese savings home by selling Treasuries then dollars to buy yen and JGBs, and a carry-trade unwind where yen shorts cover by selling Treasuries and tech stocks then buying yen. With USD/JPY near 157.50 toward a ~160 intervention line and $1.10T of Japan's $1.35T reserves in Treasuries, either path means higher US yields with a weaker dollar rather than the usual haven bid.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] Higher JGB yields trigger repatriation because Japanese capital that bought Treasuries for lack of home yield sells Treasuries for dollars, sells dollars for yen, then buys JGBs and settles at higher US yields with a lower dollar-yen and lower JGB yields.
- [TAKE] A narrower US-Japan premium directly shrinks carry appeal because 2.20% excess over JGBs pays far less than the prior 3.50% before currency-hedging costs turn the spread thinner still.
- [TAKE] Yen intervention is a Treasury-negative event because selling $1.10T-scale Treasury reserves to raise dollars for yen buying mechanically adds US duration supply while supporting yen.
- [TAKE] JGB losses recreate the 2023 US regional-bank trap because pension funds, insurers and banks holding long bonds face insolvency pressure if deposit-like claims force sales at depressed prices.
- [TAKE] Bond-market linkage is global rather than domestic because a weak clearing yield in Japan forces higher clearing yields in the US through both repatriation flows and carry-trade hedging math.
