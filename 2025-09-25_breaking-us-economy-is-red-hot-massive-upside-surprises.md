# Breaking: US Economy is Red Hot: Massive Upside Surprises = Stocks, Bonds Lower

## 1. Thesis

Q2 real GDP's third revision to 3.8% (from 2.8-2.9% to 3.3%) with Atlanta Fed Q3 near 3.25%, claims at 218K versus 235K, durable goods up 2.9% versus -0.4%, and a -$85 billion goods deficit versus -$95 billion all repriced cut expectations at once — the 10-year rose ~20bp to 4.20% and the 30-year ~12bp to 4.77% with the S&P off a third straight day because hot nominal growth near 5-5.5% lifts term rates while deflating the easy-money bid. The tariff front-run explains the -0.6% Q1 to +3.8% Q2 violent swing, and the open risk is that any genuine trade-deficit repair drains the capital inflows funding US assets.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] [02:45] The Q1-to-Q2 GDP swing is a tariff front-running artifact because surging imports subtract from GDP in Q1 and the reversal adds back in Q2.
- [TAKE] [12:44] Bonds sell off on hot prints because 10-year and 30-year yields track nominal-growth expectations near 5% to 5.5% rather than the Fed's cut alone.
- [TAKE] [15:45] Stocks fall on strong data because the rally was priced on rate-cut expectations, and fewer cuts remove the easy-money fuel.
- [TAKE] [13:20] Japan's rising yields show the benign version of the same logic, where real growth after 25 years of stagnation justifies higher rates without signaling crisis.
- [TAKE] [10:00] Genuine trade-deficit repair would mechanically shrink the capital inflows funding Treasuries, stocks, and real estate through the balance of payments.
