# 2019 Repo Rate Crisis: When Wall Street's Plumbing Broke - Explained

## 1. Thesis

The September 2019 repo spike was not a technical glitch but the predictable result of draining cash while flooding collateral: Fed hikes plus quantitative tightening shrank bank reserves, a 5%-plus deficit pushed ever more Treasuries into the funding market, and a September tax-season TGA surge pulled the last cash out — so the overnight rate ran from 2.3% to 10% intraday in two days until the Fed resumed billions-per-day repo injections, abandoning balance-sheet normalization and establishing the template for how QT plus large deficits plus tax-date drains reproduce funding stress today.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] [02:57] Repo is a cash-versus-collateral supply-demand market, so collateral abundance plus cash shortage raises the overnight price.
- [TAKE] [09:13] Hikes plus QT plus a September TGA tax drain removed cash from three directions at once into the seizure.
- [TAKE] [15:56] A 5.00%-plus deficit is a collateral pump because every deficit dollar must be sold as Treasuries used in repo.
- [TAKE] [20:34] Only large Fed cash injections rebalance the equation, which is why tens of billions per day brought the rate back down.
- [TAKE] [22:59] Without that backstop, a 2.30%-to-10.00% funding-cost shock in two days would have halted credit creation system-wide.
