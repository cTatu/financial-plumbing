# Surprise CPI Inflation Report - What's Going On & Where It's Going

## 1. Thesis

June CPI printed hot at 2.7% year over year with core at 2.9%, and on this read inflation bottomed near 2.2-2.3% back in April and now reaccelerates. The call rests on the monetary side rather than any single basket item: M2 sits at a $22 trillion all-time high against a $5 trillion base, the TGA has been drawn from about $950 billion to about $320 billion since April 2024, and QT is ending — all pro-liquidity and stimulatory. Meanwhile sticky services reflect workers still clawing back the post-2020 purchasing-power collapse, and the energy drag that suppressed headline (gasoline down 8.3%) fades as crude bounces about 30% off its May low. The administration's stated answer — grow nominally with roughly 300 basis points of cuts funded in short bills via a shadow Fed — is fiscal dominance that steepens the curve, pressures the dollar, and represses bondholders while scarce assets absorb the debasement.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] [04:58] Inflation is a monetary phenomenon first, so M2 at $22 trillion with TGA and RRP drawdowns and QT ending explains the reacceleration call better than any single basket item.
- [TAKE] [02:01] CPI construction with substitution and owners-equivalent rent understates lived inflation, so the cumulative loss of dollar purchasing power is the honest metric.
- [TAKE] [13:08] Services stay sticky because workers never recouped the post-2020 purchasing-power collapse and keep demanding catch-up wage gains.
- [TAKE] [24:00] Cutting the funds rate by 300 basis points and funding in 1-to-6-month bills ends Fed independence because any future hike would instantly reprice the rolled debt.
- [TAKE] [31:36] Bills are more pro-liquidity than bonds because low-volatility paper levers and rehypothecates into more dollars, so all-bill funding steepens the long end.
