# Dollar Bazooka Fired: Massive US Bailout Announced for Argentina - Bailout Explained

## 1. Thesis

Bessent fired the "dollar bazooka" one day after the mechanics explainer: the Treasury will buy Argentine dollar bonds in primary and secondary markets plus extend standby credit through the ~$220 billion ESF, while negotiating a $20 billion central-bank swap line — four times China's $5 billion facility. The package targets a textbook dollar-short squeeze after bonds collapsed to ~50 cents and the Merval lost ~50% in dollar terms, and it works through balance-sheet recycling of prior ESF profits, asset sales, and SDR certificates rather than new congressional debt or printed money short of default — critical because $280 billion of dollar-denominated external debt means every basis point of yield relief counts.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] [04:15] Bessent's repeated "tools to defeat speculators" language is deliberate financial warfare from a former currency trader engineering a short squeeze rather than generic reassurance.
- [TAKE] [18:32] ESF purchases of dollar bonds lift bond values and compress yields directly, which matters more than the loan size because every basis point reprices $280 billion of dollar debt.
- [TAKE] [13:50] The swap line is short-term dollar liquidity against peso collateral settled at the original exchange rate, so the Fed earns overnight-rate-plus-spread interest with no currency risk short of default.
- [TAKE] [10:45] The $20 billion package is balance-sheet recycling from prior ESF profits, asset sales, or SDR certificates credited by the Fed rather than new congressional debt or broad money printing.
- [TAKE] [18:06] Argentina is structurally short dollars, so peso weakness mechanically widens the debt burden and the rescue must break that reflexive loop to hold.
