# Red Hot Inflation Sends Stocks Lower - What if it's Even Worse?

## 1. Thesis

May CPI repriced risk because the shock is cost-push energy and services, not demand-pull: headline sits at a 3-year high with core at 2.9% while PPI runs near 6% and ISM prices-paid sit at 2022 highs, so firms must compress margins or destroy demand. Official CPI already trails actual per-capita outlays by 66% over 35 years and M2 by 174%, electricity is up 45% since 2020, and the WTI curve prices $77 for May 2027 — which is why stocks, duration, and the 2-30 spread sold together even as the market priced another hike into a supply shock.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] CPI measures the rate of change of a shifting BLS basket, so owner's-equivalent rent, substitution, and hedonics understate shelter and services pressure.
- [TAKE] Monetarist arithmetic drives the call because 6.6% average M2 growth dwarfs wage growth and the 2021 27% surge still compounds into the price level.
- [TAKE] Energy plus core services carry headline inflation while tariffs explain little, since core-goods inflation sits far below 2021-2023 and just printed month-over-month deflation.
- [TAKE] PPI running near 6% forces a binary outcome of margin compression if firms absorb costs or demand destruction if households cannot pay higher prices.
- [TAKE] Pricing another hike into a cost-push oil disruption repeats the June payrolls error because higher front-end rates cannot produce more barrels or kilowatt-hours.

## 4C Predicts

- [PREDICTS] PMI prices-paid strength passes through PPI near 6% into higher CPI over 1-2 months. -> check: next PPI then CPI; PPI rolling over with stable CPI invalidates.
- [PREDICTS] Firms either compress margins or trigger demand destruction as the PPI-minus-CPI spread blows out. -> check: next earnings margins versus real PCE; stable margins with firm spending refutes.
- [PREDICTS] The WTI curve keeps pricing higher-for-longer near $77 for May 2027 if war escalation persists. -> check: WTI forwards over next month; fall back toward $65 on ceasefire refutes.
