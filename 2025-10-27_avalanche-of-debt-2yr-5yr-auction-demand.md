# An Avalanche of US Debt - What's the Demand?

## 1. Thesis

The $139 billion two-auction day cleared respectably but unevenly: the $69 billion 2-year stopped only 0.1bp through the tail side with record 34.77% directs covering for indirects sliding to 53.6%, the weakest foreign share since the March 2023 regional-bank crisis, while the $70 billion 5-year stopped 0.1bp through with 66.84% indirects, the best since May. Both drew B+ grades and fit a bull-flattening bid — the 2-year at 3.50% is the lowest since August 2022 and the 5-year at 3.62% the lowest since September 2024 — yet the split reads as domestic absorption plus Fed-cut front-running alongside softening foreign sponsorship, with the 2-year plumbing lower lows while the 5-year holds higher lows into concurrent SOFR and funds-market strain.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] A 0.1bp tail after 9-10 stop-throughs is a softening signal rather than a failed auction because the Treasury still paid a price concession to clear $69 billion.
- [TAKE] Record 34.77% directs offset 53.6% indirects mechanically because domestic bidders absorb duration that foreign and institutional buyers leave behind.
- [TAKE] The 5-year 66.84% indirect rebound inverts the 2-year foreign fade because belly demand held above its 64.2% average for the first stop-through since May.
- [TAKE] The 2-year near 3.50% tracks expected Fed cuts because the front end prices the near-term policy path while the 5-year prices growth plus accumulated short rates.
- [TAKE] Bonds bid across the curve into a bull flattener on lower growth plus cut expectations just as SOFR and funds spreads flag concurrent dollar-liquidity strain.
