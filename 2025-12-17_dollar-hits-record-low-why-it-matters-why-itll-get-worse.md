# Dollar Hits Record Low - Here's Why it Matters & Why It'll Get Worse

## 1. Thesis

The dollar's record low is not euro strength but dollar debasement, visible only once prices are measured in the right denominator — gold, Swiss francs, the S&P, houses — where wages buy less every year. The twin fiscal-plus-current-account deficits require roughly $1 trillion per year of foreign financing, and the old recycling loop where surplus dollars were reinvested into Treasuries is breaking as surplus holders sell dollars to buy gold instead. Gold therefore acts as the pivot that revalues currencies by balance of payments, which devalues the dollar over decades and makes further downside the base case rather than a one-off weak patch.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] [01:50] The dollar and the euro tell two sides of the same trade, so a rising euro chart is arithmetically a falling dollar chart.
- [TAKE] [05:37] Record unaffordability reflects denominator failure because wages priced in dollars trailed the 150% rise in housing interest while homes themselves barely changed.
- [TAKE] [08:05] A $1 trillion per year current-account deficit ships $1 trillion of value abroad annually, which is unsustainable on a balance-of-payments basis.
- [TAKE] [09:35] The old loop suppressed US rates and supported the dollar because $400 billion of surplus dollars were reinvested into interest-bearing dollars instead of being sold.
- [TAKE] [12:44] Gold restores balance-of-payments valuation because surplus dollars sold for gold lift gold in dollar terms while yuan gold demand stays flat, resetting the dollar-yuan cross.
