# France & UK Dumping US Treasury Bonds - Why?

## 1. Thesis

France and the UK are selling Treasuries not as a verdict on the US but because twin deficits plus stronger currencies plus rising energy costs force them to raise dollars for imports and interest bills. The UK pairs 1.3% growth with 3.8% inflation into stagflation while France pairs 0.9% growth with weak nominal growth, both with widening current-account gaps and early-stage debt pressure that tax hikes have not fixed — so UST sales are how a current-account deficit is financed, and a higher oil price or stronger pound/euro would accelerate it.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] Twin-deficit arithmetic forces the sale because a current-account deficit must be paid for by selling assets or borrowing, and Treasuries are the liquid asset on hand.
- [TAKE] UK stagflation plus gilt-rate pressure creates early debt-crisis dynamics where higher rates lift interest expense, widen the fiscal gap, and tax hikes shrink revenue through capital flight.
- [TAKE] China systematically weakens its currency to protect surplus while the UK and France let stronger currencies widen deficits, which explains opposite reserve outcomes.
- [TAKE] Rising oil hurts Europe disproportionately because imported energy must be paid in dollars while an economy cannot run on Treasuries, so oil up means UST sales.
