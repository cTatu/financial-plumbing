# $163 Billion US Debt Sale, Japanese Yields Soar, Repo Stress Continues & Venezuela Market Reaction

## 1. Thesis

First trading day after US military action in Venezuela: oil and metals rip higher while the dollar sells off instead of rallying as a safe haven — more evidence, on this read, that the "rules-based" reserve-currency bid is eroding. Underneath the headlines, funding stress persists (SOFR printed at the Fed's upper bound on Jan 2, past quarter-end), Japanese yields hit multi-decade highs that threaten capital repatriation out of Treasuries, and the long end keeps selling off despite Fed cuts — all consistent with a market repricing who will absorb $38T+ of US debt.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] The Jan 2 SOFR print at the upper bound, days past quarter-end, means stress is persistent funding tightness (willingness-to-lend, not just dollar abundance) — which is why the Fed restarted reserve-management bill buying.
- [TAKE] Soaring JGB yields trigger a repatriation chain — Japanese capital sells Treasuries (yields up), sells dollars for yen (dollar down) — so US long-end pressure is imported, not just domestic supply.
- [TAKE] Venezuela/Iran risk producing dollar *weakness* instead of a haven bid signals eroding confidence in the rules-based dollar order; a kidnapping-foreign-leaders regime is definitionally dollar-negative.
- [TAKE] The US-Japan rate-differential premium (once +3.5-4%) keeps eroding while USD/JPY stays firm — the correlation breakdown since Liberation Day marks regime change in the pair.
