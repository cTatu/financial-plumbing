# Consumer Debt Hits Record - When Everything Broke in the US Economy & Financial System

## 1. Thesis

Record $5 trillion-plus consumer credit with $571 billion of annual personal interest is not a late-cycle blip but the visible leg of a break that started in 1990 and completed in 2008: private banks that create 85-90% of dollars stopped extending credit on the old trend, leaving roughly $20 trillion of unrealized bank credit and $30 trillion of unrealized M2 that official M0 money (11% to 26% of M2) and a rise from 50% to 73% for M2-to-GDP papered over. With revolving credit reaccelerating at 9.7%, card rates at 22%, delinquency rising from 2.2% to 3%, and 107 hours of work a year now required just to pay interest — a level seen only before prior recessions — the consumer funds overconsumption with leverage because a twin-deficit nation can accumulate nothing but debt.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] Private banks create 85% to 90% of dollars by extending loans, so M2 reflects private credit creation while M0 reflects only Mint currency plus Fed reserves.
- [TAKE] The 1990 S&L and 2008 breaks left about $20 trillion of unrealized bank credit because private lenders never returned to the 1950-2008 trend growth.
- [TAKE] Real GDP follows private credit with a six-month lag at an almost one-to-one four-year correlation, so the post-2005 correlation breakdown foreshadowed weaker output.
- [TAKE] A twin-deficit nation must finance overconsumption with debt because fiscal plus current-account deficits leave debt as the only accumulable asset.
- [TAKE] Official M0 money rose from 11% to 26% of M2 while M2 rose from 50% to 73% of GDP because government money printing filled the private-credit gap.
