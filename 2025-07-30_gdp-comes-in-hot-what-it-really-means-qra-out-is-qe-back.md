# GDP Comes in Hot - What it Really Means + QRA Out, is QE Back?

## 1. Thesis

Q2 real GDP printing 3% versus 2.3% expected is mostly tariff arithmetic normalizing rather than a growth boom: Q1 was dragged negative by corporations front-running tariffs as imports surged toward $420 billion, and Q2 was flattered as imports fell back toward $350 billion for a record 5-point net-export contribution. What matters for debt sustainability is nominal growth versus borrowing cost, so the companion QRA — about $1 trillion of borrowing to rebuild the TGA toward $850 billion with steady coupons, slightly higher bills, and doubled buyback frequency — keeps long-end supply pressure on while bill-heavy fiscal dominance risks reaccelerating inflation and steepening the curve.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] The 3% print reflects normalization rather than strength because the trade balance round-tripped from about $72 billion to $138 billion and back to $71 billion on tariff front-running and reversal.
- [TAKE] Debt burden math turns on nominal growth versus borrowing cost, so joint reacceleration in real output and inflation eases the ratio only if long-end yields stay contained through the QRA supply wave.
- [TAKE] Cutting government spending improves fiscal health but subtracts from GDP by construction, which explains why prior state-heavy growth flattered prints while current restraint drags them.
- [TAKE] Holding long coupons steady while nudging bill issuance higher avoids immediate duration indigestion but concentrates funding in leverable short collateral that is pro-liquidity and potentially inflationary.
- [TAKE] Doubling long-end liquidity-support buybacks at $2 billion to $4 billion per operation is two orders below $50 billion to $100 billion QE, so the move signals sensitivity rather than a return to easing.

## 4C Predicts

- [PREDICTS] Nominal growth reaccelerates as real GDP and CPI both tick higher over the next 12 months. -> check: rising real GDP with CPI and 5-year breakevens higher into mid-2026 confirms; both rolling over refutes it.
- [PREDICTS] CPI reaccelerates on negative real front-end rates plus heavier bill supply. -> check: CPI moving up from 2.4% with bills pricing negative real yields confirms; CPI falling toward 2% refutes it.
- [PREDICTS] Formal QE returns within 9 to 12 months, while the current buyback doubling is not QE. -> check: Fed balance-sheet expansion or large-scale coupon buying announced by mid-2026 confirms; continued QT with only small buybacks refutes early return.

- Watch 10-year to 30-year yields with coupon sizes steady and bills higher: long yields rising on $1 trillion supply confirms demand-shortage thesis, while long yields falling says supply was pre-priced.
