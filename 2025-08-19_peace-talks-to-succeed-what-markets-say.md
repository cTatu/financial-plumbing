# Peace Talks to Succeed? -- What Markets Say

## 1. Thesis

Bond, currency, and equity markets are jointly pricing rising odds of an end to the Russia-Ukraine war: Russia's long-inverted curve has been bull-steepening since late 2024 as short yields fall fastest on expected inflation relief and central-bank cuts, while Ukraine remains trapped in short-only, high-rate, inverted-curve financing with 48% one-year yields against 14% inflation. The cross-checks agree — the ruble is down only 4.3% against the dollar since February 2022 versus 30% for the hryvnia, Russia's dollar-denominated index is up 18% since January 2022 despite the war, and pre-invasion harmonic selling plus an October 2021 curve inversion show insiders priced the shock early — but the last few days' re-inversion in Russia warns the peace signal is tentative, not settled.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] [02:40] Ukraine exhibits emergent-market payday financing because it can issue only short-dated paper at punitive rates, which is the same bill-heavy pattern the US is starting to show.
- [TAKE] [02:50] An inverted curve means short-term risk commands the premium, so Ukraine's inversion reads as near-term survival doubt while Russia's un-inversion reads as expected normalization.
- [TAKE] [11:28] Russia's bull steepener — all yields falling with short yields falling fastest — is what genuine pre-rate-cut, pre-disinflation pricing looks like, which exposes the US bear steepener as the abnormal one.
- [TAKE] [17:52] Rising volume into falling prices before February 24, 2022 marks harmonic selling by large informed holders, so the invasion was substantially priced before the headline.
- [TAKE] [18:34] Invasion day marked the short-term bottom for Russian equities, which is the classic sell-the-rumor, buy-the-news resolution once the priced shock arrives.

- [PREDICTS] [09:15] Short-dated Russian paper outperforms as the peace bid continues. -> check: short yields falling faster than long yields with bond values rising; long yields falling faster or all yields rising refutes it.
