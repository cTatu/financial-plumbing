# Shocking Inflation Data - A $110 PER HOUR WAGE? - Real Inflation vs Official Data

## 1. Thesis

The November CPI printed cool at 2.7% headline and 2.6% core versus 3.1% expected, but the print skipped October entirely and patched the gap with non-survey imputations after the shutdown, so its quality is poor and the bond market looked straight through it. Measured against what households actually spend per worker — up 37% since 2019, tracking money growth rather than the 22-23% CPI basket — real wages have fallen for decades and M2 has outrun CPI by 293% since 1971. The twin-deficit flywheel explains the split: a roughly $1 trillion per year mandated foreign bid into assets held by the top 1-10% lifts markets while global labor-supply expansion plus immigration suppresses US wages, leaving a $110-per-hour counterfactual versus $31 actual.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] [03:49] Low benefit levels suppress initial claims because displaced workers choose gig work over filing, so the 224,000 print understates true layoff flow.
- [TAKE] [04:25] The missing October survey plus non-survey imputations lower the report's quality, so a soft headline deserves a discount rather than a clean read.
- [TAKE] [04:08] The bond market looked through the cool CPI because the 30-year barely moved and the 2-year faded its rally, pricing supply and sticky services over one report.
- [TAKE] [06:56] Deflating pay by CPI flatters real wages because the 22-23% basket trails the 37% rise in per-worker spending and the faster M2 expansion behind it.
- [TAKE] [09:43] The $1 trillion inflow counterpart bids assets owned by the top 1-10%, so deficit accounting mechanically lifts financial wealth while global labor pooling and low- and high-skill immigration suppress American wages.
