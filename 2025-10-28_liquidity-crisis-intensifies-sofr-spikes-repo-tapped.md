# Liquidity Crisis Intensifies: SOFR Spikes, Repo Tapped — Is the Fed Losing Control?

## 1. Thesis

Funding stress escalated from elevated to above-the-ceiling: SOFR moved back above the 4.25% discount-window rate after also doing so on October 15-16, the Standing Repo Facility was tapped for $7.70 billion on top of $8.50 billion the prior day, and Fed funds kept ticking higher despite a cut last month and another expected in days. With the reverse-repo buffer empty, the Treasury rebuilding cash through $110.00 billion bill auctions, QT still draining, and bank reserves down from $3.40 trillion to $2.90 trillion, the presenter frames this as the Fed losing control of the $3.00 trillion overnight cost of capital and argues only reserve printing — ending QT and potentially restarting QE — can durably fix it.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] SOFR holding above the discount-window ceiling outside month-end, quarter-end, or tax day means the corridor has stopped containing the overnight price of dollars.
- [TAKE] Repeated Standing Repo draws plus SOFR and funds spreads widening together confirm collateral-rich but cash-short funding because firms must pledge securities to borrow dollars.
- [TAKE] QT plus TGA rebuild plus $110.00 billion bill supply plus an empty reverse repo all tighten liquidity because Treasury cash-raising removes dollars while QT destroys reserves.
- [TAKE] Rate cuts cannot fix repo strain because the binding constraint is the quantity of reserves and willingness to lend them, not the administered policy rate.
- [TAKE] The 2019 template is reserve printing to buy Treasuries and MBS because swapping bank securities for fresh cash directly rebuilds lendable dollars.
