# Foreign Demand Craters in Latest 7yr Bond Auction - Red Flags for US Debt Demand

## 1. Thesis

The $44 billion 7-year auction cleared soft with a 3.79% high yield that tailed by 0.80 basis points for the third straight tail and the largest tail since August 2024, while 2.46 bid-to-cover trailed the 2.57 six-auction average and 59.00% indirects sat far below the 67.00% average near a 50-auction low against 13.00% dealers and 27.80% directs. The split reads as waning foreign strong-hand sponsorship for belly duration into a hawkish Fed repricing — the 30-year at 4.66% now sits 68 basis points above the 3.98% level when cuts began — where bonds sold off after a 25-basis-point cut because easing into 2.40% above-target average inflation looks like fiscal dominance.

- [TAKE] [02:40] A 0.80-basis-point tail after two prior tails signals softening demand because the Treasury paid a higher yield discount to clear $44.00 billion.
- [TAKE] [03:20] Indirects at 59.00% against a 67.00% average matter because surplus-country and institutional strong hands absorb duration without forced selling.
- [TAKE] [04:05] Rising 13.00% dealer takedowns plus record-adjacent directs offset weak indirects mechanically because last-resort and domestic bids absorb what foreigners leave.

- [TAKE] [10:20] Cutting into 2.40% above-target average inflation reads as fiscal dominance because debt service needs lower rates while inflation stays elevated.
