# Repo Market Stress is Back, DESPITE Fed Printing $15 BILLION in Past Two Days - Year-End Meltdown?

## 1. Thesis

The Fed restarted money printing via reserve-management purchases ($8.1B on Dec 12 plus $6.8B on Dec 15) yet SOFR still printed at the 3.75% upper bound with ~$800B of $3.3T in daily repo financing above it, while the $16.8B standing-repo draw overwhelmed an effectively empty reverse repo. With QT plus a $600-700B TGA rebuild having already drained reserves, bill redemptions requiring a $650B minimum buffer, and year-end regulatory hoarding still ahead, the read is persistent willingness-to-lend tightness rather than a quarter-end blip — the same deterioration tracked since July-August.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] Standing repo use proves dollar shortage because the facility exists only for scarce-reserves states, the mirror of reverse repo for excess liquidity.
- [TAKE] Reserve-management buying is QE in short maturities because the Fed creates bank reserves from nothing to buy bills in order to suppress short rates rather than long rates.
- [TAKE] SOFR at the bound means control is slipping because the Fed sets only a corridor — RRP floor versus SRF/discount-window ceiling — while supply and demand set the traded rate.
- [TAKE] The crunch is willingness-to-lend rather than headline dollars because reserves can exist while holders hoard them into quarter-end and year-end for regulatory cash positions.
- [TAKE] QT plus TGA is the mechanical drain because shrinking reserves against a $650B five-day bill-redemption buffer leaves the $1.8T repo-funded basis trade one spike from forced deleveraging.

## 4C Predicts

- [PREDICTS] Year-end funding prints worse than mid-December because stress arrived before regulatory hoarding peaked. -> check: late-Dec SOFR 75th percentile wider with SRF draws above $16.8B confirms it; SOFR falling back toward RRP with SRF near zero refutes it.
- [PREDICTS] Reserve-management buying grows because the Fed sees persistent cracks rather than a one-off squeeze. -> check: larger or more frequent RMPs plus balance-sheet expansion confirms it; RMP pause with reserves rebuilding refutes it.
