# Trump Announces New Tariff Bombshell on Europe - Dollar and Bonds

## 1. Thesis

The 50% Europe tariff threat knocked SPY down 1.1% toward its 200-day near 5,700, but the session is read as another balance-of-payments print rather than ordinary risk-off: the dollar fell 0.5% when textbook tariff theory says it should rise, long bonds first bid then faded to unchanged-to-higher yields, and stocks plus bonds plus dollar fell together. That joint decline with US-Japan spreads 30bp wider and euro spreads weaker yet both currencies stronger is framed as capital repatriation doing the work of a weaker fairly valued dollar. With Japanese CPI at 3.6% versus US at 2.3% and oil 11% lower with breakevens flat, higher long-end rates look like real-yield fiscal premium rather than tariff inflation, and chasing daily tariff headlines misses the slower reindustrialization signal the dollar is sending.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] [05:31] Textbook tariff theory predicts a stronger implementing-country currency via trade-balance and capital-flow channels, so dollar weakness on a 50% Europe threat signals balance-of-payments repricing.
- [TAKE] [10:56] The US-Japan 10-year spread widening 30 basis points while the dollar falls breaks the rate-differential rule that higher relative US yield should attract inflows and support the dollar.
- [TAKE] [21:58] Yen strength while US rates outpace Japanese rates indicates repatriation where Japanese holders sell dollar assets and buy yen to buy higher-yielding JGBs.
- [TAKE] [28:48] Stocks down plus bonds down plus dollar down on the same day is textbook capital repatriation and rotation toward a more fairly valued dollar.
- [TAKE] [12:26] Tariffs alone cannot deliver reindustrialization without a weaker fairly valued dollar because reserve-status overvaluation keeps United States exports uncompetitive even as duties raise import prices.
