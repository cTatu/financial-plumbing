# How the Middle Class Went Extinct - How to Fix the Silent Depression?

## 1. Thesis

Walking through Mike Green's paper, the video argues the middle class was not robbed by conspiracy but hollowed out by bipartisan policy errors: radically expanding labor supply through globalization and immigration suppressed per-capita wages even as total labor share fell, while antitrust leniency plus post-GFC zero rates let consolidated incumbents extract rents and bid financial assets to multiples of GDP. The fiscal side locked the pattern in by shifting taxation from corporate profits onto wages, so workers fund a state whose outlays and asset values grow faster than the economy they live in. The fix, on this read, is restoring a pursuit economy of competition, production, and creative destruction over a property economy that protects incumbent assets — because no GDP print matters if the denominator workers live in keeps diluting them.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] [06:53] Expanding labor supply through globalization and immigration mechanically suppresses wages by the law of supply and demand, so total labor share understates the per-capita damage.
- [TAKE] [02:09] Public-sector unionization created a double monopoly with a soft budget constraint because government covers concessions with higher taxes while private unions face bankruptcy discipline.
- [TAKE] [03:28] Bork's consumer-welfare standard legalized consolidation, trading competitive market structure for cheap goods while giving incumbents pricing power that post-2020 demand exposed.
- [TAKE] [07:07] Capital went to China to arbitrage labor rather than to sell goods, so America sold factories and productive capacity in exchange for foreigners buying Treasury debt.
- [TAKE] [09:22] The tax code swapped a tax on corporate profits for a tax on wages, so capital pooled at the top while the working class shoulders roughly 36% of revenue through payroll taxes.

## 7. Follow-ups

- Watch payroll vs corporate revenue shares in the next Treasury receipts release: payroll share holding above 30% with corporate near 10% confirms the tax-shift thesis; convergence says the bargain is unwinding.
- Watch per-capita wage vs M2 and CPI prints: wages beating CPI while losing to M2-per-capita confirms the dual-basket undercount; wages beating both says real purchasing power is healing.
- Watch antitrust and entry signals (business formation, IPO breadth, concentration ratios): rising formation with falling concentration confirms a pursuit-economy turn; continued consolidation with buyback-led markets says the property economy persists.
