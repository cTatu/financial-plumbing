# Global Bond Market Meltdown, HORRIBLE Bond Auctions -- Energy Crisis = Sovereign Debt Crisis?

## 1. Thesis

An 18-million-barrel-per-day supply shock has repriced energy into a global sovereign-bond selloff through two linked channels: higher inflation compensation plus a balance-of-payments squeeze that forces energy importers to sell Treasuries to fund wider current-account deficits. The US is less damaged than the UK, Japan, and France but cannot escape because foreigners own $69 trillion of US assets, this week's $750 billion of mostly bill supply cleared with large tails and collapsing bid-to-cover, and the resulting bear flattener plus soaring MOVE volatility points toward equities breaking and eventual QE to hold debt costs below nominal growth.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] [02:26] Physical oil must balance supply and consumption, so an 18-million-barrel disruption must destroy an equivalent amount of demand through price.
- [TAKE] [04:02] Energy importers must fund a larger dollar oil bill by selling Treasuries because a current-account deficit cannot be financed out of thin air.
- [TAKE] [09:50] The US bond meltdown is driven mainly by term-premium uncertainty over deficits and geopolitics rather than by breakeven inflation expectations.
- [TAKE] [16:12] Higher foreign yields pull US yields higher because investors sell a 2% Treasury to buy a higher gilt, which pressures US bond prices downward.
- [TAKE] [20:36] The endgame is QE to push debt costs back below nominal growth, with the dollar's purchasing power absorbing the release valve instead of outright default.
