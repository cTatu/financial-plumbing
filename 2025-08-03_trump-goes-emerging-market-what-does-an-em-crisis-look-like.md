# Trump Goes Emerging Market - What Does an EM Crisis Look Like?

## 1. Thesis

Trump firing the BLS commissioner after a weak payrolls print is read as an emerging-market tell — political pressure overriding economic logic and eroding institutional independence, the theme the series has tracked since summer 2024. Turkey is the template: twin deficits, populist spending, credit downgrades, and repeated central-bank purges (Murat sacked in 2019 for resisting cuts, Agbal sacked in 2021 after hiking to 19%) collapsed into rate cuts at 85% inflation, a 50% lira loss in 2021-22, and negative 74% real 10-year returns. The US twin deficits are larger — about 6% current-account and 7% fiscal versus Turkey's 2-5% — but America retains the reserve currency, the deep consumer, and corporate exceptionalism, so the rhyme runs through nominal asset inflation on a failing denominator rather than an identical currency collapse.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] [03:57] Credit downgrades followed consolidation of power despite low debt-to-GDP, so institutional erosion reprices before fiscal ratios break.
- [TAKE] [04:47] Populism is economically uniform across flavors because tax breaks, subsidies, and minimum-wage hikes widen deficits and feed inflation.
- [TAKE] [07:20] Sacking governors for hiking while inflation runs 9-21% reads as fiscal dominance, the Treasury-Fed Accord reversal where the central bank becomes a government arm.
- [TAKE] [20:36] Nominal asset booms during currency collapse are denominator illusion because lira stocks and gold soar 10x-plus while barely re-breaking 2018 dollar highs.
- [TAKE] [22:32] Debt ratios melt through massively negative real rates, not repayment, because minus 74% real 10-year returns liquidate bondholders.
