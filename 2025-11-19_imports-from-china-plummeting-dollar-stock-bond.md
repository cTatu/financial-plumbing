# Imports from China PLUMMETING - Implications for the Dollar, Stock Market and Bond Market

## 1. Thesis

The sharp narrowing of the US trade deficit — with the monthly gap down from ~$140B to ~$60B and the quarterly China goods deficit nearly halved — looks like trade-war success but mechanically withdraws the ~$1T-per-year foreign bid for Treasuries, corporates, equities and housing that funds about half the ~$2T fiscal deficit. Because the current-account deficit must equal a capital-account surplus, every dollar of deficit reduction is a dollar less flowing into US financial assets that accrue to the top 1% and corporate profits, while pushing toward true balance would end the Triffin mechanism that sustains structurally overvalued dollars and therefore reserve status itself.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] A smaller trade deficit directly shrinks the capital-account surplus because $251B per quarter of dollar outflow must return as $251B per quarter of foreign buying of Treasuries, corporates, stocks and real estate.
- [TAKE] Balancing trade removes the inelastic foreign bid that overvalues the dollar versus twin-deficit fundamentals and simultaneously supports asset prices, corporate margins from cheap offshore labor, and top-1% wealth.
- [TAKE] The 2022 two-quarter GDP contraction with a bear market and the pre-2020 2019 import rollover show falling imports can be a late-cycle demand signal rather than pure trade-war success.
- [TAKE] Funding roughly half of a ~$2T fiscal deficit with ~$1T of mechanical trade-recycled inflow means deficit-narrowing without lower issuance pushes adjustment onto domestic buyers via higher yields or lower asset prices.
- [TAKE] True balance would exit Triffin's dilemma because reserve provision requires persistent deficits to supply dollars abroad, so reserve status ends if deficits end.
