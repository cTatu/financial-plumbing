# US Economic Growth vs Bond Yields - A Turning Point?

## 1. Thesis

The cost of US debt has crossed above the growth that services it — 10-year at 4.5% and 30-year at 5.0% versus 4.3% nominal growth — flipping the government from the favorable regime where growth outran rates into one where debt compounds faster than income. That crossover is framed as the market pricing a longer break that started in the late 1990s: real growth has sat below 3% since the GFC versus 3% to 3.5%+ before, while personal income/M2, wages/gold, velocity, SPX/gold, and GDP/M2 all peaked around 1995-2001 and then faded as M2 outran GDP by 36% over 30 years. With a 7%-of-GDP deficit at full employment forcing ever more supply and the real yield plus mortgage-spread both flagging a higher true risk-free rate, the read is America living its own lost-decades silent depression where printing lifts nominal assets but not real purchasing power.

- [11:10] The federal deficit is running near 7% of GDP with unemployment near historic lows outside the GFC and 2020. (as of May 2025)
- [12:52] The 10-year real yield measured as 10-year minus CPI is breaking out higher while the 30-year mortgage minus 10-year spread holds a 2.3% floor. (as of May 2025)
- [17:27] The 10-year minus nominal GDP spread spiked to 9.3% to 9.4% in the GFC as debt-to-GDP surged higher. (as of 2008-2009 episode)
- [30:18] M2 money supply has increased faster than GDP by 36% over the 30 years from April 1995 to April 2025. (as of Apr 2025)
- [30:39] Real GDP measured against M2 is down 30% since April 2007 just before the financial crisis. (as of Apr 2025)

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] [04:23] Nominal growth above Treasury yields lets the government outgrow debt service, while yields crossing above nominal growth makes the debt load compound faster.
- [TAKE] [07:52] Short-dated issuance is money-like and stimulative, but rolling every three to twelve months removes the buffer when inflation forces the Fed to hike.
- [TAKE] [15:53] The mortgage minus Treasury spread stuck near 2.3% means the true risk-free rate is higher than the 10-year alone suggests.
- [TAKE] [20:48] Growth persistently trailing the 10-year pushed debt-to-GDP up after the early 1980s and GFC, while growth leading the 10-year let the ratio fall or flatten.
- [TAKE] [29:28] M2 outrunning GDP since the late 1990s shows printing buying less real output, with GDP/M2 deteriorating rather than compounding.

## 7. Follow-ups

- Watch nominal GDP near 4.3% with 10-year near 4.5% and 30-year near 5.0%: yields holding above growth with deficit near 7% confirms debt-load compounding, while nominal growth reclaiming 5%+ with yields flat says the crossover was noise.
- Watch 10-year minus CPI with mortgage minus 10-year near 2.3%: real yield pushing higher with spread stuck confirms dearer true risk-free rate, while real yield falling under 1% with spread compressing says financial-repression pricing has returned.
- Watch GDP divided by M2 with velocity: continued new lows in GDP/M2 with velocity flat confirms printing without real growth, while GDP/M2 turning up with velocity rising says real activity is absorbing money growth.
