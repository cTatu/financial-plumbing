# Japan's Bond Market COLLAPSE — Global Market Chaos, De-Dollarization Heats Up, Crisis Brewing?

## 1. Thesis

Japan's 30-year JGB spiking 30bp+ in a day on dovish fiscal talk (a food sales-tax cut plus a snap election) looks like meltdown, but the domestic solvency risk is overstated: most issuance sits under five years, the Bank of Japan owns half the market, and only 12% sits with foreigners against a 240% debt-to-GDP stock. The larger risk runs the other way through repatriation — higher JGB yields pull Japanese savings home, pressuring US duration — and the US session already flashes the more ominous pattern: stocks, bonds, and the dollar falling together while gold, the franc, the euro, and the yen absorb the bid, an early balance-of-payments-style rotation that tariffs and a shrinking trade deficit failed to reverse via a dollar short squeeze.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] Japan's long-end velocity matters less for Tokyo solvency than for Washington funding because Japan funds itself short, holds its own bonds via the BoJ, and owes almost nothing externally.
- [TAKE] The US 10-year is independent of Fed funds because mortgages, autos, and business loans price off duration compensation for fiscal, term-premium, growth, and inflation risk rather than the overnight rate.
- [TAKE] Joint US stock, bond, and dollar weakness with euro, franc, yen, and gold strength is textbook capital-flight arithmetic that normally belongs to emerging markets, not the reserve issuer.
- [TAKE] A shrinking trade deficit should, all else equal, starve the world of dollars and squeeze the dollar higher, so the failure to rally exposes Triffin's constraint binding in reverse.
- [TAKE] Repatriation is the transmission channel: yen sold for dollars to buy Treasuries reverses when JGB yields finally compensate domestic savers, lifting US yields while weakening the dollar.
