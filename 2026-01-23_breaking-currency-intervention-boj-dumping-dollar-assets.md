# BREAKING — Currency Market Intervention by BoJ, Dumping Dollar Assets to Prop Up the Yen?

## 1. Thesis

An overnight dollar-yen plunge of about 1.5% after the Bank of Japan held at 0.75% looks like textbook FX intervention — a rate-check threat followed by actual dollar-asset sales to buy yen — and the mechanics point at US duration: Japan funds yen buying by selling FX reserves held as roughly $1.2T of Treasuries, then selling the dollars, which lifts US long-end yields even as the dollar falls to a three-month low near 97.7. The episode extends the regime break from the Greenland week — risk-off producing weaker dollars, softer long bonds, and records in gold near $5,000 and silver over $100 — while bill auctions still clear with 2.7-2.8x cover, proving cash-market dollar demand coexists with duration distrust, and a Trump administration that wants a cheaper dollar is unlikely to oppose a coordinated intervention.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] A 1.5% dollar-yen plunge right after a dovish hold is almost certainly intervention because fundamentals alone would have strengthened the dollar against the yen.
- [TAKE] Intervention is duration-negative for the US because the BoJ must sell Treasuries to raise dollars before it can sell dollars to buy yen.
- [TAKE] The US-Japan 10-year premium keeps compressing while dollar-yen earlier strengthened, so the Liberation Day correlation breakdown persists and regime risk sits with the dollar leg.
- [TAKE] Bill auctions clearing above 2.7x while duration wobbles proves the reserve bid survives in cash markets even as term compensation reprices fiscal and FX risk.
- [TAKE] The Trump administration is unlikely to resist a yen intervention because a grossly overvalued dollar on PPP and balance-of-payments grounds blocks reindustrialization.
