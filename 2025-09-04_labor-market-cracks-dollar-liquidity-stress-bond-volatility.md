# Labor Market Cracks + Dollar Liquidity Stress + Bond Market Volatility Signal Flashes Red

## 1. Thesis

The labor market is cooling on every margin except the headline: Challenger cuts hit the worst August since 2020, ADP missed by 14,000, claims printed 237,000, and 7.24 million unemployed now exceed 7.18 million openings for the first time since 2021. At the same time the funding backdrop is tightening rather than confirming a soft landing — the MOVE index jumped from 77 to 90, SOFR sits at 4.39% essentially on IORB with a 6-basis-point spread over funds, and the TGA rebuild has already drained $270 billion while the reverse-repo buffer sits at only $17.9 billion. The sting is that ISM new orders just pushed back above 50 while ISM prices at 63.7 still point to PPI above 3%, so any Fed easing into this mix risks repeating the inflation rebound the bond market is starting to price.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] [01:09] Job cuts near the top of the five-year range without nonlinear claims means the labor market is cooling rather than breaking.
- [TAKE] [04:27] Healthcare dominance of employment since 1990 means a 181,000 drop in healthcare openings carries more cyclical weight than smaller sector moves.
- [TAKE] [08:58] The unemployed-minus-openings cross looks alarming in isolation but was the norm from 2001 to 2018, so context argues for tight-loose normalization rather than recession proof.
- [TAKE] [13:21] New orders crossing above 50 while prices sit at 63.7 signals accelerating real activity alongside rising costs, which complicates any cut narrative.
- [TAKE] [24:16] A $270 billion TGA drain with only $17.9 billion left in reverse repo means hundreds of billions more must come from reserves rather than sterilized excess.
