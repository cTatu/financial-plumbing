# Why Americans Feel Broke Despite 'Booming' Economy - A Silent Depression

## 1. Thesis

Headline growth looks booming — Q2 GDP at 3.8%, income up 0.4% and spending up 0.6% in August — yet Michigan sentiment at 55.1 sits below the 2008 crisis low because the denominator is broken: wages divided by M2, GDP, or gold keep falling while assets melt up, so the top 10% who own 93% of equities spend from the wealth effect and the bottom 80% live a silent depression where 3% inflation compounds the purchasing-power loss.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] The data are not fraudulent because nominal income, spending, and GDP really are strong; the lens is wrong when the denominator is ignored.
- [TAKE] GDP flatters waste because debt-fueled consumption and unproductive government spending add to output without adding prosperity.
- [TAKE] The average wage buys less of everything that matters because it trails M2, GDP, gold, homes, and equities on every denominator shown.
- [TAKE] K-shaped spending persists because asset holders recycle the wealth effect while the bottom 80% without equities absorb 3% inflation.
- [TAKE] Sentiment tracks the blue and green purchasing-power lines rather than the white nominal lines, which resolves the strong-data versus broke-feeling puzzle.

## 4C Predicts

- [PREDICTS] K-shaped spending and GDP stay firm while sentiment stays depressed as long as equities melt up. -> check: S&P and spending firm with Michigan near 55; sentiment rebounding above 65 with wages outpacing M2 refutes it.
- [PREDICTS] Affordability keeps worsening because homes and the S&P outrun wages by roughly 10-to-1. -> check: hours-per-SP-unit and price-to-income holding or rising; wages closing the gap with assets refutes it.
