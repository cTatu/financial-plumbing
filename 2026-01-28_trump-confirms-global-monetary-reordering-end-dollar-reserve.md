# Trump Confirms: A Global Monetary Reordering IS HERE — The End of the Dollar's Reserve Status

## 1. Thesis

Trump's Iowa answer that he is not worried about dollar weakness, alongside Bessent's "strong dollar means right fundamentals" line and the prior Friday NY Fed rate check, is read as de facto confirmation that Washington now prefers a weaker dollar to preserve domestic industry over reserve-status privilege. The mechanics run through Stephen Miran's November 2024 reordering blueprint — tariffs plus dollar devaluation plus a user fee or foreign tax to zero out the $1.2T annual capital inflow — which would balance the current account by ending the reserve bid, push gold as the neutral successor, lift import inflation, and hit the top 10% who own 92% of equities plus a Treasury market that can no longer rely on foreigners to absorb a $1.7T fiscal deficit.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] Trump saying a weaker dollar is not bad and may be good reverses his prior king-dollar reserve-status stance and signals domestic industry now outranks reserve privilege.
- [TAKE] Bessent's strong-dollar-means-right-fundamentals line redefines strength as fair balance-of-payments valuation, which on PPP arithmetic means a much weaker dollar and higher gold.
- [TAKE] Miran's user-fee logic balances trade by zeroing foreign asset purchases because the capital surplus is the mirror of the current-account deficit, not its consequence.
- [TAKE] Ending the $1.2T inflow ends the reserve-currency bid by construction because cutting dollars to the world removes the financing that sustains dollar overvaluation.
- [TAKE] Balancing the current account without balancing the $1.7T fiscal deficit forces domestic absorption of Treasuries that Americans lack capacity to buy, so fiscal consolidation must accompany reordering.
