# Consumer Confidence Plummets to Crisis Level - Why It'll Get Worse

## 1. Thesis

University of Michigan sentiment sits at the second-lowest reading since 1953 with current conditions at an all-time low, and the deterioration has spread from a partisan split to Republicans and independents alongside a 12-year low in feeling better off than five years ago. The presenter reads this as an inflation-driven trap rather than noise: consumers feel the ~27% cumulative price step-up while CPI still runs ~3% with ISM-services prices vertical, so the only path to the lower prices voters want is a recession that would itself crush sentiment, jobs and equities further.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] Sentiment deterioration now spans Republicans and independents rather than a partisan divide, which makes the signal harder for the administration to dismiss as polling noise.
- [TAKE] Consumers respond to the cumulative 27% price level while economists quote the 3% annual rate, so easing inflation does not repair sentiment because the price step never reversed.
- [TAKE] Actual lower prices require deflation and deflation requires a recession, so granting what households want means job losses that push sentiment even lower.
- [TAKE] Fading retail dip-buying matters for equities because institutions watch the same survey share expecting gains, and a retail step-back removes a marginal buyer into existing weakness.
- [TAKE] The late-1970s K-shape between a rising S&P 12-month rate and falling sentiment resolved through high inflation, high rates and back-to-back recessions rather than a painless re-convergence.
