# Why the US *Can't Afford* a Recession - A Sovereign Margin Call

## 1. Thesis

Starting from a 7%-of-GDP procyclical deficit, even a garden-variety recession acts as a sovereign margin call: revenue falls about 15% ($4.92T to $4.20T) while spending rises about 29% ($6.70T to $8.70T), so a $2.00T deficit doubles to roughly $4.50T just as GDP shrinks from $30T to $29T, producing a 15.5%-of-GDP gap. Because balance-sheet capacity is already exhausted — debt-to-GDP doubled to 125% since 2007, the Fed's share tripled to 15% of GDP, and each new debt dollar buys only $0.34 of GDP — the supply surge lands in a market that no longer wants duration, so the current bear-steepening regime (30Y up 100bps since cuts began) says long rates would rise into the downturn, lifting interest into a $5T deficit doom loop instead of providing the usual relief.

## 4B TAKE

- [TAKE] [02:29] Revenue falls while spending rises in every recession, so the two deficit legs move in opposite and wrong-way directions by construction.
- [TAKE] [08:55] GDP is the balance-sheet capacity that must absorb new supply, so a 4.5% GDP fall alongside a doubled deficit makes the ratio deteriorate nonlinearly.
- [TAKE] [11:33] The market no longer wants the marginal debt because the Fed's GDP share of holdings tripled from 4-5% to 15%, which marks demand-side exhaustion.
- [TAKE] [14:24] Each borrowed dollar buys less output as state share and debt-to-GDP rise, with only $0.34 of GDP per $1.00 of debt since 2007 capping real potential.
- [TAKE] [18:22] The post-September-2024 bear steepener breaks every prior cutting-cycle template, so recession rate cuts would likely push long yields higher rather than lower.
